# Changelog

All notable changes to ommat are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and the project follows [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html). Before 1.0, a minor release may break the API; each break is listed under **Changed** or **Removed**.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- `decide_sync` and `replay_sync` take `runner=None`, an `asyncio.Runner` the caller owns. Each turn then runs by `runner.run` in a copy of the caller's context, not under `asyncio.run` on a fresh loop, so many turns on one thread share one loop and pay for none. A runner not exactly an `asyncio.Runner` is an `EntryRefusal`, raised before the coroutine is built; a closed runner raises its own `RuntimeError` and runs nothing.
- With a runner, a task a binding leaves is cancelled, and a `to_thread` thread past its timeout waited for, when the runner closes, not when the turn ends.

### Changed

- A design names no policy, and decide designates no entry: a turn's result is its record, and a caller reads each estimate it acts on from `decision.record.entries[ref]` and judges for itself whether an entry it acts on is undecided (holds a marker). Interpret fires every rule, as before; load refuses nothing for a rule nothing reads.
- The design document is format 12, where it was 11: it holds no `policy` member, and check 11 refuses a format-12 document holding one as a member its form does not have. **Every design hash changes**, so a record decided under 0.4.0 replays under the same design rebuilt with a `design-mismatch` finding, and the evaluation jobs refuse it `wrong-design` under it. Load refuses every stored design document of format 11 or earlier with check 11, naming its format; the outline and the lens still read them.
- The record is format 11, where it was 10, and `RECORD_FORMATS` is 1 to 11. Its `settings` item names no `policy`: the reader refuses `policy` in a format-11 record, and still requires it, a `d:` key of `providers` with no field path and no key of `grounded`, in a record of formats 8 to 10. Each old format reads to its own bytes and hash under its own rules.
- A broken entry writes `t:state` `Unavailable` with the entry cause in round 0 and nothing after it, where it also wrote the policy's ref with the same marker: the log ends at the `t:state` entry, and the reader refuses any item after a `t:state` entry holding a marker in a format-11 record, keeping the old order for formats 8 to 10. `defects` lists `t:state` alone for it, and `escalation(record, "t:state")` gives its cause; `escalation` at any rule's ref gives `None`, as the record holds no entry there.
- A record is unfinished when it holds a `cause` item, as a cancelled turn's, whatever entries it holds. Replay, the do-and-diff jobs, rescore and the gold jobs refuse such a record `unfinished` as before. A record an observer's exception ended is now finished however far it got, where one that ended before the policy's entry was unfinished: replay reruns it and reports the entries past its end as `entry` findings, its unanswered calls as `stale-question`; `use_answers`, `intervene`, `scenario`, `rescore` and `gold_record` rerun it; `deciding_inputs` and `question_reach` refuse it `baseline-differs`.
- The lens has two views, Gravity and Turn, where it had eight. Gravity, the design as bodies on a force layout in 3D, or 2D with `g3=0`, opens for any set with a design; Turn, what a record shows in plain words, opens for a record no design pairs with. Gravity's panel carries what the other views said: with a turn, the verdict, play over the rounds with each round's sources, and entry and target chips that filter; for a selection, its neighbours as reads, read by and bears on, a table's rows with the output beside the row number as the turn ran them or as declared, a rule's parameters and bounds, a question's or tool result's wording and whole answer as a distribution, a target's statement, questions and estimators, and for the policy what it decides between; with a table and no turn, a `fired, of N loaded turns` column, a count over the turns paired by hash; with nothing selected, the design summary. Turn's All turns table has a tally of the loaded turns, decided by value and undecided by marker ref, whose counts filter it, and a turn that no design pairs with lists its entries by round. The Path, Overview, Explore, Targets, Outcomes and Graph views, the bounds panel and the header's lines are removed, as are the turn-overlay and lineage-only checkboxes and the hash keys `state`, `und`, `sort`, `up`, `down`, `dir`, `info`, `table`, `tmode`, `tgt`, `gdrag`, `ghide`, `gdom`, `gx` and `gov`, which are ignored; an old `node=` link selects that node's body. A held `null` reads `none`, not `no value`.
- `combine` keeps one copy of a source or rule two parts declare alike under one id, as it keeps a type, group or target, and refuses one they declare differently; an id one part declares as a source and another as a rule is still refused. Before, a repeated id was refused even when alike. A question several parts ask alike now goes in a source each part declares alike, so each part runs alone and the combined design asks it once.
- `escalation(record, ref)` takes the ref of the entry the caller acts on, required, where `escalation(record)` read the policy's entry from the record's `settings`. It returns the marker's five members at `ref`, and `None` when `ref` holds a value or no entry, for old and new records alike; a `ref` that is not exactly a `str`, not a ref, or carries a field path is a `ValueError`. Pass the ref your design named as its policy for the result 0.4.0 gave.
- `to_chat_completions(record, design=None, *, refs, turn=None)` and `to_model_messages(record, design=None, *, refs, model_name=None, provider_name=None, turn=None)` take `refs`, a required, non-empty `list` or `tuple` of distinct `d:` refs of the record's rules. Decide's result is keyed by those refs, each `{"value": v}` or `{"undecided": {"kind", "root_cause", "refs"}}`, where it was `{"decision": v}` or `{"undecided": ...}` for the policy alone: the `decision` member is gone, and `refs=["d:<former policy>"]` gives `{"d:<former policy>": {"value": v}}`. Decide's arguments leave out every ref of `refs`, and with the design are cut to the reads of all their rules and every marker. A record with no entry at one of `refs` renders `[]`; a ref the record's `providers` lacks, or `refs` that are not such refs, is a `ValueError`.
- `dead_questions(loaded, refs, *, values=None)` takes `refs`, required: a non-empty `list` or `tuple` of declared whole `d:` refs a rule provides, a repeat counted once. A question is `live` when two runs differ at some ref of `refs` by the entry's value or marker bytes, a marker's cause and refs included, or one run holds no entry there, where the runs were compared by the policy's entry alone. `refs` not a list or tuple is a `TypeError`; one empty, or naming any other ref (a `q:` or `t:` ref, a field path, a grounded declared ref, a ref no rule provides), is a `ValueError` before any run. Pass `[the former policy's ref]` for the report 0.4.0 gave.
- `question_reach(records, loaded, *, refs, values=None)` and `question_reach_sync` take `refs`, required and checked as `dead_questions` checks it, before `values` and before any record is read. A trial reaches when the entry it wrote at some ref of `refs` differs from the record's by those bytes, or one of the two holds none, where it compared the policy's entry alone. Pass `refs=[the former policy's ref]` for the report 0.4.0 gave.
- `deciding_inputs` judges the entry at a record's `settings.policy` as it judges every other entry, by value, or by marker kind and cause code: a trial that changes only the cause refs of a marker at that ref does not make a read `deciding`, where it did.
- The do-and-diff jobs' refusal of a record before format 8 says that this engine does not rerun its turn, where it said the record holds no policy's entry; the reason, `wrong-design`, is unchanged.
- `rescore(records, recorded, designs, *, refs)`, `rescore_sync`, `rescore_grid(records, recorded, loaded, points, *, refs)` and `rescore_grid_sync` take `refs`, required and checked as `dead_questions` checks it, against every design of `designs`, or `recorded` with none, and against `loaded` for the grid, before any record is read, the `ValueError` opening with the design it names. A row is `{"design", "record", "entries", "stale"}`: the member `decision` is renamed `entries`, which maps each ref of `refs` the run wrote, in code-point order, to `{"value": v}` or `{"marker": {...}}`, or is `"not-determined"`. Pass `refs=[the former policy's ref]` and read `row["entries"][ref]` for 0.4.0's `row["decision"]`. A state that does not type gives `entries` `{}`, since a broken entry writes `t:state` alone, where `decision` held the policy's `Unavailable` marker. The grid's regions do not depend on `refs`, so it reruns as often whichever are named.
- `switching_values` lists no entry always: `entries` holds each ref whose entry some run changed, or that carries the leaf, where it also held the policy's entry always, and a caller reads `entries.get(ref)`. Every entry is compared by value, or marker kind and cause code, so a change of a marker's cause refs alone is no switch. A value whose design does not load counts as a change to every `d:` entry the record holds, so each is listed with its `not-loading` class. A run that asks a stale question lists every `d:` entry the record holds in the same way, with its `not-determined` class. The `switching-value` entry is format 3, where it was 2; the job, single and batch, refuses a key the store holds under a format other than 3 `unknown-entry`, before `held-key` and any replay.
- The outline and the lens read a design that names no policy, every design of format 12, and a record whose `settings` item names none, every record of format 11, keyed on the member, not the format. The outline keeps every key: the design's `policy` and `policy_node` are `null`, each node's `policy` false and `steps_to_policy` `null`; the turn's `policy`, `decision` and `escalation` are `null`, each entry's `policy` false, `lineage` empty, and `status` `unfinished` only at a `cause` item. Its text says `decision  none: the record names no decision` and `why  no decision: the record names none`, for a record before format 8 too, where it said the record holds no policy, and its `summary` gives `-` in the decision column where it gave `no-decision`; Turn's Bounds fold says nothing of a decision. Turn heads such a record by its status, `Finished in N rounds`, `Broken entry` or `Cancelled`, where it said `No decision`, lists `The tables that ran` in the order they ran, and leaves out the All turns table's decision and root columns and its tally when no loaded turn's record names a policy; Gravity draws such a design with no centre and no lean, and its panel names no steps to a policy. A record or design that names a policy draws as before.
- The lens's and the outline's commands take a JSON object with a `rules` object and no `log` list as a design, where they took one holding `policy`. Every design document of every format holds `rules`.

### Removed

- `Design.policy`. `Design(state, *, sources=..., rules=..., parameters=..., types=..., groups=..., name=..., targets=...)` takes `state` as its one positional field; `Design(state, "d:x")` and `Design(policy=...)` are a `TypeError`. Delete the argument; the rule it named stays an ordinary rule, read from the record at its ref.
- `combine`'s `policy=` and its checks: `combine(*parts, bind, name=None)` returns the union of the parts and the rules bound to their ports. A rule that reads across parts, the former top policy, goes in as a part of its own, `Part(Design(state, rules={"act": rule}))`, or is merged into the combined design with `dataclasses.replace`; passing `policy=` is a `TypeError`.
- The authoring reader's `policy` key: `design_from` requires `state` alone, and a document that still says `policy = "d:x"` is refused with `AuthoringError` as `document: unknown key 'policy'`. Delete the line.
- `transcript(record)`, deprecated since 0.4.0, and its export from `ommat`. Render the record with an `ommat.conversation` renderer, or read the record for every value.
- `Decision.estimate`. A `Decision` holds `record` and `design_hash`, and its `repr` is `Decision(record=…)`. Read the entry at the ref you act on, the one your design named as its policy for 0.4.0's value, from `decision.record.entries[ref]`: `{"value": v}` with the value as recorded, or `{"marker": {"kind", "cause": {"code", "refs"}}}` where `estimate` rebuilt an `Unsure`, `Unavailable` or `Unasked`; test `"value" in entry`. For the marker object, `escalation(decision.record, ref)` gives its kind and cause.
- The stopping certificate, which this engine reserved and never ran: `RunSettings.certificate_cap`, the `certificate` item and the `settled` cause code. `RunSettings(certificate_cap=...)` is a `TypeError`, as any unknown argument is, and `CauseCode.settled` is gone. A format-11 record's run settings hold no `certificate_cap`, and the reader refuses it there, as it refuses a `certificate` item and a `settled` code in a format-11 record. A record of formats 1 to 10 reads as before, `certificate_cap` required in its run settings; replay and the evaluation jobs refuse one whose `certificate_cap` is not null with an `EntryRefusal`, as decide refused that setting. Drop `certificate_cap=None` from any `RunSettings(...)` call.
- `Source.feedback`, a flag nothing in the engine acted on. `Source(..., feedback=...)` is a `TypeError`, as any unknown argument is; a format-12 design document's source holds no `feedback` member, and check 11 refuses one holding it as a member its form does not have; the authoring reader refuses a source's `feedback` key as an unknown key with its path. Every design hash changes again, since every source's document member went with it. The outline reads the flag of a design of format 11 or earlier as before, and gives each node of a source without it `feedback` false. Delete the argument.

### Fixed

- The design and evaluation stores write on Windows. Every write raised there: `_create` called `os.fchmod`, absent before Python 3.13, and synced the directory through `os.open`, which Windows refuses. It now skips both on Windows, which has no POSIX modes and cannot open a directory to sync it.
- The lens and the outline refuse a document nested deeper than 128 as too deep whatever its top-level type. A deep top-level array was refused as not a JSON object, and whether parsing caught the depth first depended on the C stack, so on Python 3.14 under Linux a 100,000-deep array took the wrong refusal.
- `dead_questions` runs every point on one event loop. It opened a loop per run, and on Windows each loop opens a loopback socket pair whose ports wait about two minutes to close, so some 16,000 runs exhausted the ephemeral ports and the lint stalled.
- A git install of ommat on Windows is byte for byte the one on macOS or Linux. Git's Windows default, `core.autocrlf=true`, wrote every installed file with CRLF; `.gitattributes` now keeps text LF in every checkout. A rule's source hash never depended on line endings, so no design hash changes.

## [0.4.0] - 2026-11-16

### Added

- `ommat.authoring`: `design_from(doc, version=None, values=None, types=None)` reads a parsed mapping, such as a TOML document read with `tomllib`, as the `Design` it says, and `slice_from` as the `Slice` of `sources`, `rules` and `parameters` a larger design merges. A document names a question by its kind, a rule by `fn`, `type` and `reads`, and a decision table by `rows` that name only the inputs they test, with `like` copying another table; it has `[types]` and `[labels]` names, a `version` and parameter `values` the caller may supply, and a rule's `params` may hold patterns. A slip in a document is an `AuthoringError`, a `ValueError`, naming its path; `load` refuses what the design gets wrong. The reader imports each rule's `fn`, so a document is as trusted as source code. Its designs hash as their Python twins do, `import ommat` does not import it, and it takes nothing beyond the standard library.
- A key path, `leaf.key`, names one key of a numeric mapping leaf. A function rule's `boundaries` may name one wherever it names a `number` leaf, load refusing under check 8 a key path whose leaf is no `mapping<string, number>` or lacks the key; the answer grid, the lints' read points and the outline's bounds read the key's value. `rescore_grid` takes a key path in a point, `{"clarify/part_at.email": 0.6}`, beside or in place of the leaf given whole, and refuses a point naming a leaf both ways as `overlapping-paths`, a new `RefusalReason` member; it checks paths in code-point order. A function rule naming a key's path sees that key alone: the grid refuses `hidden-leaf` a key it varies that the rule names neither way, and `switching_values` lists the rule in `unseen` unless it names every key. Each key of a mapping leaf is bucketed, and probed, against the comparisons naming the leaf whole or that key's path.
- `rescore_grid(records, recorded, loaded, points)` in `ommat.evaluation`, async with a `_sync` twin: `rescore` over the designs a list of parameter points gives, each point `loaded` with its leaves set, returning the same rows in the same order while rerunning each record once per region of points whose runs cannot differ, read from each rerun's compared values, a tie joining the side its predicate puts it on. A grid leaf a run could reach other than by a comparison the job can see is refused `hidden-leaf` before any run, and points not naming one leaf set `uneven-grid`, both new `RefusalReason` members. On a 1,089-point grid over 384 records it runs in a small fraction of `rescore`'s time, its rows equal.
- `switching_values_batch(records, loaded, paths, *, functions=None, results)` in `ommat.evaluation`, async with a `_sync` twin: the switching-value job for many records and leaves on one event loop, sharing the design's work, the entries in record then path order. Every record and path is checked, the document loaded with `functions`, refused `invalid-design`, and every key looked up, in that order, before any replay; a held key is refused `held-key`, naming each.
- The rescoring job in `ommat.evaluation`: `rescore(records, recorded, designs)`, async with a `_sync` twin, decides each recorded turn again under each of several designs from its own answers, as replay would but checking each asked source against the record under `recorded`, and returns each decision as plain data, building no record; a design that asks a question the record does not answer gives `not-determined` with the stale refs. Scoring a grid of settings this way runs about three times as fast as deciding each point, on one event loop.
- Path C of [Add a source](docs/how-to/add-a-source.md#path-c-write-a-binding-over-a-chat-endpoint): a binding over a chat-completions endpoint, through a transport the caller passes, that answers `noul` and `choice` questions with verbalised probabilities to two places, marks a refusal `refused`, a reply off its form `invalid-answer` and a failed request `timed-out` or `raised` on its own ref, and records one provider and model, its tokens, a reason for each null detail and a provider figure in `extras`, importing only `ommat` and `ommat.bindings`, with its example in `docs/how-to/add_a_source/`.
- A how-to, [Lay out a design module so it reads](docs/how-to/lay-out-a-design.md): a docstring map checked against the design in a test, sections in one order, rule functions named for their refs, refs as literals, a table's inputs named, one bar per decision, a read-back into the caller's type, and a check that a tidy moved nothing but the hash, with its example in `docs/how-to/lay_out_a_design/`.
- `ommat.outline`, outside the compatibility promise: `design_outline(document)` and `turn_outline(record_document, design_document=None)` give a stored design or turn, as bytes or parsed, as a plain dict of fixed shape, every key present and `null` where the documents give nothing; `render_design` and `render_turn` print it as deterministic text, one node, call or entry per line. A design's nodes are in the dependency graph's topological order, ties in code-point order, with its edges, parameters, targets and a table's rows in the notation. A turn's rounds hold each call's provenance and each entry's value or marker, then the decision, `escalation` and `defects`; with the design the record ran under, each entry adds its declared reads, params and inputs, marked declared, its wording and targets, and each bound its design declares for it, with the distance in the choice helpers' exact arithmetic and `short` or `clear`. `python -m ommat.outline PATH... [--json] [-o FILE]` prints the files or folders given, each record beside the design whose bytes its `design` item hashes.
- The outline at `outline_version` 2 computes, from the documents alone: for a design, `layout`, each node's column and row, its columns sorted by barycentre sweeps to cross fewer edges; `fingerprint`, a hash of its shape with the names erased; and `short`, a short label per ref. For a turn, `deciding_path`, the entries the decision rests on, back to its root; `near_misses`, every bound nearest first; and `summary`, one line of fixed columns. The text gains a `why` line, a `path` and a `near misses` block, a star on each entry of the path, and folds each call's wire members into one line, which `render_turn(outline, wire=True)` and `--wire` expand; `--oneline` prints each record's summary.
- The lens draws from the outline. `ommat.lens` embeds each design's and each record's outline and text form, and its page draws the design as its dependency graph, one band per round and a sub-column per wavefront, each edge by its role; a turn over that graph, each entry washed by what it holds and marked against the bounds its design declares, the lineage from the decision to its root as a tree, the round costs, and a headline from the outline's `why` line; a panel of every bound by entry; a sortable table of the turns with a tally; and the text form beside them. A record pairs with the design whose hash it holds, else with one of the same name with a notice, else is drawn alone. The page opens outline JSON, the output of `python -m ommat.outline --json`, where it opened records; the Design, Turn and Turns views, the selection folding, the grouping by question identity, each answer's per-label percentages, tracing a selection's lineage through a design alone, and opening a folder of records in the page are gone. `page` also takes designs as a mapping from a name to the bytes.
- The outline at `outline_version` 3 takes the engine's words. A design's node has `kind` `state`, `source` or `rule`, the policy a rule with `policy` true and id `rule:<name>`, and `round`, the round interpret runs it in, and `wavefront`, with `predecessors`; an edge says `dependency`; `layout` has one column per round and wavefront. A turn's `status` is `finished` or `unfinished`, an entry `holds` its value or marker kind and has `nearest`, and the turn has `lineage`, `nearest_bounds` with `on_lineage`, and `absent`. `render_turn(outline, detail=True)` and `--detail` expand each call's `call detail` line.
- The outline at `outline_version` 4 computes, over a design's dependency edges, each node's `steps_to_policy`, the fewest edges to the policy node or `null`; `requirements`, each source with a requirement as `{source, provider, decided_from, behind}`, the node providing its ref, that node and every node it depends on, and every node behind the source; each target's `questions`, `{ref, node, role}`, `role` `asks` or the latent role, `estimated_by`, `readers`, the other rules reading those questions, and `path`, from its nearest estimating rule, else its nearest reader, to the policy; and each table row's `tests`, `{input, read, node, cell}`, the inputs whose cell is not `-`. The text form adds `to policy n` or `no path to the policy` to a node's line, a `requirements` block, and each target's questions, estimators, readers and path. At `outline_version` 5 a turn's outline adds `review`, each source asked, not asked with why, or with no entry, and each table entry's rows matched, not matched or untested, with the row that gave the value and each test's entry; `summary_nearest`, the bound the summary names; and `why`, the text form's why line. The text form ends with a `sources` block and a `table` block per table entry, and the lens reads `why` and `summary_nearest` rather than composing them.
- The lens draws outline version 6 and opens on the Turn tab at the first turn when given records, else on a design's overview. Turn says what took place in the selected record in plain words: the decision, or what an undecided turn rests on and the bound the summary names; each table the turn ran, every row with its result and each test beside what the turn held; the lineage; each source asked, with its questions' wording and answers; each source not asked, grouped by why; the sources that wrote nothing; and, folded, the bounds and the record's details. A menu in the header picks the turn. Its other tabs are Overview, the counts, the policy, the sources with and without a source requirement, the policy table's outputs and the targets' reach; Explore, a slice of the dependency graph around one node in the layout's columns and rows, a sidebar of every round's sources and rules grouped by their requirement, a map of the whole design and the node's details, with the selected turn's entries marked on the slice, the sidebar and the map, the round costs, the lineage starred, and each box showing what its refs hold; Targets, each target's questions, estimating rules, readers and path to the policy, or a matrix; Outcomes, a card per table row with its tests and, with a turn selected, the row's result and the turn's values; Targets also shows what the turn held; and Graph, the dependency graph and turn views as before. A source requirement edge is violet. The hash gains `view`, `up`, `down`, `dir`, `info`, `table` and `tmode`, and Explore's keys are `n`, `p`, `[`, `]`, `i`, `o`, `m` and `/`.
- Combining designs in `ommat.helpers`: `combine(*parts, bind, policy, name=None)` unions parts, each `Part(design, ports)`, its ports the `d:` refs it reads and no declaration of it provides, into one design under a top policy, renaming nothing. `bind` gives each port a rule or table, or a `d:` ref the port takes through a pass-through table. It raises `InvalidCombination`, its `failures` each a `CombineFailure`, listing every failure: parts whose states differ, an id or parameter path two parts declare, a type, group, target or class tree two parts declare differently, a port a part provides, an unbound port and a bind key that is no port, among others; a type, group, target or class tree declared alike is kept once, and load checks the rest. `standalone(part, ports=None, *, given=())` gives each port in `ports` a constant and each port in `given` a value each turn from the source `ports`, under the oracle `ports`, so a part loads and decides alone. The guide [Combine small designs into one](docs/how-to/combine-designs.md) shows a workflow selector and a workflow asked in one call.
- The question-reach job in `ommat.evaluation`: `question_reach(records, loaded, *, values=None)`, async with a `_sync` twin, asks the dead-question lint's question from recorded turns, for a design whose cross product of read points is too large to run. For each record in list order, and each `q:` entry a returned call wrote, in log order, it sets the entry, as `intervene` sets one, to each of its declared question's read points other than its recorded value or marker, a grounded entry's own marker last, a candidate-valued question's each fixed label and then the first candidate at 1 over the labels the call asked, and replays once per point. A trial whose replay reports `stale-question` is undetermined, and any other reaches when the decision it wrote differs from the record's. It returns `{"records", "trials", "questions": {ref: {"status", "witness"}}, "narrowed"}`, each `q:` ref a source provides `live`, with the first reaching trial's record hash, entry ref and value as its witness, `not-determined`, `no-change-found` or `never-asked`. `values` is checked as `dead_questions` checks it, its keys `q:` refs, before any record is read; each record is then refused `invalid-record`, `unfinished`, `nothing-to-replay`, `wrong-design` or `baseline-differs`, naming its turn, before the next is read and before any trial.
- `ommat.helpers.top(r, ref, *, tie="abstain")`, the top label of an answer and its probability; at an exact tie it abstains, or with `tie="first"` gives the tied label first in the answer's `labels`. The helper version is 4, so a record written under version 3 replays with a `helpers-mismatch` finding.
- `ommat.bindings.fixed(values)`, a binding to no provider answering each asked ref from a mapping, for a fact the caller holds, such as the date, or a test's scripted answers; an asked ref it does not hold is `unsupported`, with a line of `warnings`.
- `ommat.DefectWarning`, a `UserWarning` that `decide`, `decide_sync` and each turn of `decide_many` emit once the record closes when it holds a defect, an entry `defects` lists, carrying `record` and `defects` and naming each defect with its traceback. `warnings.simplefilter("error", ommat.DefectWarning)`, or pytest's `filterwarnings = ["error::ommat.DefectWarning"]`, makes such a turn raise with its whole record; replay, the evaluation jobs and `dead_questions` emit none. A turn with a defect prints a warning by default, which an `ignore::ommat.DefectWarning` filter silences.
- `defects(record)`, every entry whose cause is a defect by `escalation`'s test, whatever the policy gave, so a rule that raised on a decided turn is found, and `None` for a record before format 8; and `Record.entries`, each entry item by its ref, for a record of any format.

- An exception's recorded traceback starts at the component's own frame and names each file by its module, so the record holds no local paths from the traceback.
- `read_record` reads format-1 and format-2 records and `load` loads format-1 and format-2 design documents to their own hash, each under its format's rules. A format-2 record holding a described label, `precision`, `warnings`, `unsupported` or a `hash` member is refused.
- `Step` takes its arguments as any `Mapping`, read once through `items()`, and still keeps a read-only copy; a non-mapping is a `TypeError`. The recorded plan is unchanged.
- Check 4's message for a rule that provides another ref names the rule's own `d:` ref, and for another rule's `d:` ref, whose it would be.
- A `load` refusal for a type that does not fit names the type as written: check 9's text and `reference_date` refs, check 10's state declaration and parameter shape, check 8's boundary leaf, and check 9's boundary key and role leaves.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-10-22

### Added

- The `extraction` answer kind: a question that asks for a typed value read from a text. `Question` takes `value_type`, `is_list`, `text` and `reference_date`; `ValueType` names the six value types; `ExtractionAnswer` holds the answer's `value`, `guess`, `p_null`, `confidence` and `span`, and checks each when built. Dates, times, datetimes and durations are checked by jurat's own grammar, alike on every Python.
- `RenderedQuestion` gives an extraction question's `value_type`, `is_list`, `text` and `reference_date`, keyword-only; a question of another kind leaves them at `None`, `False`, `None` and `None`.
- `CallResult.truncated`: the `q:` refs whose answer came from a response the provider cut short, recorded under the call in `unhashed` and outside every hash.
- `jurat.helpers.extract`: an extraction answer's value, or `Unsure` with `abstained` when the value is null or `p_null` is at or above the bar.
- The extraction roles of a `boundaries` entry, `null`, `confidence` and `value`, and the dead-question lint's points over an extraction read; an extraction read with no `value` role takes its values from the lint's `values` argument.

### Changed

- Records and design documents are format 2, and the helper version is 2. Every design hash and record hash changes, so golden hashes must be updated.
- `AnswerKind` has a fourth member, `extraction`; code that matches on every kind must handle it.
- The ledger refuses a `q:` answer whose kind is not its call item's question's.
- 0.2.0 reads format-1 records and loads format-1 design documents to their own hash, each under format 1's rules, so a record written under 0.1.0 replays under its stored design with `helpers-mismatch` as its only finding.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-10-21

### Changed

- `Source.batch` is now `Source.oracle`, the name of the binding that answers the source, and the design document's `batch` member is `oracle`. Every design hash changes, so a record written under 0.0.1 names a design hash that no reloaded design has. `EntryRefusal` messages name the oracle, and a missing `default` binding says it serves the sources with no oracle.
- `load` refuses a source that provides both `q:` and `t:` refs (check 4). Split it into two sources that name one oracle: ready in the same round, they are still one call.

## [0.0.1] - 2026-10-20

### Added

- The design types: `Design`, `Source`, `Question`, `Text`, `Rule`, `Policy`, `When`, `Requirement`, `SourceRequirement`, `Group` and `Parameter`, with `Table`, `PolicyTable` and `PlanDeclaration` for the forms `load` refuses in this release.
- `jurat.types`: constructors for the type a field holds (`boolean`, `integer`, `number`, `string`, `bytes_`, `label`, `list_`, `mapping`, `record` and `nullable`).
- `load`: checks a `Design`, or its canonical document, before any turn runs and returns a `LoadedDesign`; an invalid design raises `InvalidDesign` naming each failed check.
- `decide` and `decide_sync`: one turn, interpret over rounds of sources and rules, then compile with the policy and the guard, returning the plan, the verdict and the record. Rules and the policy are Python functions.
- `RunSettings`: the run settings, with a round bound of 8 and a 60 s timeout by default; `EntryRefusal` for a wrong argument at `decide`'s entry, raised before any record is written.
- The markers `Unsure`, `Unavailable` and `Unasked`, each with its cause, for every value a turn could not settle.
- The record: its canonical JSON encoding and hashes, the observer, and `read_record` for a stored record, which raises `InvalidRecord` for one it cannot read.
- `replay` and `replay_sync`: a recorded turn rerun under a loaded design with no model call, reporting each difference as a finding.
- The record views: `escalation` and `redact`.
- The binding protocol between the engine and a model adapter: `SourceCall`, `RenderedQuestion` and `CallResult`.
- `jurat.helpers`: the choice helpers and combinators rules call, the design lints `dead_questions` and `unsure_to_execute`, and the reference runner `run_plan`, which raises `RunRefusal` for a plan it refuses before calling any tool and returns `BindingNotFound` for a step argument whose binding missed.
- Project scaffolding: package, license, code of conduct, contributing and security policies, CI.

### Not included

- `load` refuses decision tables, policy tables, data rules, plan declarations, candidate sets, candidate-valued label sets and grounded declarations.
- No state carries from one turn to the next.
