# Spec

The normative description of ommat's behavior. The Python package is one implementation of it. JSON conformance fixtures that any implementation, in any language, must pass come later, and the spec will then carry its own version, separate from the package version.

The spec covers what record format 10 and design format 11 hold and the engine does:

- loading a design, and the load checks that refuse an invalid one;
- decide: the turn's entry with the codec, the broken entry, interpret over rounds of sources and rules, functions or tables, the three markers and their causes, the policy, whose estimate is the turn's decision, and the undecided turn's root cause;
- source requirements: a source asked only when an estimate holds one of its values, the estimate not sent unless it is also a need;
- the ledger and the observer, the record with its canonical JSON encoding, order objects and hashes, the ordered encoding a model reads, and reading a stored record back;
- the targets a design declares, the properties its questions ask about, bear on and estimate, for a reader;
- the record views: the escalation view, the defects view, the transcript view, which is deprecated, and the redacted view;
- the conversation renderers, which render a record as its turn history in Chat Completions or Pydantic AI messages;
- the authoring reader, `ommat.authoring`, which reads a parsed mapping as a design or a slice of one, the design the author would write in Python;
- the record lens, which draws stored records and designs, from their outlines, as an HTML page a person reads;
- replay of a recorded turn with no model call, the do-and-diff evaluation jobs `intervene`, `scenario` and `use_answers`, the deciding inputs, the switching-value job, the question-reach job, the gold-answers job, the gold record and the answers table, a design's groups, and the design store and the evaluation store;
- the binding protocol between the engine and a model adapter, the provenance profile each call states, the binding kit and the binding conformance suite;
- the answer kinds: `noul`, `choice` and `score`, which give a probability to each label, and `extraction`, a typed value read from a text, with the probability that the text holds none, the model's reading whatever it answered, its confidence and the span it read;
- the candidate-valued label set: a `choice` whose labels are a fixed part and the members of a `list<string>` candidate set, rendered each time it is asked;
- per-candidate questions: a source grounded over a `list<string>` candidate set asks a question once per candidate, beside its questions asked once;
- the helpers: the choice helpers, `extract` and the per-candidate picks among them, `determined` and the combinators rules call, `decide_many`, which decides many turns at once, and `combine`, which unions small designs into one;
- the live frontier, the answer grid and the `boundaries` fields, and the design lint that reads them;
- decision tables: rule tables under every hit policy, data rules and a policy that is a table among them.

Outside it, and refused by load in a design that declares one, with check 12, which load uses for nothing else: a grounded declaration that is itself a candidate set; a per-candidate extraction question and a per-candidate question whose labels are a candidate-valued set; and a need on a grounded declared ref. Also outside it: the run-time stopping certificate, multi-turn continuation, and the evaluation jobs the spec defers, calibration and the holistic shadow question.

The sections below are written; the rest is not yet.

## Formats

A record and a design document each carry a format number, each its own, which changes only when that document's rules change. This engine writes record format 10 and design format 11. It reads record formats 1 to 10, each under its own rules, and design format 11 alone: load refuses a document of any other format, an earlier one included, with check 11, naming the format it holds. A record of a format before 8 is read but never rerun: it holds no policy's entry, so the views give `None` for it, and replay and the evaluation jobs refuse it as `wrong-design`. A design built in Python is written at the engine's design format, so it has a new hash when that format changes. A design document loads to the hash of its own bytes.

From record format 10 a `calls` entry may hold `extras`, one object per request the call sent (see [The binding protocol](#the-binding-protocol)), its `usage` holds `input_tokens` and `output_tokens` alone, no `missing` key or `claimed` name is `usage.passes`, and the call item's `provider` and `model_requested` each hold no line boundary (see [The provenance profile](#the-provenance-profile)). The reader refuses `extras`, as a member or an `invalid` key, in a record before format 10, and from format 10 a `usage` key `passes`, `usage.passes` as a `missing` key or a `claimed` name, and a line boundary in `provider` or `model_requested`; a record of format 8 or 9 may hold `passes` in each place.

From record format 9 the `settings` item holds `shapes`, each ref's shape tree, in place of `classes`, its class tree: a design declares no redaction class, and the caller gives the redacted view its policy (see [The redacted view](#the-redacted-view)). The reader refuses `shapes` in a record before format 9 and `classes` from it, and requires the one its format holds. A design document holds no `classes` member, which check 11 refuses.

From record format 8 a turn ends at the policy's entry (see [The record document](#the-record-document)): the `settings` item names the `policy`, the `t:state` entry may hold `Unavailable` at a broken entry, the log holds no `plan` or `verdict` item and no `cause` item but `cancelled`, and a `calls` entry's `usage` holds only the closed set of keys (see [The provenance profile](#the-provenance-profile)). The reader refuses a `policy` member in a record before format 8, a `plan` or `verdict` item from format 8, and a `context` item in every format.

A record before format 7 holds no `sets` in its `settings` item, which the reader refuses, while from format 7 the `settings` item requires it (see [The candidate-valued label set](#the-candidate-valued-label-set)). A record before format 6 holds no `order` in any item, which the reader refuses, and from format 6 each `entry` item, and in formats 6 and 7 each `plan` and `verdict` item, requires one. A record before format 6 holds no `grounded` in its `settings` item and no grounded ref, which the reader refuses, and from format 6 the `settings` item requires `grounded` (see [Per-candidate questions](#per-candidate-questions)). A record before format 5 holds no `context` in its `plan` item. A record before format 5 holds `snapshot` in a call item in place of the provenance profile's `provider`, `model_requested`, `model_reported`, `revision`, `runtime` and `claimed`, `precision` only as an integer or null, and no `request_id`, `calibrated`, `missing` or `clients` in a `calls` entry, nor any of these, nor the profile's `provider`, `model_requested`, `model_reported`, `revision`, `runtime` or `claimed`, as an `invalid` key; from format 5 `snapshot` is neither a call item's member nor an `invalid` key. A record before format 7 may hold `calibrated`, a provider's claim that its probabilities are calibrated, as a `calls` entry's member, a `missing` or `invalid` key or a `claimed` name; from format 7 none holds it, and nothing writes it. A format-1 record holds no extraction answer and no `truncated` in its `unhashed`. A format-1 or format-2 record holds no `unsupported` cause, no call item `precision`, no `warnings` in a `calls` entry, no `warnings` or `precision` as an `invalid` key, and no document `hash`. A record before format 4 holds no entry with a `timed-out` or `raised` marker whose call item is recorded `returned`. A record's cause codes are read against its own format's set. The record's `design` item also carries the helper version, which [the choice helpers](#the-choice-helpers) give.

A design document holds each object's order where a model may read it: a source's `payload_order`, the order object of its `payload`; a parameter leaf's `order`, the order object of its `value`; and a structured wording's `order`, the order object of its template, written only when the template is not in canonical order (see [A structured wording](#a-structured-wording)). Load refuses an order object that does not fit its value, and a loaded design holds each payload and each leaf's value in the order its order object gives, so a binding and `r.param` receive them as declared.

## The record document

From format 3 the record document is `{"hash", "log", "unhashed"}`, where `hash` is the record hash over the log, derived from the log's bytes when the record is written. A reader requires it, recomputes it and refuses a mismatch, and refuses any other member. It is never read as truth. It catches an edit made without recomputing it. It does not catch an adversary, whom only a turn id held outside the record catches. A job that edits a record's log recomputes the hash. The redacted view carries `hash` as an open value.

A format-10 log, as a format-9 one, is a list of items, each an object whose `item` member names its kind, in this order. The `design` item, `{"hash", "format", "helpers"}`, comes first, and the `settings` item second: `settings`, the run settings in force; `providers`, each provided ref's source or rule; `shapes`, the shape tree of `t:state` and of each provided ref; `grounded`; `sets`; and `policy`, the policy's ref, a `d:` key of `providers` with no field path and no key of `grounded`. The `t:state` entry follows in round 0. Each round then holds its `call` items and the `entry` items the calls and the rules write, and a `round` item, `{"round", "cost", "deadline"}`, closes it. The policy's entry is among them, written by its rule as any entry is, in the round its reads settle or at the drain of a cut. A finished record holds the policy's entry and no `cause` item; a record an observer's exception ended after the policy's entry holds it, and is finished. At a broken entry the `t:state` entry is followed by the policy's entry, the log's last item, in round 0 with `call` null and the same marker (see [Decide](#decide)). A cancellation ends the log at the `cause` item, `{"cause": {"code": "cancelled", "refs": []}}`, the one `cause` item a record from format 8 holds. The reader refuses any other order.

A shape tree is a ref's type's structure: an object at each record, keyed by its fields, a one-member list at each mapping layer, and the string `leaf` at every other node, a list or `or null` layer having no node of its own; an extraction answer's is a record of its eight members. A class tree, which a record before format 9 holds, has that structure with a class name, `open`, `identifier` or `content`, at each leaf. The reader holds each entry's value to its ref's tree: an object where the tree has one, its keys among the record's fields; from format 9 it refuses a shape tree of any other form, whether or not an entry is held to it. Load refuses a design whose provided refs' types, with `t:state`'s, spell more than 2^20 code points in all with their definitions resolved, since every record writes each of their shape trees in full.

A model reads the order of an object's members, so key order is part of what the model sees, as the wording is: two orders of one state may draw two different answers. ommat keeps every object's order from where it is declared or built to the text a model reads, and chooses none. A record type's fields stand in the order declared, so `ommat.types.record(b=..., a=...)` spells `record{b: ..., a: ...}`, the design hash covering that order, and a record value's fields stand in its type's order. Any other object keeps the order its author or producer gave it. A record type conforms to one with the same fields in another order.

The canonical encoding is RFC 8785's JSON: each object's members sorted by key, no whitespace. Every hash is over it, so a hash stays interoperable. The ordered encoding writes a value as the canonical encoding does, but with each object's members in the order the value holds them: strings and numbers as RFC 8785 writes them, each key by its string data, lists in order, and no whitespace. It takes a value in the value domain, returns text, and raises `ValueError` where the canonical encoding does. It is the text a model reads.

An order object records the order of some data's objects beside its canonical bytes. It names each object whose members are not in the canonical key order: the object's index among the data's objects, counted from 0 in pre-order as the canonical encoding writes the data, as decimal text, mapped to its members' positions in the canonical key order, listed in the order held. It is `{}` when every object is in canonical order. It holds no key, so it reveals no content, and it grows with the data. The data rebuilt from an order object holds each object it names in that order and every other in the canonical key order; a reader refuses an order object that is not the order object of some ordering of its data. The order object enters the hash beside the data, so two orders are two designs or two records.

From format 6 each `entry` item, and in formats 6 and 7 each `plan` and `verdict` item, holds `order`, the order object of the item with its own members in canonical order. The ledger derives it at the append from the order each object is given in; no producer writes it. `read_record` takes each such item's `order` off and rebuilds the item in that order before the append, refusing an item with no `order` or one whose `order` does not fit, and the record hash refuses an `order` the append would not derive. `Record.log`, the observer and every other reader get each such item with its `order`, each object in the order it gives. `redact` derives each item's `order` afresh over the item as the redacted view writes it.

A `Record`'s `entries` is a `dict` from each entry item's ref, a grounded ref as written, to the item as its `log` gives it, for a record of any format and decoded afresh at each read. The ledger writes one entry per ref, so it holds every entry, its keys in log order.

A call's `unhashed.calls` entry holds `start`, `latency`, `usage` and `attempts`, and, each only when it holds something, `unexpected`, `invalid`, `sums`, `truncated`, `warnings`, `request_id`, `missing`, `clients` and, from format 10, `extras`. `extras` is a non-empty list of objects, not all empty, no longer than `attempts` when that is an integer; the reader refuses it off that shape and size-tests it as one value. As every `unhashed` member it is stored in the canonical encoding: each object's keys sorted, the list in its order, with no order object.

An exception's `traceback` in `unhashed` is CPython's text. It is built as `traceback.TracebackException` with its source lines looked up, its frames edited, then formatted. Along the chain and each exception group, a stack drops its leading run of frames of `ommat`, `asyncio` or a module under either, so its first frame is the component's. A stack made only of such frames, as a builtin component's is, keeps them all. Each remaining frame's filename becomes the frame's `__name__` global when that is a string, else the text after its last `/` or `\`; a pseudo-file such as `<string>` is kept. The same rule holds for a rule, the policy among them, a function `when`, the codec and a binding. This does not make a raw record shareable: the redacted view is the form to share.

## The binding protocol

Interpret calls a binding as `binding(batch, seconds=s)`: `s` is a positive `float`, the smaller of the batch's timeout and the time left to the deadline, read on the monotonic clock as the call starts, and the engine's timeout ends the call at that same instant. A binding converts it to its provider's deadline once and invents no budget of its own. A binding that does not take the keyword raises `TypeError` at its first call, so the call is `Failed` with `raised` on every ref of the batch. The fixture binding and `from_record` take it and ignore it.

A binding receives a batch's sources in source id order, and each source's refs and questions in the code-point order of their refs. `SourceCall.questions` keys a per-candidate question by each of its grounded refs, so its candidates stand in the code-point order of those refs, not the set's order (see [Per-candidate questions](#per-candidate-questions)). That order is part of the contract: a provider may answer a question differently under another order or beside other questions, so the order is part of what ran, as the wording is. A binding for such a provider may ask one question per request or frame its own order, and names that method in `elicitation`.

A source's `needs` is a fresh `dict` keyed by each need as declared, in code-point order. Each value keeps its own order: a record value's fields in its type's declared order, and any other object, a payload's included, in the order its producer gave it. A binding sends each object in the order it receives it, since the model reads that order. A design whose model needs its state in another order declares one record need, in that order, and has the binding send it whole, as the idia binding's `state` does.

Each round, the ready sources that name one oracle form one batch, sent to that oracle's binding in one call, and the ready sources that name none form one more, sent to `default`. A round's calls run concurrently, so a binding bound under several names, or as `default`, is called concurrently with itself. A batch's timeout is the largest its sources declare, else the run settings' `timeout`, 60 seconds by default. Decide refuses with `EntryRefusal`, before any record is written, a `bindings` that lacks an oracle a source names, lacks `default` when a source names none, or holds a key other than `default` that no source names.

Interpret tests what a call returns at the write. The call is `Failed`, and every ref of the batch `Unavailable` with its code, for the first of these that applies: `timed-out` when the call has not returned or raised before its end, whatever it then returns or raises; `raised` for a raise; a returned `Failed`'s code when it is `raised`, `timed-out`, `invalid-return` or `unexpected-ref`, and `invalid-return` for any other `Failed`, for a return that is neither exactly a `CallResult` nor exactly a `Failed`, and for a `values` that is not exactly a `dict` or holds two keys of one string; `unexpected-ref` for a key the batch does not provide; and `invalid-return` for a provenance member or `precision` out of its form. Otherwise each ref is written from its value: a ref left out is `Unavailable` with `not-returned`; a `q:` value off its kind's form, or a span past its text, is `invalid-answer`; a `t:` value its type does not hold, or a candidate set's that repeats a member, is `invalid-return`; and a marker that is not exactly one of the six a binding may return is `invalid-return`. A binding that cannot use a provider's reply returns the code interpret would write: `invalid-answer` for a `q:` ref, `invalid-return` for a `t:` ref.

A `choice` or `score` label is a name or exactly `{"name": n, "description": d}`. A `noul` declares no labels, or exactly `yes` then `no` in that form with at least one described; its answer is still a probability for each. Both are strings; the description holds a character that is not whitespace and has no slots. Names are distinct; descriptions may repeat. A candidate-valued set's `fixed` part takes the same form. `extraction` declares no labels. Load's count bound counts labels; its code-point bound sums names and descriptions. The answer grid, the lint, load's `boundaries` checks and every answer read label names alone. The design document, the question's identity and the call item's `questions` hold the labels as declared, a candidate-valued set's as its `{"fixed", "candidates"}` object, so a description change makes a new question identity.

`RenderedQuestion.wording` is the rendered wording: a string, or for a structured wording an exact JSON object or array. `RenderedQuestion.labels` holds the names. `descriptions`, keyword-only, is aligned with them: `None` where a label is undescribed, `(None, None)` for an undescribed `noul`, `()` for `extraction`. A `RenderedQuestion` built with none gets `None` for each label. A candidate-valued question is rendered from its set's entry at each call, its set's members placed among its fixed part's names at `at`, each member's description `None`, so the labels of one `q:` ref may differ from call to call; a binding keeps no labels by ref. They are distinct and there is at least one; a binding whose provider takes fewer marks the ref `unsupported`. The set reaches the binding only as these labels, unless the source declares it a need.

`RenderedQuestion` and `SourceCall` check their fields when built, raising `TypeError` for a field of the wrong type and `ValueError` for a wrong value: `kind` and `value_type` a member or its string, stored as the member; labels `yes` and `no` for `noul` and none for `extraction`; `descriptions` as long as `labels`; an extraction question's `value_type` and `text` present and a probability kind's extraction members unset; and a source call's `refs` and `questions` not both non-empty. The checks read types, lengths and the kind's members only. `CallResult` checks nothing; interpret tests it at the write.

`CallResult.warnings` conforms when it is exactly a `tuple` of exact `str`, each holding a character that is not whitespace and in the value domain, at most 2^8 of them and 2^16 code points in all; order is kept and a repeat is allowed. Interpret tests it on every `CallResult`, a structural fault included. A conforming one is written under the call's `unhashed.calls` entry as `warnings` only when non-empty. A non-conforming one is never a fault: nothing is written and its repr goes under `invalid["warnings"]`. A late result keeps none. `redact` digests the list as one value.

`CallResult.extras` holds whatever a provider reports beyond the provenance profile, the closed `usage` keys among it, as one JSON object per request the call sent, its `attempts` above 0, in the order the requests were given, not the order they completed: `{}` for a request that reports nothing more, and `()` when every one is empty or none was sent. An element is tied to its request by that order alone, never to a ref or a `request_id`. It conforms when it is `None` or exactly a `tuple` or `list` of at most 2^8 exact `dict`s, counted before any is read, each key an exact `str`, the whole a value of the value domain whose canonical encoding spells at most 2^16 code points, and no longer than `attempts` when `attempts` is an `int`. Interpret tests it on every `CallResult`, a structural fault included, and writes a conforming one, copied, under the call's `unhashed.calls` entry as `extras` unless every object is empty. One off its form is never a fault: nothing is written and its repr goes under `invalid["extras"]`. A late result keeps none. It is outside every hash and is no detail of the provenance profile: it takes no reason in `missing` and no name in `claimed`. `redact` digests the list as one value.

`CallResult.precision` says how the provider rounds probabilities: an integer from 0 to 17, `bool` excluded, the decimal places; the string `unrounded` when it does not round; exactly `{"step": s}`, `s` a string of `0.` then at most 17 digits ending in one from 1 to 9 and no power of ten, for a step no count of places gives, such as `"0.05"`; or `None` when the binding does not know, with a reason (see [The provenance profile](#the-provenance-profile)). Any other value is `invalid-return` for the whole call, with `invalid["precision"]`. It is hashed on the call item, present on every call item from format 3, null on a failed one; `unrounded` and the step are format 5's. It is the provider's declaration, recorded and unchecked: the engine does not test that probabilities are rounded to it, and rounds none. A `noul` whose provider gives one probability holds `yes` as given and `no` as the binding's provider library derives it. The fixture binding returns `None`, as it returns no provenance and no `truncated` or `warnings`.

A binding that will not put a question to any provider, because its ref, kind or shape is outside what the provider takes, returns `Unavailable` with the code `unsupported` for that ref. A binding that refuses a whole batch marks every ref. Interpret writes the marker with a cause naming that ref; the cause carries no text. The binding gives its reason as a line of `warnings`. The call is `returned` and charged, and its call item holds null provenance where no provider was called. `unsupported` is one of the six markers a binding may return, with `refused`, `invalid-answer`, `invalid-return`, `timed-out` and `raised`. It is never a `Failed` outcome's code (a `Failed` carrying it is `invalid-return`). A rule that returns the marker it read provides it unchanged, so `unsupported` stands on a `d:` entry with its refs as any binding code does. Replay's fixture returns it as recorded.

When a binding sends a batch as several provider requests and one ends with no answer, each ref that request carried is `Unavailable` with `timed-out` when the request ran out of the call's `seconds`, or with `raised` when it failed any other way, and the other refs stand; the request's status and message go in lines of `warnings`. The call is `returned` and charged, and its provenance, `attempts`, `truncated` and `warnings` are written as for any returned call. A `timed-out` or `raised` on a ref under a `returned` call is the binding's provider request; `Failed` with `timed-out` on the call is the engine's timeout. Replay's fixture returns either marker as recorded; `from_record` returns it as `unsupported`.

A need whose value is a `dict` of exactly `media_type`, a `str` naming an image media type such as `image/png`, and `data`, the image's `bytes`, is an image, declared as `record(media_type=string, data=bytes_)`; a binding whose provider takes images sends it as one.

`from_record(record, loaded)` returns an oracle binding that answers a caller's live turn from a stored record, a `Record` or a document read through `read_record`; a `loaded` that is not exactly a `LoadedDesign` is an `EntryRefusal`. Bound under the oracle names whose sources the record should answer, beside live bindings under the rest, it answers each source from the returned call item that lists it and each ref from the entry that call wrote, in the binding's form. It refuses with `unsupported`: every ref of a source the record holds no returned call for, or whose `needs` differ from the record's entries at the same refs, `t:state` included, by their ordered encoding, so a need in another order differs, or by canonical bytes for a record before format 6, which holds no order; a question whose kind, labels as declared, value type, list flag or question identity under `loaded` differ from the call item's `questions` entry, a per-candidate question's under its declared ref, so a question reworded under another design is refused; a ref with no stored entry naming its call, a candidate the stored turn did not ask included; and a ref whose recorded marker is not `invalid-answer` or `invalid-return`, which come back as recorded, so a recorded `refused` comes back `unsupported`. Each refusal is a line of `warnings`. It has no provenance, its `usage`, `attempts`, `request_id`, `missing` and `clients` are null and `truncated` is empty; its first `warnings` line is `answered from record <hash>`, and its lines are bounded by `bounded_warnings`. Nothing in the hashed record tells these call items from live ones. These checks are replay's stale-question check (see [Replay](#replay)); a binding cannot see a label set's ref, so a set named otherwise holding the same members is answered.

`ommat.bindings` holds the binding kit, what a binding author calls, never imported by `import ommat`; with it `DETAILS`, the provenance profile's detail names, `REASONS`, the reasons a `missing` gives, and `USAGE_KEYS`, the keys a `usage` holds, so a binding is written against the protocol with no provider library. The names its `__all__` lists are public, as are those `ommat.__all__`, `ommat.types.__all__`, `ommat.helpers.__all__` and `ommat.evaluation.__all__` list. They carry no stability promise before 1.0, when a compatibility policy is set. `ommat.bindings.conformance` and the bindings ommat ships are outside them. `OracleBinding` is the type of a binding, `async def binding(batch, *, seconds)`, which `ommat.bindings.idia.oracle_binding` returns. `extraction_value(value, guess, p_null, confidence, span)` returns an extraction answer's five-member `dict`, each `date`, `time`, `datetime` or `timedelta` in `value` or `guess`, or in a list of either, written in the grammar below by its exact type, a tuple as a list and `span` as lists; it raises `ValueError` for a negative duration, a duration with a fraction of a second, an offset that is not a whole number of minutes or an aware value with no offset, and checks nothing else. A zero offset is written `Z`, a fraction with six digits, a duration with days as `nD` and `PT0S` for zero. `bounded_warnings(lines)` returns a conforming `warnings` from any lines of `str`: each surrogate escaped as the ledger escapes one, an empty line or one of whitespace alone left out, the order kept, and past either bound, counted after the escapes, a last line `<n> more lines left out` (`1 more line left out` for one). `json_value(value)` returns a need's value in record form: each `Answer` and `ExtractionAnswer` as its document, a tuple as a list, each type matched exactly and any other value as given; it raises `TypeError` for `bytes` at any depth, which a JSON part cannot carry. `image_need(value)` returns the `(media_type, data)` pair of an image need, each member's type matched exactly, and `None` for any other value. `prompt_text(prompt)` returns a `str` or `None` as given, and a mapping as `json.dumps(prompt)` with its defaults, each mapping's keys in the order given at every depth, sequences in order and a tuple written as a list, so the text keeps the order the model read; a key that is not a `str`, which JSON would write as one, and any other value, within a mapping or in place of one, is a `TypeError`. `call_result(values, requests, *, precision, warnings=())` returns the `CallResult` of a call sent as `requests`, each a `RequestProvenance(*, provider=None, model_requested=None, model_reported=None, revision=None, runtime=None, elicitation=None, prompt=None, claimed=(), usage=None, attempts=0, request_id=None, missing=None, clients=None, truncated=(), extras=None)`, which checks nothing when built, read only from the requests whose `attempts` is above 0: the one `provider` and the one `model_requested` they name, a `ValueError` when they name two of either, a null mixing with none, `None` when none names one; each distinct `model_reported`, `revision`, `elicitation`, `prompt` and `request_id` one per line in the order first given, `None` when none has one, so a call whose `model_reported` or `elicitation` spans lines has no one calibration key; `runtime` and `clients` each merged, a package at two versions a `ValueError`; `claimed` the union, sorted; `missing` a detail's reason when every request gives one for it and the result holds no value for it, the first reason other than `not-sent`, else `not-sent`; `usage` summed per key over the requests that report it, `None` when none does; `attempts` summed; `truncated` sorted and distinct; `warnings` as given; `extras` each request's copied as a `dict`, `{}` for one with none, in the order given, `()` when every one is empty. When no request made an attempt, the result has no provenance and `attempts`, `precision` and `missing` are `None`. `fixed(values)` is a binding to no provider, for a fact the caller holds or a test's scripted answers: it answers each ref a batch asks, a grounded ref as interpret passes it, from the mapping `values`, in the binding's form and unchecked, a marker among them as given, so a bad value fails its own ref at the write; a held ref the batch does not ask is left out, and an asked ref it does not hold is `Unavailable` with `unsupported`, with the line `no fixed value for <ref>`. It copies `values` when made and each value at each call, has no provenance and a null `precision`, and never waits; a `values` that is not a mapping, or holds a key that is not exactly a `str`, is a `TypeError`.

`ommat.bindings.conformance.check(binding, *, batches=None, deterministic=False)` holds a binding to this protocol: it calls the binding with each of `batches`, or with its own, a source of each answer kind, one needing an image, and a `t:` source, each alone, then one question asked over three label sets as a candidate-valued question is rendered, its candidates after or before its fixed labels, then a source asking a question per candidate beside one asked once, its candidates out of their grounded refs' code-point order and their refs holding escapes and a non-ASCII character, then all the sources in one batch, and returns its failures as strings, each naming its check (`seconds`, `returns`, `values`, `markers`, `answers`, `per-ref-cause`, `warnings`, `precision`, `provenance`, `unsupported-provenance`, `deterministic`); an empty list passes. `answers` judges each `q:` value by interpret's own test; `per-ref-cause` asks a `warnings` line beside a `timed-out` or `raised` ref of a binding whose `attempts` is above 0, and of none other; `provenance` asks each detail of the provenance profile its form, `provider` and `model_requested` with no line boundary, `usage` of `input_tokens` and `output_tokens` alone, `extras` in its form, no longer than `attempts` and empty when no request was sent, a value or a reason for each detail of a result whose `attempts` is above 0, never both of any result, no provenance in one whose `attempts` is null or 0, and `claimed` only over details holding a value; `unsupported-provenance` asks a result whose every ref is `unsupported` for empty `extras` too; `deterministic` compares two calls' results but for `request_id`, which a provider gives each request afresh, and `extras`, whose per-request figures may differ from run to run. It runs its own event loop, and raises `RuntimeError` when called inside a running one. `from_record`, the fixture binding, `fixed` and the tutorial's binding pass it.

## The provenance profile

Each call item states who answered and how, the details idia's request outcome states too. The hashed details, on the call item from format 5: `provider`, the provider's name, a provider library's registered name where one names it; `model_requested` and `model_reported`, the model the binding asked for and the one the provider said answered; `provider` and `model_requested` each name the one service and model the call asked, so from format 10 each holds no line boundary, no character `str.splitlines` ends a line at; `revision`, the exact version of the model's weights or service, pinned or resolved; `runtime`, the code that ran the model, an object from package to version; `elicitation`; `precision`; and `prompt`. Beside them `claimed` lists, by name in code-point order, the details the source asserts and cannot verify, each holding a value. The details kept in the call's `calls` entry, outside every hash: `usage.input_tokens` and `usage.output_tokens`, and `request_id`, the provider's id for the request. `clients`, the versions of the libraries that made the call, sits there too and is no detail, as do the call's `extras`, whatever else its provider reports per request (see [The binding protocol](#the-binding-protocol)). A router that chooses the model puts the one that answered in `model_reported`, and names it in `claimed` when it cannot verify it. A design that asks one question of an ensemble, or of a model chosen per source, declares two oracles, each bound to one provider and one model.

A call that sent a request reports `attempts` above 0, and each detail it leaves null has a reason in its `calls` entry's `missing`, unless the call failed: `not-reported`, the provider or its runtime gives none; `not-pinned`, no fixed version was asked for or resolved; `not-sent`, the request failed, whether it was sent or not; `hidden`, the provider's service withholds it; `not-applicable`, the detail has no meaning for this source, as `runtime` for a wire API; `not-exposed`, a library between the provider and the binding has it but does not make it public. A detail with a value has no reason, and `attempts` and `claimed` never take one. A call that sent no request, `attempts` null or 0, has no provenance, its hashed details null and `claimed` empty, and owes no reason: a binding that called no model, the fixture binding and a `Failed` call hold it so. The conformance suite's `provenance` check holds a binding to these rules; interpret tests each member's form and writes what the binding gave.

Interpret tests them at the write. A hashed detail off its form, a string detail not null or a string of the value domain, a `runtime` not null or a non-empty object of strings, or a `claimed` not a tuple of distinct detail names each holding a value, fails the call with `invalid-return`; `claimed` is written sorted. A `request_id`, `missing` or `clients` off its form fails nothing: it is not written, and its repr goes under `invalid`. A `provider` or `model_requested` holding a line boundary is off its form too. The usage keys are a closed set, `input_tokens` and `output_tokens`: a `usage` that holds another key, `passes` among them, or a figure that is not an `int` of magnitude at most 2^53-1 or a finite `float`, also fails nothing, its `usage` written null and its repr under `invalid["usage"]`; a `missing` naming `usage.passes` is likewise not written, its repr under `invalid["missing"]`, and a `claimed` naming it fails the call. The reader refuses a `usage` key outside `input_tokens`, `output_tokens` and `passes` in a record of format 8 or 9, and outside the two from format 10. A `missing` that gives a reason for a detail holding a value is off its form. A `Failed` call holds no provenance and no `missing`; its `calls` entry keeps `usage`, `attempts`, `request_id` and `clients` as any call's does. The redacted view digests `prompt` and `request_id`, which may echo an id the caller supplied, and keeps the other details open.

The calibration key, by which a calibration job outside the engine would group a question's answers against their gold answers, and which the engine neither builds nor stores, is the question identity, the provider, the model, the reported one else the requested, the revision, whether `model_reported`, else `model_requested`, is in `claimed`, and whether `revision` is, and the elicitation; the runtime moves scores but asks no new question, so it stays out of the key. A reader of a call item before format 5 takes a non-null `snapshot` as a claimed revision, with null provider and model and nothing else claimed.

## The idia binding

`ommat.bindings.idia`, installed by the `idia` extra and never imported by `import ommat`, binds an oracle to a provider through the idia library. `oracle_binding(provider, *, state=None)` takes an idia `Dispatcher` over one leaf and refuses, with `ValueError`, any other provider, a dispatcher whose re-ask is on and one whose overflow policy is not `Fail`, since a second answer or a trimmed state is not what the call item records, a leaf whose declared probability decimals fall outside 0 to 17, and a `state` that is not a non-empty string. A design that asks label kinds and extractions of different models binds one dispatcher per oracle. `state` names a need that a label source sends as the whole JSON part, its value alone in place of an object keyed by refs, so a caller sends the state shape it measured a provider on, since the wrapping is part of what the model reads.

Each call sets one idia deadline from `seconds`; seconds idia's deadline cannot hold send nothing, and every ref is `unsupported` with the reason as a line. The sources of a call that send an equal state with equal options go as one idia request, each source's questions in the order received after the earlier sources', keyed by their refs, each label carrying its description; a source whose joined request idia's preflight refuses goes as a request of its own, and a source whose state differs goes alone. A request's lines name its sources, joined by `+` when it holds several, and a structural fault on it fails the refs of every source it holds. A source that asks an extraction sends the text its spans index as the state's first part, so a span indexes part 0, with its reference date in the request's options and its other needs not sent; a source that asks only label kinds sends its needs as one JSON part keyed by ommat's refs, never renamed, or, when `state` names the source's one need besides its images, that need's value as the part. A label source that needs `state` beside another need, images aside, is `unsupported`, with its reason as a line; an extraction source, a source that does not need `state`, and an image need named by `state` send as they would without it. Each object goes in the order the binding receives it. A source that asks both sends the extraction's state, so its label questions see the text alone. An image need follows as an image part. A described `noul` is sent with idia's `yes` and `no` labels and their descriptions, an undescribed one with none; a structured wording is sent as idia's JSON instruction, each array a tuple. A question whose described `noul` or structured wording the provider's capabilities do not carry, `carries_noul_descriptions` or `carries_structured_instructions` false, is `unsupported` alone before any call, with a line naming the form. A question idia's `Question` refuses, a `choice` rendered with fewer than two labels among them, is `unsupported` alone, with its reason as a line, and the source's other questions are sent; a source left with none sends no request. These are `unsupported`, each with its reason as a line: a `t:` ref; a source whose extractions read different texts or name different reference dates; a need a JSON part cannot carry; a question idia cannot build; and a request idia's preflight refuses. The requests run concurrently.

A `noul` answer is `yes` as the provider gave it and `no` as idia's `noul_complement` derives it; a `choice` or `score` answer is its probabilities by name, idia's chosen label and confidence left out, so a tie at the top abstains in the picks, and in `top` at its default `tie`, where the provider named one; a refusal is `refused`; a fault is `invalid-answer` with a line naming its rules; an extraction is written through `extraction_value`, and a value it refuses or a span into a part other than the text is `invalid-answer` with a line. Each ref of a wire request that did not answer is `timed-out` when its error is idia's `DeadlineExceeded`, the request the call's time could not hold, else `raised`, with the request's status and message as a line; the other refs stand. Each outcome's `provider`, `model_requested`, `model_reported`, `elicitation`, `request_id` and `usage` go under the same names, its `snapshot` as `revision`, its `versions` as `runtime`, its `prompt_sent` as `prompt` and its `client_versions` as `clients`, an empty mapping as null, and its `unreported` reasons and `claimed` under those names, never a reason idia did not give; its `calibrated`, a claim nothing checks, is dropped, from `unreported` and `claimed` too; a prompt the leaf built as a mapping is written through `prompt_text`, as JSON in the order the leaf built it, the form idia's recorded log keeps. `usage` sums each outcome's input and output tokens, `extras` holds each sent outcome's `extras` as idia gives them, its raw usage never read, `attempts` each outcome's attempts, `truncated` holds the refs of an outcome the provider cut short, and `precision` is the answering leaf's rounding, its probability decimals or `unrounded` for `None`, and `None` when no request made an attempt; a package at two versions, or two providers or requested models, across the outcomes fails the call `invalid-return`; each outcome is one `RequestProvenance`, folded by `call_result`. Every line goes through `bounded_warnings`, each need through `json_value` and each image need through `image_need`.

Importing it without idia installed raises `ModuleNotFoundError` naming the extra; any other import failure propagates.

ommat's end-to-end and tutorial tests of the binding replay live runs from idia's `Recorded` logs and call no provider. A log recorded before idia kept the order sent is re-keyed offline by idia, exactly, since each request in it is text or a one-key JSON part whose value holds no object. Its `prompt_sent`, and so the call prompts a replay from it writes, keep sorted keys until the log is recorded again live.

In the binding's source, each site that writes a marker, rounds or raises carries a `# spec: <section>` comment naming the section of this spec that states the rule it keeps, its heading as written.

## The candidate-valued label set

A `choice` may declare its labels as `{"fixed": [...], "candidates": ref, "at": k}`: a fixed part, possibly empty, in the form of any label list, the ref of a candidate set, and `at`, how many fixed labels come before the candidates, an integer from 0 to the fixed part's length. A `Design` that leaves `at` out puts the candidates last; the document always writes it, and the question's identity hashes it. A candidate set is a source's `t:` ref declared `{"type": list<string>, "candidates": true}`, or the `d:` ref of a rule declared with `candidates=True` whose `outputs` is `list<string>`; or a list of a record type whose `candidates` member names its candidate field, a field of type exactly `string`, each other field a scalar, a scalar or null, or a list of a scalar. A member's candidate value is the member itself, or a record member's candidate field: labels, grounded refs, a candidate slot, a per-candidate read's keys and an `in` cell over the set all use it. Load refuses a label set naming any other ref, a candidate set of any other type, `true` over a list of records and a field name over `list<string>`, and the name `candidate` for any declaration. A set's members are distinct by the canonical bytes of their candidate values: a value that repeats one is `Unavailable` with `invalid-return`, naming the set's ref for a source's set and the rule for a rule's, and is never merged. The asking source has an edge from the set's provider; the set is among its needs only when it declares it.

After the source's own need, requirement and `when` markers, each candidate-valued question takes the first of these that applies, naming the set's ref, with `call` null, and is left out of its batch: a marker on the set, `Unavailable` with `need-unavailable` or `Unasked` with `need-undetermined`; an empty set, `Unasked` with `when-false`; rendered labels past load's bounds, more than 2^18 names or more than 2^24 code points of names and the fixed part's descriptions, `Unavailable` with `label-overflow`; and a member equal to a fixed name, `Unavailable` with `label-collision`. The source's other refs are asked. A source left with no ref to ask is not called and not charged. When the budget or the deadline cuts the round, a question left out keeps its own marker and the source's other refs take the cut's. A key a binding returns for a question left out is `unexpected-ref`.

Otherwise the question is asked over its labels in the declared order, the first `at` fixed names, then the set's members in the set's order, then the rest of the fixed names, and its answer is validated against them. Its identity hashes the fixed part and the set's ref, never the members. Replay's `stale-question`, clause `question`, names it when the set's entry in the rerun holds members, in order, other than in the base record. `from_record` rebuilds a stored question's labels from its fixed part and the set's entry in the stored record, and refuses the question when that entry is missing or a marker. In the redacted view, each label and `probabilities` key of its answer that is not a fixed name takes the set's class; a set digested whole leaves an opened answer showing how many members it held. The dead-question lint answers it over the labels each run renders, each fixed label on top and then a member, and never reports it on the grid.

## Per-candidate questions

A source may declare `grounded=ref`, naming a `list<string>` candidate set as [The candidate-valued label set](#the-candidate-valued-label-set) defines one. A question of a grounded source whose wording holds a slot with the origin `{"candidate": ref}`, naming that set, is per-candidate; at least one of its questions is. Load refuses a candidate slot in an ungrounded source or naming another set, and a ref with a grounded bracket in a need, read, slot, group, label-set ref or a source's `grounded`, and a group whose `q:` refs mix grounded and ungrounded ones or are grounded over different sets. This engine also refuses a grounded source or rule that provides a candidate set, a per-candidate extraction question, a per-candidate question whose labels are a candidate-valued set, and a need or slot ref naming a per-candidate question's declared ref.

A per-candidate question is asked once per candidate, in the same request as the source's other questions, which are asked once. Each is asked under its grounded ref: the declared ref, then `[`, then the candidate as RFC 8785 serializes a string, then `]`, such as `q:email/folder_role["Dana"]`. A reader parses the bracketed string with a JSON parser, never by searching for `]`. The slot renders the candidate; every other slot, the labels and the kind are the declared question's, and its one identity covers every candidate. The source has an edge from the set's provider, and the set is among its needs only when it declares it, so a binding otherwise gets the candidates in the wordings alone.

What the set holds decides what is written for the question. Over a valued set, interpret writes one entry per candidate at its grounded ref, and the source's own need, requirement and `when` markers, and a cut's, go on each grounded entry. Over an empty set nothing is asked or written for the question; a source left with nothing to ask is not called, nor is its `when`, and is not charged. The source's own markers win over the set's. Over a set holding a marker nothing is asked for it, and one entry is written at its declared ref: `Unavailable` with `need-unavailable`, or `Unasked` with `need-undetermined` for `Unsure` or `Unasked`, naming the set's ref, with `call` null. When asking ends before the set is on the board, the declared ref holds the cut's marker. A key a binding returns outside the grounded refs it was given is `unexpected-ref`.

A rule reads a per-candidate question by its declared ref. The read returns the marker written there, or else a `dict` from each candidate, in the set's order, to its grounded entry's `Answer` or marker, `{}` for an empty set; `require` stops only on the marker at the declared ref. Load adds the set's ref to the read set of each component that reads the declared ref. No read set holds a grounded ref, so a one-ref choice helper takes no single candidate's answer; `pick_each_with_margin` and `pick_each_with_thresholds` pick every candidate of the `dict` at once (see [the choice helpers](#the-choice-helpers)).

## Grounded sources of `t:` refs

A source grounded over a candidate set may provide `t:` refs, facts about each member: each is grounded and declares the type of one candidate's entry, and none is a candidate set. The engine forms each candidate's grounded ref, `t:x["<candidate value>"]`, and passes them in `refs`, in code-point order; the binding returns a value under each, reading the candidate with `ommat.bindings.candidate_of(ref)`, and, when the source declares its set a need, finding a record candidate's other fields as the member whose candidate field equals it. Each value is checked and written as any `t:` value, so a fault or a missing key marks that entry alone, and the declared ref as a key is `unexpected-ref`. The marker paths of [Per-candidate questions](#per-candidate-questions) hold for it.

A per-candidate question may hold a slot on a declared ref grounded over its own set, a field path allowed: the engine fills it with that candidate's entry, and the binding gets it among its needs under the grounded ref, field path kept, `t:x["Ana"].city`, which `from_record` compares. After the source's own markers, a marker there leaves out that candidate's question alone, `Unavailable` with `need-unavailable` or `Unasked` with `need-undetermined`, naming each such needs key of the winning kind once, in code-point order, with `call` null. Load refuses a slot on a grounded declared ref in any other question, and a need on one.

## Grounded rules and tables

A function rule or a table may declare `grounded=ref`, naming a candidate set. It is one node of the dependency graph, with an edge from the set's provider, and the one provider of its declared ref and of every ref grounded from it. Interpret runs it once per candidate, in the set's order, each on a read view whose current candidate is that candidate, and writes the entries once all have run, one per candidate at its grounded ref, `d:x["<candidate value>"]`, in ref code-point order. Each call's result is its own entry's alone: a raise, an invalid return, a stop or an undeclared read marks that entry, a function rule's entry holds its own call's `derivation`, and a table's its own `rows`, a `C` table aggregating that candidate's rows. Over a set holding a marker it writes the set's need marker at the declared ref, `Unavailable` with `need-unavailable`, or `Unasked` with `need-undetermined` for `Unsure` or `Unasked`, naming the set's ref, with no `derivation` or `rows`; over an empty set it writes nothing, and its declared ref reads `{}`. A grounded rule or table that is itself a candidate set is nested grounding, which load refuses.

A grounded component reads `candidate`, the current candidate value, and `candidate.f`, a field of a record candidate, each declared as spelt in a function rule's `reads` or a table input's `read`; neither is a ref, and load refuses either in any other component, a `candidate.f` naming no field of the set's record type, and a candidate read in `boundaries`. It reads a declared ref grounded over its own set as the current candidate's entry, a field path allowed, or as the marker written at the declared ref; a component reading a grounded declared ref otherwise gets the `dict`, and load refuses its field path. A table reads a grounded declared ref only when grounded over the same set. A cause and a `derivation` name a `candidate` or `candidate.f` read as the set's ref, once.

The `settings` item holds `grounded`, from format 6, an object keyed by each grounded declared ref, a per-candidate question's or a grounded rule's or table's, each value its set's ref, `{}` when the design grounds nothing, and from format 7 `sets`, an object keyed by each candidate set's ref, each value its candidate field's name or null for a `list<string>` set; the replay record takes both from the design replay ran under. The reader refuses a `sets` key that is not a key of `providers`, and a value that is not null over a leaf or a field whose node in the set's tree is a leaf; from format 7 each `grounded` value is a key of `sets`. `redact` digests a candidate under its class: the policy's class at the set's ref, or for a set of records at its candidate field. A call item's `questions` holds one entry for a per-candidate question, under its declared ref. The reader refuses a `grounded` key that is not a key of `providers`, a value that is not a key of `providers` with no grounded bracket or field path, or, before format 7, one whose class tree is not a class name. It admits a grounded ref only as an entry's `ref`, in a cause's `refs`, and as a `calls` entry's `sums` key or `truncated` ref, each only when its declared ref is a key of `grounded`; it refuses a call item's `questions` key with a grounded bracket, a `value` entry at a key of `grounded`, and a marker there beside a grounded entry of the same declared ref. A lookup in the `settings` item's maps or a call item's `questions` takes a grounded ref as its declared ref.

In the redacted view, wherever the record holds a grounded ref, its bracketed string is replaced by the keyed digest of the candidate under the class of the set `grounded` names, `q:x["hmac-sha256:<hex>"]`, and passes unchanged under `open`. The digest is over the candidate string's canonical bytes, so where the set's entry is opened it equals the digest of the matching member. A grounded entry's value takes its declared ref's class tree in the applied policy.

Replay grounds the question again from the set's entry in the compared record. The fixture answers each grounded ref from the recorded entry that names the call item listing its source; a grounded ref with no such entry, as for a candidate the recorded turn did not ask, is stale, gets no key and is named by `stale-question` at its grounded ref.

## A structured wording

A question's wording may be `Structured(template, slots)`, its template a JSON object or array in the value domain. Each string value is a template under the same rendering and escape rules as a `Text`'s, from the one slot list: each placeholder names a slot, each slot is in some string value, and a literal brace is doubled. Keys and other values are literal and never rendered, so the shape is the design's. Some key or value holds a character that is not whitespace outside its placeholders. The document writes `{"template": ..., "slots": [...]}` as for a `Text`, the template's JSON type telling the two apart, with `order`, the template's order object, when the template is not in canonical order, so a template in canonical order keeps its identity; the question's identity hashes it as written. Each object is rendered and sent with its members in the order the template declares them, rebuilt from the document, so a design and its document ask alike.

A placeholder renders its slot's value by one closed rule: a string as itself; a list as its members' renderings in order, joined by `, `, so an empty list renders as nothing; any other value as its ordered encoding, each object in the order the value holds it. The same rule renders a `Text` wording. A question's identity does not cover a slot value's order; the design hash and the call's `prompt` do.

## The extraction kind

An extraction question declares, beside its kind and wording, no labels, a `value_type` (`text`, `number`, `date`, `time`, `datetime` or `duration`), `is_list`, `text`, a board ref naming the string its spans index, and `reference_date`, a board ref or null naming the string a date written without a year is read against. Both refs become needs of the source and are exactly of type `string`; the question's identity covers the four members, never the values they name.

The binding returns, for the question's ref, exactly an object of `value`, `guess`, `p_null`, `confidence` and `span`, null where it has nothing. `value` and `guess` are null or a value of the type, or when `is_list` a list of such values with no null; `p_null` and `confidence` are null or a number in [0, 1]; `span` is null, a pair `[start, end]` of code-point offsets into the text, end exclusive, or one pair per list item, and locates `value`, or `guess` when `value` is null. A `number` is held as a float. A `date` is `YYYY-MM-DD`; a `time` is `HH:MM:SS`, an optional fraction and an optional `Z` or `±HH:MM`; a `datetime` is a date, `T` and a time; a `duration` is ISO 8601's `P` form without weeks or fractions. These grammars are the engine's own, with ASCII digits and uppercase `T` and `Z` only. Any other return is `invalid-answer` on that ref alone. A model's none is a null `value`, never a marker; [The choice helpers](#the-choice-helpers) say when `extract` abstains.

The binding may also report the refs whose response was cut short as `truncated`, which the record keeps outside every hash.

## Decide

A design names its policy, `Design(policy="d:<name>", rules={...})`: the `d:` ref of a rule the design declares, a function rule or a table, which the document writes as the string `policy`. Load refuses a design whose policy is missing or not a string (check 10, and check 11 for a document that lacks the member); a policy ref that has a field path (check 7); one with no provider (check 3); and one that is not a `d:` ref or is a grounded declared ref (check 4). Interpret fires the policy as it fires any rule, and at a cut, by the round bound, the budget or the deadline, its drain fires every rule not yet fired, so the policy's ref holds an entry on every turn decide returns. The policy's failures are any rule's, its cause naming its rule. The `settings` item records its ref as `policy`, so a reader finds the decision without the design.

The policy's estimate is the turn's decision, and every other estimate only feeds it. Decide returns a `Decision`, which only decide builds: `record`, the closed record; `estimate`, read from the policy's entry in the record, the value as recorded, or an `Unsure`, `Unavailable` or `Unasked` rebuilt with its cause; and `design_hash`, the record's. It stores neither apart from the record.

A turn is undecided when its decision is a marker, of any kind. Its root cause is the marker's cause, or, while that cause's code is `required`, the cause of the marker at the entry its first ref names, a field path dropped and a grounded bracket kept, until a code is not `required`, the ref names no marker entry, or an entry comes round again. A caller routes an undecided turn by its root cause: a root `raised`, `invalid-return` or `undeclared-read` naming a rule or the codec is a defect, for whoever maintains the design or the codec; any other root cause hands the turn on. [The escalation view](#the-escalation-view) gives both causes and this routing as `defect`.

Two decisions are compared by their bytes: the canonical bytes of `{"value": v}` or `{"marker": m}` from the policy's entry, a marker's kind, cause code and refs included, and the entry's other members (`round`, `derivation`, `rows`, `order`) left out. The dead-question lint and question reach compare decisions so.

A codec that raises an `Exception`, a codec that returns an awaitable, which is closed, and a state that does not conform to the state declaration are a broken entry. Decide writes the `t:state` entry `Unavailable` with the entry cause, `raised` naming `codec` for a raise and `invalid-return` naming `t:state` otherwise, then the policy's entry `Unavailable` with the same cause, both in round 0 with `call` null; it runs no interpret. The log holds no `cause` item for it, and the codec's exception text is in `unhashed["exceptions"]`, keyed by the `t:state` entry's index. The reader admits a marker at `t:state` only from format 8, only `Unavailable` with `raised`, `invalid-return` or `design-changed`, and only followed by the policy's entry.

A component that raises, a rule, the policy among them, or a `when`, does not end the turn: its ref, or its source's refs, is `Unavailable` with `raised`, and its exception's type, message and traceback are in `unhashed["exceptions"]`, keyed by the log index of the item it wrote.

A turn `decide` or `decide_sync` returns whose record holds a defect, an entry [the defects view](#the-defects-view) lists, emits one `ommat.DefectWarning`, a `UserWarning`, once the record is closed; decide never warns mid-turn, and a turn that ends by an exception, a cancellation or the observer's included, emits none. Its `record` is the closed record and its `defects` the view's list; its message names each defect's ref, marker kind, cause code and refs, and the exception text `unhashed["exceptions"]` holds at its index, its traceback, or its type and message when the traceback is not representable. Under a filter that makes it an error, decide raises it instead of returning. Its location is the line that awaited `decide` or called `decide_sync`, where the caller's frame is the one that awaits. Replay, the evaluation jobs and the dead-question lint emit none.

A rule, the policy among them, and a `when` do not change a value they read. A read returns the board's own value, not a copy, so a change reaches every later reader in the turn, while the record keeps the value as it was written.

A `-0.0` in a value written to the board, an answer's probability or member included, is held as `0.0`, as the record writes it, so a component reads the same value live and in replay.

A marker a rule returns is written with its cause when each of the cause's refs is a ref the design provides, with or without a field path, a grounded ref whose entry the turn wrote, a source or rule name, a parameter path, or `codec`. Otherwise the rule's entry is `Unavailable` with `invalid-return` naming the rule, so a cause carries none of the turn's input.

Decide and replay refuse an async observer with `EntryRefusal`: an `async def` function or async generator function, a bound method or `functools.partial` of one, or an object whose class defines one as `__call__`. The test reads types and runs none of the observer's code. A sync observer that returns a coroutine is not caught, and what any observer returns is not read.

A cancellation of a running turn appends the `cause` item `cancelled`, closes the record, and propagates the `CancelledError` with the record as its `record`. To read `exc.record`, catch the `CancelledError` inside the task that awaits decide; a `CancelledError` that asyncio raises afresh carries no record. A cancellation requested from the observer takes effect at decide's next await. If the turn has none left, decide finishes, asyncio marks the finished task cancelled and raises a fresh `CancelledError`, and the decision is lost.

## Source requirements

A source may declare `requirement=SourceRequirement(ref, values)`: the source is asked only when `ref`, a `d:` ref with no field path, holds one of `values`. It is a `when` written as data, and it reads one estimate a rule wrote. Values are compared by their canonical bytes, so a number matches by its canonical form, `1` and `1.0` alike, and a list matches only a list in the same order.

The ref is an edge in the dependency graph, so the source waits for the rule that writes it, as it would for a need; it is never sent to the binding unless the source also declares it a need. A source may declare both a requirement and a function `when`; both must hold, and the requirement is tested first, after the needs. What the source's refs get follows what the ref holds. Its marker ranks with the needs' markers: an `Unavailable` need outranks an undetermined one, the cause names every need of the winning kind in code-point order, and a marker on any need wins over a requirement that does not hold:

| The ref holds | The source's refs |
|---|---|
| one of `values` | the source is asked |
| another value | `Unasked` with `when-false` naming the ref |
| `Unsure` or `Unasked` | `Unasked` with `need-undetermined` naming it among the undetermined needs |
| `Unavailable` | `Unavailable` with `need-unavailable` naming it among the unavailable needs |

A requirement cannot fail, so `when-failed` comes only from a function `when`, which reads only its source's needs and the state. A need, unlike a requirement, is sent to the binding: under the idia binding's `state=`, a label source that needs the `state=` need beside another need is `unsupported`. The design hash covers a requirement as data, its values in any order, with no `version`.

Load refuses a requirement whose ref is not a `d:` ref, `t:state` and a `q:` answer included, so a condition over an answer goes through a rule; a ref with a field path, so a record's field is projected by a rule of its own; a ref with no provider; no value, a value outside the value domain, a value the ref's type cannot hold, or a value given twice; and a cycle through it. A label-typed ref lets load refuse a misspelt value, which a plain `string` would let through to make the source `Unasked` on every turn.

## Targets

A design may declare `targets={id: Target(statement, values=None)}`, the properties of the state its questions and rules are about, keyed by target id. An id is any string, `tg:` then a name by convention, and is neither a ref nor a declared name; `statement` says what the design is trying to determine about the state, with what counts and what does not, so that a reader can judge how a question's answer bears on it; `values` describes its value space, or is null.

A `Question` may name `target`, the one target it asks about, and `latent_targets`, the targets it is known to bear on without asking about them, each `{"target": id, "role": role}`, the role a `TargetRole`: `reflective`, the answer alone is evidence for or against the target; `formative`, the answer supplies a part of it; or `unknown`, not yet judged. One answer may bear on one target in several ways, so a target may stand under several roles. A `Rule` may name `target`, the target it estimates.

Targets guide a reader and gate nothing: no turn or binding reads one. The design hash covers them, the latent targets as an unordered list, and a question's identity leaves out its `target` and `latent_targets`, so declaring targets keeps every question's identity. Load refuses a target id `targets` does not declare, naming the question's ref or the rule's id; a question's own `target` among its latent targets; one target listed twice under one role; and a role outside the three, a `target` or `statement` that is not a string, `values` that is neither a string nor null, and a latent target that is not exactly a target and a role. A missing link is not declared, not unrelated.

## Decision tables

A rule is a function or a `Table`, and the policy is a rule like any other, so a policy table is an ordinary table whose output is the decision. A table has no `version`: the design hash covers it as data. A design document holds a table as the members below. Its entry holds `rows`, the positions from 1 of every row that matched, in table order, and no `derivation`.

```python
Table(
    "d:urgent",
    'label{"no", "yes"}',
    "F",
    inputs=[Helper("pick_with_margin", "q:urgent", bar="urgent/bar", margin="urgent/margin")],
    rows=[["-", Input("q:urgent")]],
)
```

**Inputs.** Each input is a ref, a read of a `q:`, `t:` or `d:` ref, a field path allowed, or `Helper(name, read, **params)`, which calls `pick_with_margin`, `pick_with_thresholds`, `size`, `mass` or `extract` over its read, each other argument bound to a leaf path: `bar`, `margin`, `thresholds`, `labels` (a `list<string>` leaf) or `null_bar`. A pick's input is a label of its question, a `string` over a candidate-valued label set, `size`'s an integer of at least 0 over a list read, `mass`'s a `number [0, 1]`, and `extract`'s its question's value type, a list when it asks for one. A bare `q:` input holds an answer, so only `-`, `Known` and marker cells apply to it. A `noul` question takes `pick_with_margin`, or `mass` over `["yes"]` compared against a bar, never `pick_with_thresholds`. A data rule is a one-input table. A value cell stands over a label, boolean or integer input only, never over a `number` or `string` one, but for a pick over a candidate-valued label set, where it may name a fixed label, never a candidate.

**Rows and cells.** A row is a list: one cell per input, then the output entry; a `C#` row is its cells alone. A cell is written as a string in this notation, its form decided by how it begins: `-` matches anything, a marker or null included; `Known` matches any value, null included, and no marker; `Unsure`, `Unavailable` or `Unasked` matches only that marker, `Unsure or Unavailable` either, and `Undetermined` any marker; `>= p`, `> p`, `<= p`, `< p` compare a numeric input against a leaf path, an integer literal over an `integer` input, or another input by the ref it reads; `[p, q]` is a closed interval of leaves or integer literals; `{a, b}` matches any listed label; `in d:x` is membership in a list-valued input; any other text is a label. A `bool` or an `int` is a value cell, a marker class a marker cell, and `Undetermined` or a tuple of marker classes a cell listing those kinds. A cell may also be the document's tagged form, which load writes for every cell: `"-"`, `{"is": v}`, `{"one_of": [...]}`, `{"marker": k}`, `{"markers": [k, ...]}`, `{"known": true}`, `{">=": o}` and its kin, `{"between": [o, o]}` or `{"in": ref}`, each operand `{"param": p}`, `{"literal": n}` or `{"ref": r}`. A label the notation would read as a form, such as `-`, `Known` or `Unsure`, is written `{"is": "-"}`. Null is matched by `-` and `Known` alone, and a marker by `-` and the marker cells. An interval's lower bound may not exceed its upper, and a `{a, b}` cell may not repeat a member.

**Outputs.** An output entry is a value of the table's `outputs` type, the class `Unsure`, `Input(ref)`, a pass-through of an input, or `Param(path)`, a pass-through of a leaf. A row passing an input through matches only when that input holds a value. A value `outputs` does not allow, from a pass-through or an aggregate, is `Unavailable` with `invalid-return`.

**Hit policies.** `U`, rows that never overlap; `A`, rows that overlap only with the same output; `F`, the first matching row; `P`, the highest-ranked output among the matching rows, by the table's `order`, which lists every output value once, highest first, every output a value it lists; and `C+`, `C<`, `C>` and `C#`, the sum, minimum, maximum or count over the matching rows. Under `C+`, `C<` and `C>` the `outputs` type conforms to `number`, and under `C#` it is `integer`. Under `P` every output is a value `order` lists, never `Unsure` or a pass-through, and `order` is null under every other policy; under `C` no output is `Unsure`. The sum is exact over the decimals, an `int` when every output is one, and the sum and count are 0 when no row matches; the minimum and maximum with no match follow the marker rule below. Load decides overlap from the cells and the leaves' values: two comparisons of one input against one other input that split the line never overlap, and any other cell over two inputs is taken to overlap where both inputs hold values.

**Markers.** Every input is read first, in input order, and then every row is tested. When no row matches, the output is the inputs' marker of highest precedence, `Unavailable`, then `Unsure`, then `Unasked`, with `required` naming the first input holding it, or, when every input holds a value, `Unsure` with `abstained` naming the inputs once each in input order. Under `F` a row that failed only on inputs holding `Unavailable`, a pass-through's included, and a cell over two inputs when its other input holds a value, before the winning row, and under `P` such a row ranking above it, makes the output `Unavailable` with `required` naming that row's first such input; under `C` such a row anywhere does, while a row failing on a `Known` cell is left out of the aggregate. A row that failed on `Unsure` or `Unasked`, or on a marker cell or a `Known` cell, does not propagate, so a row `Unsure or Unavailable` placed before the rows that test an optional input's value takes its outage as a row of its own, and a row holding `Known` over an input gives way to the rows after it when that input holds a marker, which keeps its cause in its own entry. A row of `-` matches an `Unasked` input too, as when a source's `when` was false or a cut left it unasked: a table meant to stay `Unasked` then holds `Known` over that input in its last row, so the row gives way and, when no other row matches, the table writes the inputs' marker of highest precedence. A helper that raises makes the output `Unavailable` with `raised`, with no row matched.

**The grid.** A table's parameter comparisons and helper bindings are its boundaries: a pick's bindings under its read, `extract`'s `null_bar` and comparisons over its input under its read's `null` and `value` roles, a comparison over a `mass` input under its read's `mass` role, over the label names held by the leaf its `labels` argument is bound to, and a comparison over a bare `d:` or `t:` read under that read, an integer literal by its value. A cell over a `size` input adds nothing, so the lint probes a count table only over the lists `values` gives. An integer value cell over a bare numeric read is a boundary too. A function rule's `boundaries` field may not repeat a boundary a table already declares.

**A function rule's boundaries.** A table's comparisons are in its cells, but a function's are in its code, so a function rule declares them in `boundaries`, keyed by its reads, a `candidate` read never. A `d:` or `t:` read, a field path allowed, of a type that conforms to `number`, takes the list of `number` leaves it is compared against. A `q:` read of a `choice`, `score` or `noul` question takes all four roles, each a list, empty when unused: `bar` and `margin`, `number [0, 1]` leaves for `pick_with_margin`'s arguments; `thresholds`, `mapping<string, number [0, 1]>` leaves for `pick_with_thresholds`'s; and `mass`, each `{"labels", "bounds"}`, label names of the question and the `number [0, 1]` leaves their summed probability is compared against. An extraction read takes `null`, `confidence` and `value` instead, `value` only over a scalar `number` question. Wherever a role takes a `number` leaf it also takes a key path, `leaf.key`, naming one key of a `mapping<string, T>` leaf, `T` numeric, whose value holds the key: it stands for that key's value, of type `T`. A leaf path holds no `.`, so the first `.` ends it, and the key is the rest. Load refuses a key path whose leaf is not declared, is no such mapping or lacks the key, under check 8. A rule declaring the mapping leaf in `params` reaches a key, for the switching values and the grid, only by the comparisons it declares for the leaf whole or for that key's path; a key it names neither way it reaches unseen. The field changes nothing the rule computes: the lint, the grid, the switching values and the outline read it.

```python
Rule(
    "d:refund",
    'label{"no", "yes"}',
    refund,
    version="1",
    reads=["q:reason", "t:order_age"],
    params=["bars/reason_at", "bars/reason_lead", "bars/fault_mass", "bars/window_days"],
    boundaries={
        "q:reason": {
            "bar": ["bars/reason_at"],
            "thresholds": [],
            "margin": ["bars/reason_lead"],
            "mass": [{"labels": ["damaged", "late"], "bounds": ["bars/fault_mass"]}],
        },
        "t:order_age": ["bars/window_days"],
    },
)
```

## The escalation view

`escalation(record)` takes any closed `Record` and returns `None` when the policy gave a value, when the log holds no entry at the policy's ref, and for a record before format 8. Otherwise it returns a plain dict of the undecided turn's members, in this order: `kind`, the policy's marker's kind; `cause`, its cause, `{"code", "refs"}`; `root_cause`, the cause its `required` chain ends at (see [Decide](#decide)); `defect`, a `bool`, true when the root cause's code is `raised`, `invalid-return` or `undeclared-read` and its first ref names a rule, a `d:` ref the `settings` item's `providers` gives, or the codec, `codec` or `t:state`, and false otherwise, as for an oracle's failure naming `q:` or `t:` refs, a source's `when`, or any other code; and `record_hash`. It raises `ValueError` for a `record` that is not exactly a `Record`. The view performs nothing: what the caller does with an undecided turn is its own. Both views read the policy's entry wherever the record holds one and do not test that the turn finished, so a record cancelled after the policy fired still shows its decision.

## The defects view

`defects(record)` takes any closed `Record` and returns `None` for a record before format 8. Otherwise it returns a list of the entry items, each as `Record.entries` gives it and in log order, whose marker's cause passes the escalation view's `defect` test, whatever the policy gave, so a rule that raised on a decided turn is listed. A `required` cause never passes, so a rule's defect is listed at its own entry and not at those that required it; at a broken entry the state's entry and the policy's are both listed. It raises `ValueError` for a `record` that is not exactly a `Record`.

## The redacted view

`redact(record, root_key, policy)` takes any closed `Record`, a 32-byte root key and the caller's redaction policy, all three required, and returns the redacted view's document as canonical bytes: `{"log", "unhashed", "redacted", "applied"}`, and from format 3 `hash`, the record hash, open. `redacted` is the record hash; `applied` is the policy applied, every ref of the record's trees mapped to its class tree in full. Each `content` or `identifier` value is replaced by its keyed digest, `hmac-sha256:` and the hex of HMAC-SHA-256 under the per-turn key over the value's canonical bytes; an `open` value passes. A list or mapping whose tree holds no `open` and no record is one digest, so its length and order are hidden; a record is opened field by field; a mapping's keys are always digested; a null passes unless it lies within a value digested whole.

The policy maps a ref of the record, `t:state` or a key of `providers`, or such a ref with a field path into its shape, to a class name or an object naming fields of the record there, each holding a class name or such an object. A class name covers every value beneath it; a mapping layer has no node of its own, or takes a one-member list, as `applied` writes it, so `applied` is itself a policy. Keys apply from the shortest field path to the longest, so a longer key overrides beneath a shorter one. A candidate set's ref, a `list<string>` set's, takes a class name, and a set of records takes one at its candidate field. Every value the policy names nothing for is `content`, a number, a label, an answer of any kind and a probability among them. In a record before format 9 the `settings` item's class trees are a floor: the class of a value is the more restrictive of the policy's, `content` where it names nothing, and the stored one, by `open` < `identifier` < `content`, so a policy never opens what the stored tree digests, and `{}` digests all data. A stored tree that is malformed counts as `content`. `identifier` and `content` digest alike, over the value's canonical bytes, and differ only in `applied` and so in the key derived from it. It raises `ValueError` for a policy that is not a mapping, a key that is not a string or not a ref, holds a grounded bracket, names no ref of the record or a field its shape lacks, two keys of one string data, a value that is not a class name or such an object, an object where the shape is no record, a list where the shape has no mapping layer or that is not a one-member list, and anything but a class name at a `list<string>` set's ref; it raises `TypeError` when `policy` is omitted. The first error found is reported in this order: a `record` that is not exactly a `Record`, a root key that is not 32 `bytes`, then the policy's keys in its iteration order, each with whether a candidate set's key holds a class name, then its values from the shortest key to the longest.

A candidate takes its set's class everywhere it is copied: a grounded ref's bracket, each label and `probabilities` key of an opened candidate-valued answer that is not a fixed name, and a set-tagged literal, which takes the set's whole tree. Structure is open whatever the policy names: refs, a grounded ref's candidate apart, kinds, cause codes, tool names, origin tags, positions, `usage`, the hashes, the codec's name, an exception's `type`, the call item's members but `prompt`, the provenance details, label descriptions, and the `design` and `settings` items. Text the engine records with no type is digested whatever the policy names: a call's `prompt`, a literal argument no set tags, an exception's `message` and `traceback`, each `reads` value, `unexpected`, `warnings`, `request_id`, `extras`, whole, each `invalid` text, an `unhashed` member the engine does not know, whole, and a `calls` entry member it does not know, whole. `usage` stays open, so a format-8 or format-9 record's `passes` shows. A plan's `reason`, `reply` and context text exist only in records before format 8, and are digested whole; so is a requirement's value there when its ref carries a field path or is outside the grammar, while for a record ref the value follows the applied tree at that ref.

The per-turn key is HKDF-Expand (RFC 5869) under the root key, one 32-byte block, its info the record hash and then the policy hash, each `sha256:` and 64 hexadecimal digits as ASCII; the policy hash is SHA-256 over the canonical bytes of `applied`. Two views of one record under policies that apply differently share no digest, and a verifier given the root key recomputes the key from the view alone and any disclosed value's digest. `read_record` refuses the view, which holds members other than `log`, `unhashed` and `hash`, so replay never takes one. It raises `ValueError` before reading anything for a `record` that is not exactly a `Record`, then for a root key that is not an instance of `bytes` of length 32, before the policy is read.

## The transcript view

`transcript` is deprecated: it emits a `DeprecationWarning`, and leaves `ommat` in a later minor release. A caller handing a turn to a model renders the record with [the conversation renderers](#the-conversation-renderers); one that needs every value reads the record. `transcript(record)` takes any closed `Record` and returns `None` for a record before format 8, and for one whose log holds no entry at the policy's ref, as for a turn cancelled before the policy fired. Otherwise it returns a plain dict of these members, in this order:

- `record_hash`;
- `design`, `{"hash"}`: the `design` item's hash;
- `rounds`: for each round with a call item, in log order, a list of calls, one per source of each call item, in the item's source order. A call is `{"id", "source", "asks", "outcome", "answers"}`: `asks` the source's refs whose entry names the call item, in code-point order; `outcome` as recorded; `answers` an object keyed by those refs, each entry `{"value": v}` as recorded or `{"marker": {"kind", "cause"}}`;
- `decide`, `{"id", "board", "decision"}`: `board` every `d:` entry but the policy's, keyed by ref in log order and tagged as an answer is; `decision` the policy's entry, tagged alike.

An id is `om_`, the record hash's first 24 hexadecimal digits, then `_c<n>` for the view's nth call or `_d` for decide, so it fits `[A-Za-z0-9_-]` and 40 characters, and one log always gives the same ids, so a conversation that holds one record twice holds its ids twice unless the renderers' `turn` sets them apart. A ref no call asked, such as one whose source's needs were markers, is in no round; its effect is on the board. The view holds no `t:state` entry, no provenance, prompt or question wording, and no `derivation`, `rows` or `slots`. Its values are the record's, content included: to share a turn, share its redacted view, from which no transcript is built. It raises `ValueError` for a `record` that is not exactly a `Record`.

## The conversation renderers

`ommat.conversation` is outside the compatibility promise and may change in any minor release. It takes nothing beyond the standard library. `ommat.conversation.pydantic_ai` takes Pydantic AI, which the `pydantic-ai` extra installs as `pydantic-ai-slim>=2.49`. `import ommat` imports neither. A renderer words nothing beyond fixed labels.

`to_chat_completions(record, design=None, *, turn=None)` returns a closed record's turn history as Chat Completions messages, or `[]` for a record before format 8 and for one whose log holds no entry at the policy's ref. For each round with a call item, in log order, an assistant message with null `content` and one tool call per source of each call item, in the item's source order, `{"id", "type": "function", "function": {"name", "arguments"}}`, then one `{"role": "tool", "tool_call_id", "content"}` message per call; then an assistant message calling `decide` and its `tool` message. A source call's arguments are `{}`, and its result, failed or not, an object keyed by the source's refs whose entry names the call item, in code-point order. Decide's arguments are the board, each `d:` entry but the policy's, keyed by ref in log order; given `design`, the record's `LoadedDesign`, only the entries whose declared ref the policy reads, field paths dropped, and every entry holding a marker. Decide's result is `{"decision": v}`, the policy's value, or for an undecided turn `{"undecided": {"kind", "root_cause", "refs"}}`, the policy's marker's kind and the escalation view's root cause code and refs. Every arguments and content string is in the ordered encoding, each object in the order given above, else in the order the record holds it, so a record renders to the same bytes each time. The caller places the messages after its own user message for the turn.

Each value is minimal. A `noul`, `choice` or `score` answer is `[label, probability]`: its top label, the first in declared order at a tie, and that label's probability as recorded. An extraction answer is `[value, confidence]`. A `t:` or `d:` value is as recorded. Every value is the record's, content included. A marker is `{"marker": kind, "cause": code}`. The history holds no `t:state` entry, no provenance, prompt or question wording, and no `derivation`, `rows` or `slots`; to share a turn, share its redacted view. An id is `om_`, the record hash's first 24 hexadecimal digits, then `_c<n>` for the record's nth source call, counted over the whole record, or `_d` for decide, so it fits `[A-Za-z0-9_-]` and 40 characters. A ref no call asked, such as one whose source's needs were markers, is in no round. Both renderers raise `ValueError` for a `record` that is not exactly a `Record`, a `design` that is neither `None` nor exactly a `LoadedDesign`, and a design whose hash is not the record's.

`to_model_messages(record, design=None, *, model_name=None, provider_name=None, turn=None)` returns the same calls and results as Pydantic AI messages, or `[]` where `to_chat_completions` gives none: for each round a `ModelResponse` of `ToolCallPart`s, then a `ModelRequest` of `ToolReturnPart`s, a failed call's with `outcome="failed"`, which Pydantic AI sends as the provider's error flag, as Anthropic's and Bedrock's, or, for a provider with none, by wrapping the content as `{"error": ...}`; decide alike, its return's `metadata` `{"record_hash", "design_hash"}`. Arguments and content are dicts. Every `ModelResponse` carries `model_name` and `provider_name`; timestamps are Pydantic AI's. Every message, both the responses and the requests, carries `metadata` `{"ommat": "bookkeeping", "record_hash"}`, which Pydantic AI keeps and never sends to a model.

ommat's bookkeeping is the source calls and decide, with their results. `is_bookkeeping(message)` tells one, a Pydantic AI message by its mark and a Chat Completions message by its ids: an assistant message with null or empty `content` whose every tool call has one, or a `tool` message answering one; a Pydantic AI request that holds anything but tool returns is not bookkeeping. `drop_earlier_bookkeeping(messages)` returns the messages without each bookkeeping call message of a record other than the latest, the record of the last such message, and without each result answering one of their calls, wherever that result stands; no result leaves on its own mark. So the current turn's bookkeeping stays for the model to answer from, an earlier turn's leaves with each result beside its call, and a request that Pydantic AI merged loses only the results it holds for those calls. Each module has its own pair; the Pydantic AI one runs as a history processor, `Agent(capabilities=[ProcessHistory(drop_earlier_bookkeeping)])`, which also writes the processed history back into the run's messages.

Both renderers take an optional `turn`, exactly a `str` or an `int` in the value domain, an `int` of magnitude at most 2^53 - 1 and a `str` with no surrogate, else `ValueError`, checked before the record is read. With one, every rendered id's stem, `om_` and 24 hexadecimal digits, is instead the first 24 of SHA-256 over the canonical JSON of `[record hash, turn]`; its suffix stays, so an id keeps its form and bound. The same design, state and answers give the same record, so one conversation holds a record once unless each rendering takes its own `turn`, as an exact retry must, since a provider may refuse a repeated tool-call id. The Pydantic AI mark then holds `turn`, and each filter's record is the record and the turn. With `None`, the ids are the record's.

A source's tool name is its name with each `/` as `-`, which no ommat name holds. Both renderers raise `ValueError` for a source whose tool name is `decide` or is over 64 characters.

## The authoring reader

`ommat.authoring` is outside the compatibility promise and may change in any minor release, as `ommat.lens` is: its public names, `design_from`, `slice_from`, `Slice` and `AuthoringError`, carry no stability promise before 1.0. It takes nothing beyond the standard library, reads no file, and `import ommat` does not import it. The code and tests are the spec for each convention; this section says what the reader is.

`design_from(doc, version=None, values=None, types=None)` reads a parsed mapping, such as a TOML document read with `tomllib` or a JSON one, as the `Design` its author would write in Python; `slice_from` reads the same document as a `Slice`, the sources, rules and parameters a larger design merges. It runs before `load` and changes no hash: a design read from a document has the hash of its Python twin. It refuses only what it cannot build, with `AuthoringError`, a `ValueError`, whose message names the path of the slip, such as `rules.op: unknown key 'read'`; the design checks, and a shape such as a `reads` that is not a list, are `load`'s to refuse.

The reader imports each rule's `fn`, which runs that module's top-level code, so a document is as trusted as source code.

The conventions a document adds expand away before `load`:

- Every table holds only the keys its kind allows, and the reader refuses any other. A question is one key naming its kind, holding its wording; `slot` fills `{name}` in the wording per candidate of its source's `grounded` rule, or names another rule.
- A decision table is a rule with `rows`, each row naming only the inputs it tests and its result in `then`; `like` copies another table's rows and changes only the `provides`, `type`, `target` or inputs it names.
- `[labels]` names label sets a question's `labels` may use, and `[types]` names type strings that `$name` expands, an entry written `{ required = true }` left to the caller's `types`.
- A `version`, a parameter's `value` and a `[types]` string each have one owner, the document or the caller, and a value given by both is refused.

## The record lens

`ommat.lens` is outside the compatibility promise and may change in any minor release. It takes nothing beyond the standard library, `import ommat` does not import it, and the page it writes loads nothing from the network, which its content-security policy also forbids. The page holds each record's outline and text form whole, user text included, so keep it as you keep the records, or pass a redacted view.

`page(records, designs=(), *, skipped=())` returns one self-contained HTML page as a `str`. `records` are record documents as `bytes`, such as `decision.record.document`, a sequence named `record 1`, `record 2` and so on, or a mapping from a name to the bytes; `designs` are design documents as `bytes`, such as `loaded.document`, a sequence named `design 1` and so on or a mapping alike, a design given twice kept once under its first name; `skipped` names files the caller passed over, which the page counts. A record is any JSON object holding a `log` list, so a redacted view or a hand-edited record shows too. A design pairs by the SHA-256 of its exact bytes, so pass `loaded.document` as it is. It raises `ValueError` for an argument of the wrong type, a document that is not one JSON object in strict UTF-8 with no byte-order mark, no repeated key, only finite numbers, no integer past 2^53 - 1 that names no double exactly, and nesting at most 128 deep, a record without a `log` list, or a design that is not a JSON object.

## The outline

`ommat.outline` prints one stored design or turn as a plain dict of fixed shape and as text a person or a program reads line by line. It is outside the compatibility promise and may change in any minor release. It takes nothing beyond the standard library, and `import ommat` does not import it. The text and the JSON hold the records' values, user text included, so keep them as you keep the records.

It has four functions. A document is `bytes`, such as `loaded.document` or `decision.record.document`, or a parsed object; bytes are read as the lens reads them, else `ValueError`.

- `design_outline(document)` gives `outline_version` 8, `kind` `"design"`, the name, format and policy, `hash` with `hash_source`, `"bytes"` for bytes and `"recomputed"` over the canonical encoding for a parsed object, the state's type, the parameters sorted by path, the named types, the nodes, the edges, `ref_node`, each provided ref's node, the groups, the targets, each with `questions`, `estimated_by`, `readers` and `path`, and `requirements`. `other_members` names the members the document holds that the outline does not read, so a design document of format 10 or earlier lists its `classes` there. Every type text is parsed into `{text, kind, labels, range, of, fields}`.
- Each node holds every key: `id` (`state`, `source:<name>` or `rule:<name>`), `kind` (`state`, `source` or `rule`), `policy`, true for the rule whose estimate is the decision, which `policy_node` names, `name`, `refs`, `round`, `wavefront`, `predecessors`, its predecessors in the dependency graph, `fan_in` and `fan_out`, and `steps_to_policy`, the fewest dependency edges from the node to `policy_node`: 0 for the policy, `null` for the state, for a node with no such path, and for every node when there is no policy node. `round` is the round interpret runs the node in, a function of the graph alone: 0 for the state, a rule's latest predecessor's round (0 with none), and one more than a source's latest predecessor's round (1 with none). `wavefront` is 0 for the state and for a source, and for a rule one more than the largest `wavefront` among its predecessors in its own round, so a rule whose predecessors are all sources or of earlier rounds is 1: the wavefront the turn's sorter hands the node in when every node of its round runs. A source adds its oracle, needs, requirement, cost, timeout, feedback, grounding, payload, `when`, its questions, each with its kind, labels or candidate-valued label set, extraction members, targets and wording, and its tool results. A rule adds its form, `provides`, `outputs`, `reads` and `params`, each tagged with where it comes from, `boundaries`, `candidates` and `target`; a function its module, qualname, version and source hash, with `body` `"not in the document"`; a table its hit policy, order, inputs and rows, each cell beside its notation and each row's `tests`, `{input, read, node, cell}` for each input whose cell is not `-` and whose read is a ref and no candidate read, `node` the node providing the read's base.
- `edges` are `{from, to, role, ref, dependency}`, sorted. `dependency` true is an edge of the loaded design's graph, by the roles need, slot, label-set, grounding, text, read, helper and source-requirement; `dependency` false is a use of `t:state`, from `state`, which that graph leaves out.
- `requirements` lists each source with a `requirement`, by `round` and then id, as `{source, provider, decided_from, behind}`: `provider` the node that provides the requirement's ref, `null` when none does, `decided_from` that node and every node it depends on, and `behind` every node that depends on the source, each in `nodes` order.
- Each target adds `questions`, each `{ref, node, role}`, `role` `asks` for a question's `target` or else its latent role, by node, then question, a question's `asks` before its latent roles; `estimated_by`, the rules whose `target` it is; `readers`, the other rules with a dependency edge from a ref in `questions`, each in `nodes` order; and `path`, from the node of `estimated_by` with the least `steps_to_policy`, or, when none has one, the node of `readers` with the least, ties by id in code-point order, the node ids to the policy, each next node the successor one step nearer, the least id on a tie; `null` when neither has a node with a `steps_to_policy`.
a table its hit policy, order, inputs and rows, each cell beside its notation and each row's `tests`, `{input, read, node, cell}` for each input whose cell is not `-` and whose read is a ref and no candidate read, `node` the node providing the read's base.: one column per `round` and `wavefront` the nodes hold, in that order, each `{round, wavefront, kinds, nodes}`, `kinds` `["state"]`, `["source"]` or `["rule"]` and `nodes` its node ids in row order, the state alone in column 0; and each node's `{col, row, round, wavefront}`. Each column starts in the nodes' order; then three sweeps, each forward and then backward, sort each column by the mean row of each node's predecessors (forward) or successors (backward) over the dependency edges, a node with none keeping its row, ties by the row each holds and then by id.
- `fingerprint` is `{"hash", "roles"}`, the design's shape with every name erased. Each node's role is its kind, whether it is the policy, its form, its question kinds with their counts, its tool-result count, and whether it holds boundaries, a target and a requirement; three Weisfeiler-Lehman iterations over the dependency edges refine it, each message an edge's direction, role and the colour across it. `hash` is the SHA-256 of the ordered encoding of the sorted final colours, and `roles` the sorted roles. Two designs that differ by their names alone share it.
- `short` maps each ref the design provides, `t:state` and each `t:state` path it uses to a label: the ref's last path segment, after the last `/` or `.`. Refs that share a label each take the segment before, until every label is unique; a ref of one segment gives its whole ref.
- `turn_outline(record_document, design_document=None)` gives `outline_version` 8, `kind` `"turn"`, `read_by`, `"read_record"`, or `"raw"` for a record `read_record` refuses, with `read_error`, the record and design hashes, record format, helper version and engine, the turn's times, `status`, `finished`, or `unfinished` with its reason, as for a turn cancelled before the policy fired, the settings, policy, providers, grounding and sets, `paired`, `design_name` and `design_hash_matches`, the state, the rounds, the `cause`, the `plan` and `verdict` items of a record before format 8, the decision, the entry at the policy's ref, `escalation`, the view's value, `defects`, the refs the view lists, and `absent`, the refs the design provides that no entry wrote. Each round holds its calls, each with its provenance and its kept `unhashed` members, and its entries, each with `holds`, `value` or its marker's kind, `Unsure`, `Unavailable` or `Unasked`, its value or marker, derivation and rows. The decision and the state hold `holds` alike. A record before format 8 gives its entries and calls, with no decision, escalation or defects.
- Paired, each entry adds what its component declares: a rule's entry its `declared_reads`, `declared_params` and `target`; a question's entry its `declared_inputs`, each ref once with its roles, `wording`, `target` and `latent_targets`; a tool result's entry its `declared_inputs`. Every paired entry has `bounds`, each `{role, param, bound, by, key, labels, measure, measured, distance, side, per_label}`, `param` a leaf or key path and `bound` its value, null where the document holds none, from the rules' `boundaries` fields, a table's helper bindings and a table's comparisons of a bare input against a leaf; `bounds_reason`, when it has none, naming the function rules that read it with no `boundaries` field and the tables that bind no leaf to it; and `nearest`, the bound nearest to its measure, the first in bound order on a tie.
- `lineage` lists the entries the decision rests on, from the policy's entry back through their derivation records, depth first, each ref once: each `{ref, holds, via, from, index}`, `via` how it was found from the entry at `from`, both `null` for the policy's entry. From an entry with a derivation it follows each derivation's `rested_on` refs (`via` `"rested_on"`), or its `refs` where `rested_on` is empty (`"refs"`); from a table's entry, paired, the reads of the inputs whose cell in a hit row is not `-` (`"row"`), and from a marker, then, its cause refs (`"cause"`). The walk first follows these alone. It then follows, from each entry that gave no such hop, the reads its paired design declares for the rule (`"declared"`), and from each entry those reach what its record names, each ref still once, so a ref some hop of the record names keeps that hop. An unpaired turn holds no declared read. A ref no entry holds closes its branch, `holds` and `index` `null`. It is empty when no entry is at the policy's ref. Its first branch, each entry found from the one before, ends at the root, before any `declared` hop, so a declared read is never the root.
- `nearest_bounds`, paired, lists every bound of every entry as `{ref, param, role, by, distance, side, on_lineage}`, a threshold's distance and side its nearest label's, sorted by absolute distance, a bound with none last, then by ref and param; `on_lineage` says the entry is on the lineage by hops the record names alone: the policy's entry, and each entry reached from one of them by a hop that is not `declared`. Unpaired, it is `null`.
- `ties`, paired with a bound that has a distance, is the number of `nearest_bounds` whose absolute distance equals the first's, 1 when none does; otherwise `null`.
- `summary` is one line of eight columns, two spaces apart: the record hash's first 12 hex digits, the status, `decided` and the value's ordered encoding or `undecided`, the marker's kind, code and refs, or `no-decision`, `root` and the root's ref, `defect` `yes`, `no` or `-`, `rounds` and the count of rounds after round 0, `nearest` and the distance, side and `param@ref` of the first nearest bound of an entry on the lineage's first branch (the entries `why` names), else of an entry on the lineage, else of the first, or `-`, and `design` and the design hash's first 12 hex digits. A run of two spaces in the value is written ` \u0020`, so the line splits on two spaces.
- `summary_nearest` is the bound `summary` names, as `nearest_bounds` gives it, or `null`; `why` is the text form's `why` line without its label.
- `review`, paired, sorts what the turn did; unpaired it is `null`. `sources` lists each source the design declares, those asked in call order, then those not asked in entry order, then the rest in design order, each `{source, refs, requirement, when, outcome, call, round, entries, marker, cause, why}`: `outcome` `asked` when a call names it, `not asked` when it wrote entries and no call, with the first entry's marker and cause, or `no entry`; `why` is `requirement` when the cause names its requirement's ref, `when` when a function `when`'s code names the source, `need` for another need, `stopped` for `round-bound`, `deadline`, `settled`, `over-budget` or `cancelled`, else `other`. `tables` lists each table entry, in entry order, as `{ref, rule, index, hit, policy, rows}`, every row of its table as `{n, output, result, chosen, tests}`: `result` `matched` when the entry's `rows` name it, `untested` when a read it tests holds `Unavailable` or no entry, else `not matched`; `chosen` marks the matched row whose output the entry holds, the first under a single-hit policy, the highest-ranked under `P`, none under a collect policy or when a row that could not be tested made the value `Unavailable`; each test is `{input, read, helper, cell, entry, holds}`, the entry its read found. The record keeps which rows matched and no cell's result, so `review` says nothing of why a row failed.
- `render_design(outline)` and `render_turn(outline, *, detail=False)` return the text as a `str`. The turn's text ends with a `sources` block and one `table` block per table entry, from `review`.

A record pairs with the design whose bytes' SHA-256 its `design` item holds. A member the documents give nothing for, or that does not apply to the node or entry, is `null`, never guessed: unpaired, every design-derived member of every entry and `absent` is `null`. The record holds no read set, so every read beside an entry is the design's declaration.

A distance is the measure minus the bound in the choice helpers' exact decimal arithmetic, so the side agrees with the helper's own comparison: `short` below 0 and `clear` at or above it, a lead equal to its margin `clear`. A `null` bound, `extract`'s `null_bar`, is `short` at or above 0, as `extract` abstains there; a plain bound has no side, its direction being the rule's.

The text is the same bytes for the same outline. One node, call or entry is one line, with detail lines indented two more spaces and no colour or wrapping. Nodes are in topological order: the state first, then by `round`, then `wavefront`, a round's sources before its rules, each in code-point order of the bare names, the policy last when nothing reads it. A node's line is tagged `[state]`, `[source]` or `[rule]`, the policy's rule line ending ` [policy]`, and names its `predecessors` and, for a source or rule other than the policy when the design has a policy node, `to policy n` or `no path to the policy`. A `requirements` block gives each requirement's source, ref, values and provider, then the nodes it is decided from and behind it; and each target line is followed by its questions, `asked by` or the latent role, its estimating rules, its readers and its path. Declared reads, params and inputs are labelled declared. Probabilities show to two decimals; a bound's measure and distance show unrounded, as the ordered encoding writes them. Prompts, warnings and other long values are cut at 120 characters with `…`, and the state is printed whole. A call's warnings fold into one line per code, with its count and its first message. An extraction prints its value always, its guess only where it differs from the value, `p_null` and its confidence when not null, and its span only when not null. Each bound shows what it measures, its distance and `short` or `clear`; a paired answer with no bound says why.

A turn's second line, `why`, says `decided`, the value and the policy's ref, or `undecided:` and each entry of the lineage's first branch with its marker's kind and code or its top label and probability, `<-` between them, the root's nearest bound, `short` or `clear` and its absolute distance, with `(tied with n-1 others)` after it when `ties` exceeds 1 (`(tied with 1 other)` when it is 2) and the root's bound lies as near as the first, and the root and `defect`. A root whose entry is paired and holds no bound says `no bound declared` and its `bounds_reason` in parentheses after its value, and an unpaired turn's root says `(no design paired)` there. A paired turn says whether the design's hash matches. The `decision` line is followed by `lineage`, one entry per line, each after the first with how it was found and, where that is not the line before, from which entry; and, paired, by `nearest bounds`, led when `ties` is above 1 by `(n bounds tie at d, side)`, the first item's signed distance and its side, then the first eight, each starred when its entry is on the lineage, or `none`. An entry on the lineage is starred after its ref in its round. A call's prompt, request id, clients, missing members and warnings fold into one `call detail` line, the prompt's length in characters, the count of missing members and of warnings and each warning code with its count; `detail=True` prints each on its own line.

`python -m ommat.outline PATH... [--json | --oneline] [--detail] [-o FILE]` prints the text, with `--detail` each call's detail members a line each, or with `--json` one JSON object per document, `{"path", "outline"}`, or with `--oneline` each record's `summary` and nothing for a design, to stdout or to `FILE`, for each record and design file and each `.json` and `.jsonl` file under a folder: the designs, then the records, each record beside the design among them it pairs with. It reads, classifies and skips files as the lens's command does. With a missing path, an output that is one of the files it reads, an output it cannot write, or neither a record nor a design, it exits 1.

## The choice helpers

`pick_with_margin(r, ref, bar, margin)` returns the top label of the answer at `ref` when its probability is at or above `bar` and leads the next by at least `margin`, else `Unsure` with `abstained` naming `ref`; `pick_with_thresholds(r, ref, thresholds, margin)` takes a bar per label. A tie at the top abstains in either pick, even at `margin` 0. A marker read is returned unchanged.

`size(r, ref)` returns the number of members of the list at `ref`, a list-valued entry or field or a candidate set, as an `int`. `mass(r, ref, labels)` returns the sum of the probabilities of the answer at `ref` over `labels`, exact and then rounded to the nearest `float`, 1.0 when the exact sum exceeds 1 and 0.0 over no label. Either returns a marker read unchanged.

`top(r, ref, *, tie="abstain")` returns the top label of the answer at `ref` and its probability as a `(label, float)` tuple, the probability exact and then rounded to the nearest `float`, as `mass` gives it. When the top ties the next highest label exactly, 0 for a one-label answer, `tie="abstain"` gives `Unsure` with `abstained` naming `ref`, as `pick_with_margin` at `bar` 0 and `margin` 0 does, and `tie="first"` gives the tied label first in the answer's `labels`: for a `noul` that is `yes`, for a `score` the lower level. `tie` is keyword-only. A marker read is returned unchanged. Only a function rule calls it.

`pick_each_with_margin(r, ref, bar, margin)` and `pick_each_with_thresholds(r, ref, thresholds, margin)` read a per-candidate question's declared ref and return a new `dict` in the set's order from each candidate to its pick, to `Unsure` with `abstained` naming that candidate's grounded ref, or to its entry's marker unchanged; the set's need marker comes back unchanged and an empty set gives `{}`. Only a function rule calls them. A rule weighs the `dict` and returns its own value; returned whole, a `dict` holding a marker fits no type and is `invalid-return`. Passing one candidate's marker on is a valid return. A rule grounded over the same set reads one candidate's answer and uses the one-ref picks.

`determined(r, *refs)` returns a `tuple` of its reads' values in argument order, for one ref as for several, or, when any read is a marker, the marker of highest precedence, `Unavailable`, then `Unsure`, then `Unasked`, the first of its kind in argument order, unchanged. A null and a per-candidate read's `dict` are values. It stops nothing: a rule passes the marker on by returning it.

The helper version, which each record's `design` item carries as `helpers`, is 4. A record of another version replays with a `helpers-mismatch` finding beside any other, and runs under the loaded helpers.

`extract(r, ref, null_bar)` returns `Unsure` with `abstained` naming `ref` when the extraction answer at `ref` has a `None` `value`, or a `p_null` at or above `null_bar`; otherwise it returns the `value`, as a list when the question asks for one. A `None` `p_null` never abstains, so `null_bar` bites only when the model gives a `p_null`. The result depends on `value` and `p_null` alone, never on `guess` or `confidence`: a rule that weighs confidence reads the answer whole. A marker read is returned unchanged.

Each of these helpers raises, rather than return a marker, on a slip in its own call, in one order. On a function `when`'s view it raises before any read. It then reads its refs and tests each argument: a `bar`, `margin` or `null_bar` that is not exactly an `int` or `float` is a `TypeError`, and one outside [0, 1], NaN and the infinities among them, a `ValueError`; a `thresholds` that is not exactly a `dict`, or holds a key that is not exactly a `str`, is a `TypeError`, each of its values tested as a `bar`; a `labels` that is not exactly a `list` or `tuple`, or holds a member that is not exactly a `str`, is a `TypeError`, and one that repeats a label a `ValueError`; a `tie` other than exactly `"abstain"` or `"first"` is a `ValueError`; and `determined` given no ref, or a ref twice, is a `ValueError`. So a bad argument raises even when the read is a marker. A marker read is then returned unchanged, and a read of any other value the helper does not take is a `TypeError`: one holding no answer for a pick, `top` or `mass`, no extraction answer for `extract`, no list for `size`, and for a per-candidate pick one that is not a per-candidate question's declared ref or holds a candidate entry that is neither an answer nor a marker. Last, a label the answer lacks, a key of `thresholds` or a member of `labels`, is a `ValueError`, tested for a per-candidate pick on every answered candidate before any is picked.

## The dead-question lint

`dead_questions(loaded, *, values=None)` fills `t:state` and every ref a source provides, and runs decide at each point, with the default run settings, no codec and one binding answering each asked ref from the point; two runs differ when their decisions' bytes differ (see [Decide](#decide)). A run that ends at a broken entry, its `t:state` entry a marker, is a `ValueError` naming the point and the cause, since that turn ran no rule; a policy that returns any marker of its own is compared like any other run. `values` maps a read the lint fills to a list of that read's values, for a read its types and the answer grid cannot fill, or to narrow a label question as below. It never takes a `d:` key, since rules compute those values; a key naming a read the lint does not fill is a `ValueError` before any run. A label question with no grid entry, other than a candidate-valued one, also takes a key: its list is the labels to try, each at 1 in declared order with the marker last, in place of every label; the report's `narrowed` lists such questions in code-point order, so it still says what was searched. A question the grid fills takes no key, since its points carry the grid's claim.

The values the lint ranges a read over are its read points. `ommat.helpers.points(loaded, ref, *, values=None)` gives one read's, in their order, each in record form and fresh: `ref` is `t:state` or a field path under it, or a declared `t:`, `q:` or `d:` ref, a per-candidate question by its declared ref, and `values` that read's own list, taken as the lint takes one. A `loaded` that is not exactly a `LoadedDesign` or a `ref` that is not a `str` is a `TypeError`; a ref naming no read of `loaded`, a list for a read its grid or type already fills, and a read nothing fills are each a `ValueError`. Question reach builds its trials from these points.

`dead_questions`' runs grow as the product of the reads' points; for a design too large to run, `ommat.evaluation.question_reach` asks the same question from recorded turns (see [Question reach](#question-reach)).

`dead_questions` reports each question `live`, `no-change-found`, `not-on-grid` or `not-grounded`. A per-candidate question has one grid entry, shared by its grounded entries, and one report, under its declared ref. At each assignment of the other reads its set's value is fixed, by the point for a `t:` set and by the run for a `d:` set, which the lint learns by first running the assignment with every grounded entry at its first point, and that value grounds the entries; a later run whose `d:` set differs raises `ValueError` naming the set, since a set that moves with the entries cannot be fixed. Each grounded entry is filled as its question is, and the entries cross after the other reads, in code-point order, so they vary fastest. An empty set, or one holding a marker, grounds nothing. The question is `live` when any of its grounded entries has two points whose runs differ, and `not-grounded` when it is not `live` and no assignment grounds an entry of it. A report's point holds each grounded entry under its grounded ref.

## Many turns

`ommat.helpers.decide_many(states, loaded, bindings, *, concurrency, settings=None, codec=None, observer_for=None)` decides one turn per state, at most `concurrency` at once, on the caller's running loop, and returns each turn's `Decision`, or its `TurnFailure`, in input order. Every turn shares `loaded`, `bindings`, `settings` and `codec`, and each has its own deadline, counted from its own start as `decide` counts one; `observer_for(index)`, when given, gives each turn's observer. A state listed twice is decided twice, so a repeat is a state listed N times; identical repeats share a record hash, so how many there were is the list's.

Each turn warns of a defect as decide does; under a filter that makes the warning an error, it is that turn's `TurnFailure.error`, with its record. A turn ended alone is collected and the others run on: an `EntryRefusal`, an observer's raise, any other `Exception`, or a cancellation of that turn alone, as from a binding raising `CancelledError`. Its `TurnFailure` holds the exception as `error` and the record so far as `record`, `None` for an `EntryRefusal`, which `settings` or a `codec` of another form gives each turn, and for a raise of `observer_for` itself, before its turn opens. A turn cancelled from outside the run keeps its place as a `TurnFailure`, its record `None` when it had not yet started. A cancellation of the call itself, and a `BaseException` that is not an `Exception`, such as `KeyboardInterrupt`, cancel every turn and propagate. To stop a run early, a caller cancels it; a cap across the turns is never a binding that returns markers (§binding-deadline). `concurrency` that is not exactly an `int` of at least 1 is a `ValueError`, and `states` that are not an iterable, or are a string, bytes or a mapping, or an `observer_for` that is not callable, a `TypeError`, before any turn.

## Combining designs

`ommat.helpers.combine(*parts, bind, policy, name=None)` returns one `Design` from several. Each part is `Part(design, ports)`: `ports` maps each `d:` ref the part's declarations read and none of them provides, with no field path, to its type. The combined design holds every part's sources, rules, parameters, types, groups and targets under their own names, renaming nothing; a rule for each port; and `policy`, a `Rule` or `Table`, as its policy, keyed by its ref's name. Each part's own policy is an ordinary rule of it, and each part's `name` is dropped for `name`. A question several parts ask alike goes in a source of its own that each part declares alike, its needs' rules declared alike too, so the combined design asks it once and each part's rules read it under their own parameters.

`bind` maps each port to its provider. A `Rule` or `Table` providing the port is added as it is, keyed by the port's name; its `outputs` conform to the port's type. A `d:` ref, a field path allowed, is a pass-through: the table `Table(port, type, "F", inputs=[ref], rows=[["-", Input(ref)]])`, keyed by the port's name, so the port holds the ref's value, and, when the ref holds a marker, the marker's kind with `required` naming the ref (see [Decision tables](#decision-tables)). Each rule combine builds is a table, so a combined design's document reloads by import whenever its parts' does.

`combine` raises `InvalidCombination`, a `ValueError`, listing every failure it finds, each a `CombineFailure` of the names it concerns in code-point order and a one-line message, ordered by names, then message; the string is the messages, one per line. It refuses parts whose `state` types differ; an id one part declares as a source and another as a rule, the two sharing one namespace; a parameter path two parts declare, alike or not; a source, rule, type, group or target two parts declare differently under one id, one copy of each declared alike, equal as a value, being kept; a port that is not a `d:` ref with no field path, whose type does not parse, that two parts declare with two types, or that a part provides; a port `bind` leaves unbound; a `bind` key that is no port; a provider that is not a `d:` ref, or a rule providing another ref, or whose `outputs` do not conform to the port's type; and a rule it adds, for a port or the policy, whose id a part declares. A refused port is not also reported unbound. A part that is not exactly a `Part`, a `bind` that is not a mapping, a provider that is not a string, `Rule` or `Table`, and a `policy` that is neither are a `TypeError`, and so is no part. Everything else, a read with no provider among them, is load's to refuse, when the caller loads the combined design.

`ommat.helpers.standalone(part, ports=None, *, given=())` returns the part's design with each port provided, so a part loads and decides alone. A port in `ports` is `Table(port, type, "F", inputs=[], rows=[[value]])`, `value` the one `ports` gives it, a value of the port's type or `Unsure`. A port in `given` takes its value each turn: the source `ports`, under the oracle `ports`, provides `t:<name>` for each such port `d:<name>`, of the port's type, and the pass-through table `Table(port, type, "F", inputs=["t:<name>"], rows=[["-", Input("t:<name>")]])` carries it to the port, so the caller binds `ports` at decide and the record holds each value as the source's tool result. Each table is keyed by its port's name. It refuses, with `InvalidCombination`, a port in neither or in both, a key of either that is no port, a port as `combine` refuses one, a table whose id the part declares, and, when a port is given, a source `ports` or a `t:<name>` the part declares.

The ready sources that name no oracle are one batch each round (see [The binding protocol](#the-binding-protocol)), so a combined design asks every part's first-round questions together. A part that depends on another reads it through a port, rule to rule, within a round, rather than gating its sources on it.

An oracle name is shared across parts as within one design, so a binding under a name several parts use answers every part's sources under it.

## Replay

`replay(record, loaded, *, observer=None)` reruns a recorded turn under any loaded design and returns the replay record and the findings. Before any rerun it returns `None` and one finding, in this order: `wrong-design` for a record before format 8, which holds no policy's entry; `unfinished` for one that holds no policy's entry or holds a `cause` item, as a cancelled turn's; and `nothing-to-replay` for one whose `t:state` entry is missing or holds a marker, as a broken entry's, which needs no codec. Otherwise the findings are, in order, `design-mismatch`, `helpers-mismatch`, each `stale-question`, and each `entry` in code-point order of its ref. The decision is compared as the policy's entry, like any entry.

`design-mismatch` gives the recorded and replayed design hashes. A function component's identity includes its module and qualified name, so the usual cause is a component whose module was `__main__`, a design defined in the script that ran it. To find the member that differs, keep the design document beside each record and diff it against `loaded.document` with any JSON diff; a design document is canonical JSON, so the output is stable. A design that must live in a script keeps its document and is loaded elsewhere with `load(document, functions={...})`, keyed by each function component's key, a rule's name or `<source>.when`, which keeps its hash.

An `entry` finding compares the two items by canonical bytes, `order` included, so a rerun that holds an object in another order than the record is a finding. Replay builds typed values from the record's values with each object in the order its item's `order` gives, a record's fields in their declared order, both the state entry it passes in place of the encoder and the recorded values its fixture answers with.

The fixture checks each source the rerun asks against the base record, the record whose calls answer: the record replayed, `other` for `use_answers`, and the record as read for `intervene`. A ref is stale, gets no key and is written `not-returned`, when the base holds no answer for it (`no-answer`): no call item lists its source, the call wrote no entry for it, or the call was recorded `Failed` and the batch asked now is not exactly that call's sources, or holds a stale source, so the failure cannot be given back whole; when a need's value differs from the base's entry at the same ref by its ordered encoding, wording and per-candidate slots included, every ref of the source (`needs`); when its question's kind, labels, a candidate-valued set's members included, value type, list flag or question identity under the replayed design differ from the call item's, or, given the base's design, a `t:` ref's declared type differs from that design's (`question`); and, given the base's design, every other ref of a source whose payload differs from that design's by canonical bytes (`payload`), or whose `oracle` key differs or that `intervene` asks afresh (`oracle`). The clauses are tried in that order, but for one ref a differing question comes before a missing entry. A ref an edit set is answered as set and never checked; a source whose call an edit failed is not checked against the base, and its failure comes back only for that call asked whole, as for a recorded one, its refs otherwise stale. `replay` and `use_answers` are given no base design, so they check no payload, `oracle` key or `t:` type. A recorded marker a binding cannot return, such as `not-returned`, is given no key, which writes it again, and is not stale. Each stale ref is one `stale-question` finding, `{"kind", "ref", "source", "clause"}`, in code-point order of `ref`, after `helpers-mismatch` and before the `entry` findings. An `entry` finding compares the two entries whole, so it can differ in its derivation or its `rows` alone.

## Evaluation

`ommat.evaluation.use_answers(record, loaded, other)` reruns `record`'s turn under `loaded` with `other`'s call items and entries answering, `q:` and `t:` refs alike; `use_answers_sync` is its twin. `record` and `other` are each a `Record` or a stored document, read through `read_record`. It returns `{"other": h, "findings": [...]}`, `h` the other record's hash. The state entry, the run settings, the cut and every comparison are `record`'s, so the findings are replay's with `other` as the fixture and the base: `stale-question` names what `other` does not answer as asked now. A `Failed` call or a returned marker in `other` comes back as replay returns a recorded one. Neither record is edited, and neither's `unhashed` is read.

After both records are read and before any replay it raises `EvaluationRefusal`, `reason` in this order: `invalid-record` when a document does not read, carrying the `InvalidRecord` as `error`; `wrong-design` for a record before format 8, then `unfinished` for one that holds no policy's entry or holds a `cause` item, then `nothing-to-replay` for one whose state entry is missing or holds a marker, then `wrong-design` for one whose design hash is not `loaded.design_hash`, each for `record` then `other`; and `different-turn` when the two state entries or `settings` items differ as replay compares items, `order` included. `turn` is the refused record's hash, `other`'s for `different-turn`, and `None` for `invalid-record`, which carries `error` instead; every other attribute is `None`. A `loaded` that is not exactly a `LoadedDesign` is an `EntryRefusal`, before either record is read.

An `EntryRefusal` from a rerun, such as one for a record whose run settings `decide` refuses, passes through every job that reruns a turn unwrapped, never as an `EvaluationRefusal`.

A replay record, the `Record` that `replay` returns, is a valid `record` or `other`. To compare two models decided under different parameter sets, replay the second's record under the first's loaded design and pass the replay record as `other`; a threshold that makes the rerun ask a source the second never called shows as `stale-question` in the replay's findings, and its entries come back `not-returned` in `use_answers`'s.

### Interventions

`intervene(record, recorded_design, interventions, *, rerun_design=None, functions=None)`, `async` with a `_sync` twin, replays a copy of `record`, decided under `recorded_design`, once with every intervention of the list `interventions` applied, under `rerun_design` when it is given and `recorded_design` otherwise. It takes `record` as `use_answers` does and returns `{"record", "findings", "stale"}`: `record` the replay record, `findings` replay's, and `stale` the refs the `stale-question` findings name, in code-point order. The edited copy is not returned; the replay record holds each pinned answer as set. Each intervention is one of five shapes:

- `{"ref", "value"}` sets the `q:` or `t:` entry at `ref`, which a call recorded `returned` wrote, to `value`: for a `q:` ref the binding's form of an answer, the probabilities by label or an extraction answer's five members, built against the call item's question, its labels as asked, a candidate-valued set's from the set's entry; for a `t:` ref a value of its declared type in `recorded_design`, a candidate set's members distinct; or `Unavailable` with one of the six codes a binding may return and the record's format holds under a returned call, written naming `ref` alone. An extraction answer's spans must lie within its question's text as the log holds it. The entry is pinned.
- `{"source", "code"}` sets the call item listing `source` to `Failed` with `code`, one of `raised`, `timed-out`, `invalid-return` and `unexpected-ref`, its cause naming the refs of every entry the call wrote in code-point order, its hashed provenance members null, `snapshot` before format 5 among them, and `claimed` empty, where its format holds them, and each of those entries `Unavailable` with that cause.
- `{"path", "value"}` sets the state entry's field at `path`, a ref such as `t:state.sender.address` naming a field the recorded state holds, to a value; the state with it set must conform to `recorded_design`'s state declaration.
- `{"rule", "output"}` replaces the declaration of the rule of the design run providing the `d:` ref `rule`, under its name, by a `U` table over the rule's reads, or a table's input reads, each read once, with one row of `-` cells whose output is a copy of `output` or, for the class `Unsure`, `Unsure`, keeping `provides`, `outputs`, `candidates`, `grounded` and `target`. Every rule so replaced is replaced in one document, loaded with `functions`, or when `functions` is `None` with the design run's own callables, since the edited document names the same code. Replay runs under the modified design, so `design-mismatch` is always a finding. The rule's own entry is a finding whenever the table's entry differs from the recorded one: always for a function rule, whose entry holds `derivation` where the table's holds `rows`, and for a table unless its recorded entry holds the same value or marker and `rows` `[1]`.
- `{"ask"}` asks the source `ask` afresh: every ref of it not stale by an earlier clause is stale, clause `oracle`, as for a rebound oracle or a re-asked failure. `intervene` asks nothing live, so the ref is written `not-returned`.

The refusals below make the targets disjoint, so applying the interventions gives one copy whatever the list's order, and replay runs once on it; an answer over a candidate set another intervention sets takes the new set's labels, and a span is bounded by its text as another sets it. `rerun_design` is a design the caller built and loaded, such as one with another payload, `oracle` key, question or type; each source is checked against `recorded_design` as the base's design, so what `rerun_design` changes in a source makes it stale (§Replay). An empty list with `rerun_design` given replays the record under `rerun_design` alone.

A record edit is made on a copy of the record: its log edited, every `entry` item's `order` derived afresh, its `hash` set to the edited log's record hash and the copy read back through `read_record`; a list of rules and asks alone makes no copy. Replay reruns the copy, its state entry, calls and cut, so a finding is a change downstream of the interventions; an edited entry itself, answered as set, is none. Each source is checked against the record as read, whose entries its call was asked over, so a source with a need an edit changed is `stale-question` and not answered; a pinned entry is never checked, even when a need of its source changed, and a call an edit failed comes back only when asked whole. Neither `record`, `recorded_design` nor `rerun_design` changes.

A `recorded_design`, or a `rerun_design` other than `None`, that is not exactly a `LoadedDesign` is an `EntryRefusal` naming it, before the record is read. `intervene` then reads the record and refuses `invalid-record`, `wrong-design`, `unfinished`, `nothing-to-replay` and `wrong-design` as `use_answers` does for `record`, `recorded_design` being the design compared, before any edit, load or replay. It then refuses, in order, `invalid-edit` when `interventions` is not a list or an item has another shape, the item's index in the message; `no-edit` when the list is empty and `rerun_design` is `None`; `duplicate-target` when two interventions name one ref, one call, by any of its sources, one path or a path within another, one rule or one asked source, naming the later's; and `conflicting-edits` when an entry is set beside a failed call that wrote it, naming `ref` and `source`, or a source is asked beside a set entry it provides in the design run, naming `ref` and `source`, or beside a failed call that lists it, naming `source`. Then come, in this order: each intervention's target refusals in list order, a record edit's against the record as read and a rule's or an asked source's against the design run, the rows of the table below up to and including `marker`; each value against its declared type in `recorded_design` alone, in list order, `nonconforming` for an entry's value, an answer whose labels are not from a candidate set among them with no span bound, and for a state field's value with the recorded state; each answer, against the copy with every edit applied, its labels from a candidate set and its spans against its text as edited, in list order, `nonconforming`; and `invalid-design` for the rules made constant, naming the first whose output holds bytes, else the first. Nothing replays before every check passes:

### Scenarios

`scenario(record, recorded_design, interventions, bindings, *, rerun_design=None, functions=None)`, `async` with a `_sync` twin, is `intervene` with its stale questions asked live: it takes the same arguments, makes the same checks and copy and gives the same refusals in the same order, but checks `bindings` as `decide` does against the design run, `rerun_design` or `recorded_design`, after the design checks and before the record is read. Each batch the turn asks is answered from the copy, every source in it with a stale ref passed whole to the binding under the batch's oracle name as a sub-batch, with the call's `seconds`; such a source is stale whole, each of its refs but a pinned one named by a `stale-question` finding, a ref fresh alone with its source's first clause. The two answers merge into the live `CallResult`, so a mixed call item carries the live provenance; its `warnings`, when they conform, are led by `answered from record <hash>`, the base record's hash, one line `<ref>: pinned` per ref answered from the copy, and one line `<ref>: named truncated, answered from record` per such ref the live `truncated` named, which is dropped from it. A pinned ref of a live source keeps its set value. A batch with no stale source is answered as `intervene` answers it, with null provenance. A live result that is not a `CallResult` holding a `dict`, a `Failed` among them, or a raise, fails the whole call, pinned batchmates included (§fault-scope); a live value for a ref of a batchmate the binding was not asked fails it `unexpected-ref`. A stale source is called as in a live turn, so a binding that writes writes, on the scenario's inputs. Bind a stand-in under the writes' oracle.

The turn runs on the running loop's clock under the base's run settings, with no forced cut: a deadline the base met can expire here, and the record says so. A later source is stale only when its needs, or its question, differ from the base's, so a live answer that leaves every entry a later source needs as it was, a rule's output among them, leaves that source pinned (early cutoff). It returns `{"record", "findings", "stale", "pinned"}`: the record the turn wrote, replay's findings against `record`, a `stale-question` finding for each ref asked live, `stale` those refs and `pinned` the refs whose answer came from the copy, a recorded failure given back among them and none of a call the live binding failed, each in code-point order.

### The deciding inputs

`deciding_inputs(record, loaded, ref, *, functions=None)`, `async` with a `_sync` twin, takes `record` as `use_answers` does and returns a `dict` from each declared read of the rule providing the entry at `ref`, in declared order, each read once, to one of four strings:

- `deciding`: some intervention on the read that ran, and was not undetermined, changed the entry at `ref`: replay gave an `entry` finding at `ref` whose value, or marker kind and cause code, differ, so a change of `rows`, `derivation`, `round` or a cause's refs alone is none, except at the policy's ref, which is compared as the decision is, refs included;
- `not-determined`: none did, and no intervention ran or one was undetermined;
- `markers-only`: none of these, and some target is not of a finite type, so it was tried at markers only;
- `not-deciding`: none of these: no intervention that ran changed the entry. Each is a single intervention, so joint effects are not explored.

A finite type is `boolean` or a label, either nullable, whose values are its labels in order, `false` before `true`, then null. Each intervention is a list of one for `intervene`, run through the same checks, copy and replay:

- A `q:` or `t:` read, other than `t:state`, is tried through its entry, field path dropped, so a read `t:x.f` is tried by setting the whole entry `t:x` at the values of its declared type, as `{"ref", "value"}` sets entries whole; a grounded declared ref through each of its entries, in log order. Each entry is set to `Unavailable` with each code a binding may return that the record's format holds, in code-point order, a `q:` answer to `refused` only; a `t:` entry of a finite type then to each value; and after every entry's, each call whose entries are all of the read's declared ref, once, in log order, as a grounded batch's are, is failed with each structural code, in code-point order.
- A `t:state` field is set to each value of a finite type; a field of another type, or `t:state` whole, has no intervention.
- A `d:` read's rule is made constant, outputting `Unsure`, then each value of a finite type, a field path dropped as for a `t:` read.
- A `candidate` read has no intervention.

An intervention `intervene` would refuse at its target, `null-call`, `failed-call`, `not-found` or `candidate-rule`, is not run, as for an entry not asked, an entry of a call recorded `Failed`, a field under a null record and a rule no table may replace; so a carried or unasked entry beside an asked one leaves the read to the asked one's interventions. Any other refusal propagates, `invalid-design` among them, as when a function the modified design names is missing from the `functions` given; with `functions` `None` the modified design loads with `loaded`'s own callables. An intervention that ran is undetermined when its replay gives `stale-question`, whatever else it finds, so a read that is a need of a later source is `not-determined` at every value that changes it. Interventions run in order and stop at the first that changes the entry. After `intervene`'s record checks, the job refuses `not-found`, naming `ref`, when `ref` is not a string or names no entry of the record a rule provides; then it replays the record unedited under `loaded` and refuses `baseline-differs`, the finding kinds in the message, when replay gives any finding but `helpers-mismatch`, which notes only the helper version; both before any intervention.

### The switching values

`switching_values(record, loaded, path, *, functions=None, results)`, `async` with a `_sync` twin, takes `record` as `use_answers` does, `path` a leaf of `loaded`'s parameter tree, the path the design declares it under (a mapping leaf, such as a `thresholds` map, is one leaf, and a dotted path into it names none), `functions` as `load` takes them, `None` taking `loaded`'s own callables, and `results` an `EvaluationStore`. It replays the record under each value's design, `loaded`'s document with the leaf replaced and loaded with `functions`, keeping none of them, and writes one entry, `{"kind": "switching-value", "format": 2, "key": {"record", "design", "parameter"}, "value"}`, the record hash, `loaded.design_hash` and `path`, which it returns. Each run's `design-mismatch` and `helpers-mismatch` are dropped. A record's runs reuse its first run's rule and source calls as `rescore`'s do, across every path of a batch.

- **Leaf types.** A `number` or `integer` leaf, or an interval of either, is tried at its own value, whose result is the record's, and in two rounds at the points of the lint's axis within the leaf's type (the boundaries, one inside each gap and one past each end): round 0 over the compared values the record holds, round 1 over those and the new ones round 0's replays recompute. On an integer axis each compared value also adds the integers either side of it, clipped to the interval, so the least integer above every boundary is tried. A compared value first recomputed in round 1 is not probed, and the component comparing it is listed in `unseen`. With no compared value the axis is the interval's two bounds, or `-(2^53 - 1)` and `2^53 - 1`. A `boolean` or `label` leaf is tried at every value. A `mapping<string, T>` leaf, `T` numeric, has each key varied alone as a leaf of type `T`, the others kept, its compared values those naming the leaf whole or that key's path.
- **Compared values.** For a table cell comparing an input against the leaf, that input's value: a bare read's entries, a grounded declared ref's each, through its field path, a `candidate` read's each member of its table's set as the set's entry holds it, or `mass`, `size` or `extract` over the read; for a pick helper's `bar` or `thresholds` binding, each label's probability in the answer, every key's at every label; for `extract`'s `null_bar`, its `p_null` when not `None`; for a `margin`, the greatest `float` whose shortest decimal does not exceed the top label's lead over the runner-up, exactly; for a function rule's `boundaries` entry naming the leaf, the read's value, or for a `q:` read the same measure by role, `mass` over its set, and `p_null`, `confidence` or `value` of an extraction. Only an `int` or `float` is a compared value, so a marker, a boolean, a string and null give none. A table whose `candidate` read finds its set's entry holding no value is listed in `unseen`.
- **Results.** A run that gives `stale-question`, as when the leaf fills a question's wording slot, is `"not-determined"`; a value whose design fails load is `{"not-loading": [{"check", "names", "message"}, ...]}`; the run goes on past both. Otherwise the result for an entry is `{"entry": item}`, the replayed item or null where the run wrote none, two compared by value, or marker kind and cause code, alone, so a change of `rows`, `derivation` or `round` is none. The policy's entry is the decision: it is compared as the decision is, by value, or by marker with its whole cause, refs included, and always listed, so a leaf that never switches the decision says so.
- **The value.** `{"unseen", "entries"}`, or for a mapping leaf `{"unseen", "keys"}`, `keys` mapping each key in code-point order to `{"entries"}`. `entries` maps the policy's ref and each other ref, in code-point order, whose entry some run changed, a `not-determined` run included. A summary is `"carries"`, and its ref listed, when at some value tried, read from that run's matched rows whatever the entry's value there, a table row giving the entry's output, under `F` the first matched row, under another hit policy each row it uses, outputs `Param` of the leaf. Otherwise, for a numeric leaf, it is `{"switching", "slacks", "classes"}`: `classes` the runs of adjacent values tried with one result, ascending, each `{"least", "greatest", "result"}`; `switching` one value between each two adjacent classes, a `not-determined` or `not-loading` one included: on an integer axis the upper class's least, on a number axis the lower class's greatest when it alone of the two facing values is a compared value, else the upper class's least; each switching value is given once; a `not-loading` class's edges are located only to the values tried, and while `unseen` is not empty so are the switching values and slacks, one of a listed component lying anywhere between two tried values; `slacks` `[below, above]`, the switching values bounding the class holding the leaf's own value, each minus that value, null on a side with none. For a `boolean` or `label` leaf it is `{"classes"}`, each `{"values", "result"}`, the values giving one result, in declared order, `false` before `true`. `unseen` is empty only when every way the leaf reaches a component is a comparison against a probed value. Otherwise it names, in code-point order, each component the leaf reaches another way: a function rule declaring the leaf whose `boundaries` field names neither it nor, for a mapping leaf, the key path of each key the leaf's value holds; a `C+`, `C<` or `C>` table with a `Param` row of the leaf beside another row, whose output mixes it with other values; each component reading an entry that holds the leaf, one a table row outputs `Param` of in the document, or a function component declaring the leaf provides when its type holds other than booleans and labels, one level deep; each component of a value round 1 found; each table comparing a `candidate` read against the leaf whose set's entry holds no value; and each component a failure names when a value tried does not load, whose log is never read for compared values. It is empty for a `boolean` or `label` leaf.

After reading the record it raises `EvaluationRefusal`, `reason` in this order: `invalid-record`, `wrong-design`, `unfinished`, `nothing-to-replay` and `wrong-design` as `intervene` does; `unknown-path`, naming `path`, or `None` when it is not a string, when `path` is no leaf, a node, a path not in the tree or a malformed one; `unsupported-leaf`, naming `path`, for any leaf but a numeric one, a `boolean`, a label and a numeric mapping, as a `string`, `bytes`, nullable, list or non-numeric mapping leaf, the type in the message; `invalid-design`, carrying the `InvalidDesign` as `error`, when `loaded.document` does not load with `functions`; `held-key`, naming the key, when the store holds it, each before any replay; `baseline-differs`, the finding kinds in the message, when replay under `loaded` gives a finding but `helpers-mismatch`; `unrepresentable-slack`, naming `path`, for a slack no finite `float` holds, or of an integer leaf past `2^53 - 1` in magnitude; and `held-key` when the store refuses the write. A caller rerunning a record it has scored reads the held entry with `results.lookup("switching-value", key)`, a key in the list a `held-key` refusal carries.

`switching_values_batch(records, loaded, paths, *, functions=None, results)`, `async` with a `_sync` twin, is `switching_values` for each record and each path, the entries returned in record order, then path order, each the entry the single job writes. `loaded` that is not exactly a `LoadedDesign` is an `EntryRefusal`, records or none; `records` or `paths` that is not a sequence, or is a `str` or `bytes`, is a `TypeError`. Every record is read and checked, every path's type checked, and `loaded.document` loaded with `functions`, refused `invalid-design`, in that order and with the single job's refusals, before any replay, the batch also refusing a record whose run settings decide refuses there, where the single job refuses it at its replay; then a key named twice is refused `held-key`, naming each repeat, and any key the store holds is refused `held-key`, naming every such key in entry order, each before any replay. A refusal after the first write leaves the entries already written.

### The switch screen

`screen_switches(records, loaded, within)`, sync, says for each record and each numeric leaf whether every value of the leaf within a window around its own value decides the record's turn as the record did, reading each record alone and replaying nothing, so a caller runs the switching values only over the records it does not clear. `within` maps each path to a width, an `int` or `float`, finite and at least 0. A path is a leaf's, as `switching_values` takes it, or a key path, `leaf.key`, as `rescore_grid` takes it; a mapping leaf given whole is screened key by key.

- **The window.** The values of the leaf's type from its own value minus the width to its own value plus it, both ends included, compared exactly as the choice helpers compare, clipped to the type's interval, or with none to the values the type holds, `-(2^53 - 1)` to `2^53 - 1` on an integer axis and the finite `float`s on a number axis; on an integer axis, the integers among them. A mapping leaf's each key has its own window around its own value.
- **The rows.** One per record and path, record-major, paths in code-point order: `{"record", "parameter", "within", "clear", "near", "unseen", "worded", "not_loading"}`, `record` the record hash and `parameter` and `within` the path and width as given. `near` lists, by key then ascending, each value the record compares the leaf or the key against, as `switching_values` reads its round-0 values, that lies inside the window or at an end where its comparison's tie does not join the window's inside, none parting a window of one value, as `{"key", "value", "components"}`, `key` null for a leaf that is no mapping and `components` the comparing components in code-point order. `unseen` names, in code-point order, each component the leaf reaches other than by a comparison the screen sees, what `rescore_grid` refuses as `hidden-leaf` with every key of a mapping leaf varied, and each table comparing a `candidate` read against the leaf whose set's entry in the record holds no value. `worded` names, in code-point order, each source with a question whose wording takes a slot from the leaf. `not_loading` lists, as `{"key", "value"}`, `key` null, each value tried in the window whose design does not load. A leaf's value is read by load only where a table cell names it: a `between` holding it, whose low bound may pass its high one, and two rows of a `U` or `A` table naming it that may come to overlap. The cuts are the numbers those checks compare it with, each bound of such a `between` and, for each such table, every number its cells hold or name by another leaf and every interval bound of an entry's type or of a helper's, `mass`'s `[0, 1]` and `size`'s `[0, 2^53 - 1]`, and with each the integers from two below its floor to two above its ceiling. A leaf no such cell names is loaded at no value; otherwise the design is loaded at the window's ends and each cut the leaf can hold, and between each two at an integer and, on a number axis, at a value that is no integer. A key of a mapping leaf, which no cell compares, is loaded at no value. `clear` is true exactly when the other four are empty; then every value of the window gives the record's own entries.
- **What it trusts.** The record is taken as the run under `loaded`, after the record checks alone: a record `switching_values` would refuse `baseline-differs` is screened as written. A function rule's `boundaries` are trusted as `switching_values` trusts them. `near` is no switching value: a value it lists may part nothing the policy decides. The window answers whether a value inside it decides otherwise, not whether a switching value lies within the width: at an end that is a compared value whose tie joins the inside, the record clears though `switching_values` gives the switching value there, its slack exactly the width, so a caller counting that as near widens the width past it.
- **The refusals.** `loaded` that is not exactly a `LoadedDesign` is an `EntryRefusal`; then `records` that is not a sequence, or is a `str` or `bytes`, and `within` that is not a mapping are a `TypeError`, in that order. The records are then read and refused as `rescore` refuses them, `invalid-record`, `wrong-design` before format 8, `unfinished`, `nothing-to-replay` and `wrong-design`, each over every record in turn; a record whose run settings `decide` refuses is screened, as the screen reads none of them. Then a path that is not a string is refused `unknown-path`, naming `None`. Then, each path in code-point order: `unknown-path` and `unsupported-leaf`, naming the path, as `rescore_grid` refuses a path, `unsupported-leaf` too for a `boolean` or label leaf, which has no window; then its width, a `TypeError` when it is not exactly an `int` or `float`, a `ValueError` when it is negative or a `float` not finite. So a later path's refusal waits on an earlier path's width. With no records, or an empty `within`, it returns `[]` after the checks.

### Rescoring

`rescore(records, recorded, designs)`, `async` with a `_sync` twin, decides each record's turn again under each of `designs` from the record's own answers, building no record, for a grid of settings scored by its decisions. `recorded` is the design the records were decided under. Each record is read in turn, one that does not read refused `invalid-record`; then, each check over every record before the next, a record before format 8 is refused `wrong-design`, an unfinished one `unfinished`, one whose state entry is missing or a marker `nothing-to-replay`, and one whose design hash is not `recorded`'s `wrong-design`, each naming `turn`; then a record whose run settings decide refuses is an `EntryRefusal`; all before any run. Each run is replay's under that design, the fixture the record's calls and the run settings and cut the record's, except that each source asked is checked against the record under `recorded`, where `replay(record, design)` checks it under `design`: a design whose source carries another payload or `oracle` key than `recorded`'s, or another type for a `t:` ref it provides, gives `not-determined` where replay under it decides: for every ref of a source whose payload or `oracle` key differs, and for a retyped `t:` ref alone. A record's first run is kept, and a later run of it under a design differing from that run's only in leaf values, its callables the same objects, takes a rule's outcome from the kept run rather than calling it when the rule uses no leaf whose value differs and reads what it read there. It takes a source call's outcome from the kept run in the same way, its entries, call item and stale refs, its cost charged to the budget again, rather than asking the record, when it asks in one batch exactly the sources that call asked together, in the same round, no question of theirs uses a leaf whose value differs, and each is passed what it was passed there: the same refs to answer, its needs' values and its questions' labels and their descriptions; a function `when` is called as ever. The rows are those of calling every rule for rules whose value is a function of what they read, their leaves and their candidate; a rule may be called fewer times than there are designs, so one with a counter, a clock or randomness can give other rows. It returns one row per design and record, design-major, `{"design", "record", "decision", "stale"}`: the design hash, the record hash, the policy's entry as decide writes it, `{"value": v}` or `{"marker": {"kind", "cause"}}`, and `[]`; or, when the run asked a question the record does not answer, `"not-determined"` and the stale refs in code-point order. A state that does not conform to a design's `t:state` gives that design's policy entry `Unavailable` with `invalid-return` naming `t:state`, as decide writes it. `recorded` or a member of `designs` that is not exactly a `LoadedDesign` is an `EntryRefusal`; `records` or `designs` that is not a sequence is a `TypeError`.

`rescore_grid(records, recorded, loaded, points)`, `async` with a `_sync` twin, is `rescore` over the designs a list of parameter points gives, each record rerun once per region of points whose runs cannot differ rather than once per point.

- **The points.** `points` is a sequence of `{path: value}` mappings, all naming one non-empty set of paths; each point's design is `loaded` with those leaves holding the point's values, as `load` would build it. A path is a leaf's, as the design declares it, or a key path, `leaf.key`, naming a key the value `loaded` holds of a numeric mapping leaf: a mapping leaf such as `route/bars` is given whole, `{"route/bars": {"email": 0.6, ...}}`, or by key, `{"route/bars.email": 0.6}`, that key's value replacing the one `loaded`'s leaf holds and every other key kept. A point may name one leaf whole and another by key, never one leaf both ways.
- **The rows.** `rescore`'s rows, one per point and record, point-major, each with its point's design hash, equal to `rescore(records, recorded, designs)` over the points' designs row for row. A key path, and each key of a mapping leaf given whole, is bucketed against the comparisons naming the leaf whole or that key's path. The equality rests on the design's `boundaries`: a function rule's are trusted as the switching-value job trusts them, each key path for its key, so a rule comparing a grid leaf its `boundaries` do not declare is refused, not detected.
- **The refusals.** `recorded` or `loaded` that is not exactly a `LoadedDesign` is an `EntryRefusal`; then `points` that is not a sequence, a point that is not a mapping, and `records` that is not a sequence are a `TypeError`. The records are then read and refused as `rescore` refuses them, and before any run it raises `EvaluationRefusal`, `reason` in this order, each path in code-point order: `uneven-grid` when the points do not all name one non-empty path set; `overlapping-paths`, naming the key path, when they name a leaf both whole and by a key path; `unknown-path` and `unsupported-leaf`, naming `path`, as `switching_values` refuses a leaf, and `unknown-path` for a key path whose leaf is no mapping or whose key `loaded`'s value of it lacks; `hidden-leaf`, naming `path` and the components in the message, for a numeric grid path that fills no question's wording slot, its leaf none, and reaches a component other than by a comparison: what `switching_values` lists in `unseen` from the document, the loaded design's types and its declared reads, except that a function rule need name only a key path's key, or for a mapping leaf given whole each key whose value differs between two points, plus a table with any `Param` row of the leaf and a function rule declaring it whose output type holds anything but booleans and labels, or records and nullables of them; and `invalid-design`, carrying the `InvalidDesign` as `error` and the point's index in the message, for a point whose design does not load.
- **The empty grid.** With no points it returns `[]` after the record checks.

### The gold answers

`write_gold_answers(gold_set, records, labels, *, results, copy_from=None)`, sync, writes a labeller's labels into `results`, an `EvaluationStore`, under the gold set `gold_set`, and returns the entries it wrote, in write order. `records` is an iterable of records, each taken as `use_answers` takes `record`; a record passed twice counts once. `labels` is `{turn_id: {ref: label}}`, `turn_id` the record hash of a record passed, so the original turn's id and never a replay record's, and `ref` a `q:` entry a call wrote in it, a marker included. A label is the name of one of the question's labels, `yes` or `no` for `noul`, a declared label's for `choice` and a level's for `score`, never its description; for a candidate-valued set, a fixed name or a candidate of the set's entry in the same record, the fixed names alone when that entry holds a marker; for an extraction question `{"value": v}`, `v` `None` or conforming to the question's `value_type` and `is_list`; or `None`, which writes nothing for its key. The question is read from the entry's call item's `questions`, and no design is read.

Each entry is `{"kind": "gold-answer", "format": 2, "key": {"gold_set", "question", "turn", "candidate"}, "value"}`: the question identity, the turn id, and for a grounded ref its candidate, the bracketed string decoded, else `""`. Its `value` is the label's name, or the extraction object with `v` as the answer holds it, a number a `float`. The refs of one turn asking one question for one candidate share a key and write one entry: refs given equal labels, or refs absent from `labels` beside given ones, take the given label. With `copy_from` a gold set's name, every key is looked up under it, and a key no ref of which is given a label takes the value held there, a format-1 label string included; a given `None` withdraws it, and a turn not passed carries nothing over. A key given no label and none copied gets no entry.

Before any write it raises `EvaluationRefusal`, `reason` in this order: `invalid-record` for a record that does not read, carrying the `InvalidRecord` as `error`, `records[i]` in the message; `wrong-design`, naming `turn`, for a record before format 8; `unfinished`, naming `turn`, for a record that holds no policy's entry or holds a `cause` item; `not-found`, naming `turn` and `ref`, for a label of a turn not passed or a ref no call wrote in it, each label in `labels`' order; `invalid-label`, naming `turn` and `ref`, the label in the message, for a label of another form, in the same order; `conflicting-labels`, naming `turn` and the key, the refs and labels in the message, when one key's given labels differ by canonical bytes, `None` and a label among them; `empty-copy`, the set's name in the message, when `copy_from` holds none of the keys, whatever their entries' formats; then for each key given no label, in key order, `unknown-entry`, naming the key, for its entry under `copy_from` of a format other than 1 and 2, and `invalid-label`, naming `turn` and the key's first ref, for an old value that is not a label of the question, checked as a given label is; and `held-key`, naming every key to be written that the store holds, in write order. A write the store then refuses is another run's, raised as `held-key` with `key` the held key, then the keys this run wrote, in write order. The writes are not atomic across keys: an `OSError` or a racing run mid-write leaves the keys already written, and since the store is write-once, a rerun refuses `held-key` naming them. Any other refusal the store raises at a write passes through. `labels` or one of its values that is not a mapping is a `TypeError`.

### The gold record

`gold_record(record, recorded_design, gold_set, *, results, rerun_design=None)`, `async` with a `_sync` twin, replays `record` as `intervene` does with every `q:` entry a returned call wrote set to its gold answer under `gold_set` in `results`, an `EvaluationStore`, found as `write_gold_answers` keys it: a label as all the probability on that label and none on the question's other labels as asked, an extraction answer `{"value": v}` as `v` for `value` and `guess`, `p_null` 1 for a null `v` and 0 otherwise, `confidence` 1 and no `span`. A question with no gold answer is given none: it is stale, clause `no-answer`, and its entry `not-returned`. A question whose call was recorded `Failed` keeps that failure, the call given back whole as replay gives one back; its gold answer, if any, is not read for an answer. Its decision is what correct answers lead to under the design run, `rerun_design` or `recorded_design`; comparing it with any run of the same turn inputs is the caller's. It returns `{"record", "findings", "stale", "missing"}`: the replay record, replay's findings, the gold answers changing what follows them and an entry set being none itself, the stale refs, and `missing`, the asked refs given no gold answer, a failed call's among them, each in code-point order.

`gold_set` that is not a `str`, or `results` that is not an `EvaluationStore`, is a `TypeError`, then the designs and the record are refused as `intervene` refuses them; a gold entry of a format other than 1 and 2, or whose value is not of its question's form, is `unknown-entry`, naming its key, before any replay.

### The answers table

`answers(records, loaded, *, gold_set=None, results=None)`, sync, reads `records`, an iterable of records each taken as `use_answers` takes `record`, in list order, and gives one row per `q:` entry a call wrote, in log order, so one per candidate for a per-candidate question; a record passed twice gives its rows twice. A row is `{"turn", "ref", "question", "candidate", "identity", "kind", "labels", "answer", "marker", "top", "target", "latent_targets", "groups"}`: the record hash, the entry's ref, its declared ref, the bracketed candidate decoded or `""`, the question identity and kind from the call item, the labels as asked, a candidate-valued set's fixed part when the set's entry holds a marker, null for an extraction question, the recorded answer object or null, the marker or null, the most probable label, null on a tie for it and for any answer but a probability one, the question's `target` and `latent_targets` as `loaded` declares them, and each group naming the declared ref as `{"group", "reason"}`. With `gold_set` and `results`, an `EvaluationStore`, given together, each row also holds `gold`, the gold answer found as `write_gold_answers` keys it, and `erred`: a probability answer errs when its top label is not the gold label, a tie erring; an extraction answer when `{"value": v}`, `v` its value, differs from the gold answer by canonical bytes, null a value like any other. A row with no gold answer has both null, and a marker's `erred` is null; no row is dropped. Counts and rates over the rows are the caller's; rows of records under different designs are not joined, since one question identity may carry other targets.

A `loaded` that is not exactly a `LoadedDesign` is an `EntryRefusal`; `records` that are not an iterable, or are a string, bytes or a mapping, `gold_set` or `results` given alone, a `gold_set` not a `str` and `results` not an `EvaluationStore` are a `TypeError`. Each record is then read and refused `invalid-record`, carrying the `InvalidRecord` as `error`, or `wrong-design`, naming `turn`, for a record before format 8 or a design hash other than `loaded.design_hash`; a gold entry is refused `unknown-entry` as `gold_record` refuses one.

### Groups

A design's `groups` name sets of refs to be considered together, `Group(refs, reason)`, and say why: `reason` is a non-empty list of `convergent`, refs that should agree, such as one question in other wordings; `discriminant`, refs that should not move together; `exclusive`, at most one true; `exhaustive`, at least one true; and `consistent`, refs that must fit by a rule a reviewer knows. The document holds a group as `{"refs", "reason"}`, each in code-point order. Load refuses a group that names an undeclared ref or one ref twice, and checks its grounded refs as [Per-candidate questions](#per-candidate-questions) says (check 3); and one that gives no reason, a reason outside the five or one reason twice (check 9). No job reads a group: whether the records bear one out, against gold answers or otherwise, is the caller's check.

### Question reach

`question_reach(records, loaded, *, values=None)`, `async` with a `_sync` twin, asks `dead_questions`' question from recorded turns: whether a question's answer, changed alone, changes the decision. A recorded turn is an assignment of the other answers the design reaches, so a question behind several gates is tried where they stand open, for a design whose cross product of points is too large for the lint to run. `records` is an iterable of records, each taken as `use_answers` takes `record`, in list order, a repeat run again; a string, bytes, a mapping or a non-iterable is a `TypeError`.

For each record, and each `q:` entry it holds whose call is neither null nor recorded `Failed`, in log order, the job sets the entry as `intervene` sets one to each of its points other than its recorded value or marker by canonical bytes, and replays once per point. An entry's points are its declared question's, as `dead_questions` fills them, a grounded entry's with the marker naming that entry, and the marker, `Unavailable` with `refused`, last; a candidate-valued question's are each fixed label and then the first candidate at 1, every other label the call asked with at 0. A returned call always rendered a candidate: an empty set, a marker, or labels the engine refuses leave the question out with its call null, so it has no trial in that record and no candidate point is ever skipped. A trial is undetermined when its replay reports `stale-question`, as when the answer opens a gate on a source the turn never called, changes a candidate set or changes a need of a later source, and otherwise reaches when the decision it wrote differs from the record's by its bytes (see [Decide](#decide)), a marker's cause and refs included.

It returns `{"records": [hash], "trials": n, "questions": {ref: {"status", "witness"}}, "narrowed": [ref]}`, `records` the record hashes in list order, `trials` the trials run, and `questions` keyed by every `q:` ref a source provides, a per-candidate question under its declared ref. A question is `live` when some trial of an entry of it reaches; otherwise `not-determined` when some trial was undetermined; otherwise `no-change-found` when some trial ran; otherwise `never-asked`. A `live` question's witness is `{"record", "ref", "value"}`, the first reaching trial by record, then log, then point order: the record's hash, the entry's own ref, a grounded entry's grounded ref, and the value set as the record writes it, an answer object or a marker; every other witness is `None`.

`values` is taken and checked as `dead_questions` takes it, before any record is read, its keys `q:` refs a source provides: a list for a question nothing else fills, such as an extraction question with no grid entry, or the labels to try for a label question with no grid entry, which `narrowed` then names. A key naming another read, a read nothing fills and each other slip is a `ValueError`. A `loaded` that is not exactly a `LoadedDesign` is an `EntryRefusal` before anything else. Each record is then read and refused as `deciding_inputs` refuses one, `reason` in this order: `invalid-record`, carrying the `InvalidRecord` as `error`; `wrong-design` for a record before format 8; `unfinished`; `nothing-to-replay`; `wrong-design` for another design hash; and `baseline-differs`, the finding kinds in the message, when replaying it unedited under `loaded` gives any finding but `helpers-mismatch`; each but the first naming `turn`. Each record is checked in full before the next is read, and every record before any trial. A trial's own refusals propagate, though none is expected, since the job tries only entries a returned call wrote, at values of their questions.

### The stores

Both stores are sync, and each is a directory, its `root`, holding one file per hash, named by the hash's algorithm as a directory and its 64 hex characters as the file name. Each file is written whole by exclusive create: the bytes go to a temporary file beside it, synced and given mode `0o644`, which is then hard-linked under its name, so a reader never sees part of a file and of two writers of one name exactly one succeeds. A store therefore needs a filesystem with hard links. Neither store replaces a file. An I/O failure other than a missing file propagates as `OSError`, never a refusal. Each refusal is an `EvaluationRefusal` whose attributes other than `reason` and `key` are `None`; `key` is a one-item list of the key refused for `bad-key`, `not-held`, `hash-mismatch`, `held-mismatch` and `held-key`, a design hash or a `dict` of an evaluation key's members, and `None` for the others.

`DesignStore(root).keep(loaded)` writes `loaded.document` unchanged at `sha256/<hex>` of `loaded.design_hash`, the hash the loaded design carries, never a key given beside it, and returns `None`; a `loaded` that is not exactly a `LoadedDesign` is a `TypeError`. Keeping a hash it holds compares the stored bytes: equal bytes are accepted, and differing bytes refused with `held-mismatch`. `fetch(key, *, functions=None)` returns the design held under `key`, loaded by `load` with `functions` passed through, refusing in order a `key` that is not `sha256:` and 64 lowercase hex characters with `bad-key`, before any path is built; a key it does not hold with `not-held`; and bytes whose hash is not `key` with `hash-mismatch`, the hash found in the message. `load`'s `InvalidDesign` passes through, so a design whose code the process lacks fails check 11, and a function defined in a function or a lambda needs `functions`.

`EvaluationStore(root).write(entry)` keeps an entry, `{"kind", "format", "key", "value"}`, as its canonical bytes at `<kind>/sha256/<hex>`, `<hex>` that of the hash of `key`'s canonical bytes, and returns `None`. The kinds and their key members, each a string:

- `gold-answer`: `gold_set`, `question`, `turn` and `candidate`;
- `switching-value`: `record`, `design` and `parameter`.

Before building a path it refuses in order: an entry that is not a `dict` of exactly those four members with `invalid-entry`; a `kind` outside the two with `unknown-kind`, the kind in the message; a `format` that is not an `int`, a `bool` excluded, then a `key` that is not a `dict` of exactly its kind's members, each a `str`, with `invalid-entry`, the first fault in the message; and an entry the canonical encoding refuses, `value` included, with `invalid-entry`, the encoder's reason in the message. An entry under a kind and key it holds is refused with `held-key`, whatever its bytes, and the first entry stands; the check and the write are one step. The store keeps `format` and `value` as given and reads neither. `lookup(kind, key)` refuses `kind` and `key` by the same checks, then returns the entry held under them or `None`; a held file that does not parse as an envelope, by the checks `write` makes, or whose `kind` or hash of its `key` differs from its path, is refused with `misfiled-entry`, the path in the message.

## Continuing a workflow

ommat has no resume within a turn. Within a turn, a source's binding may act, a write included, and the sources and rules that need its refs read what it returned. To continue a workflow past a turn, the caller decides a new turn whose state carries what earlier actions produced and the prior turn's record hash, with those fields declared in the state type. The prior turn's hash is the caller's claim, and ommat does not check it. The new turn replays without the earlier one.
