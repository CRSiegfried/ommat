# Spec

The normative description of ommat's behavior. The Python package is one implementation of it. JSON conformance fixtures that any implementation, in any language, must pass come later, and the spec will then carry its own version, separate from the package version.

The spec covers what record format 5 and design format 5 hold and the engine does:

- loading a design, and the load checks that refuse an invalid one;
- decide: the turn's entry with the codec, the broken entry, interpret over rounds of sources and function rules, the three markers and their causes, and compile with a function policy, the plan rules and the guard;
- the ledger and the observer, the record with its canonical JSON encoding and hashes, and reading a stored record back;
- the context block a design declares, rendered into the record, and the record views: the escalation view, the handoff view, the transcript view and the redacted view;
- the conversation renderers, which render a handoff as text and a transcript as Chat Completions or Pydantic AI messages;
- replay of a recorded turn with no model call, and the evaluation job `use_answers`;
- the binding protocol between the engine and a model adapter, the provenance profile each call states, the binding kit and the binding conformance suite;
- the answer kinds: `noul`, `choice` and `score`, which give a probability to each label, and `extraction`, a typed value read from a text, with the probability that the text holds none, the model's reading whatever it answered, its confidence and the span it read;
- the candidate-valued label set: a `choice` whose labels are a fixed part and the members of a `list<string>` candidate set, rendered each time it is asked;
- the helpers: the choice helpers, `extract` among them, and the combinators rules call, and the reference runner that runs a plan's steps;
- the live frontier, the answer grid and the `boundaries` fields, and the design lints that read them.

Outside it, and refused by load in a design that declares one: decision tables, the policy table among them, and data rules; plan declarations; a candidate set of records; and grounded declarations, per-candidate questions and rules over a candidate set. Also outside it: the run-time stopping certificate, multi-turn continuation, and every evaluation job but `use_answers`.

The sections below are written; the rest is not yet.

## Formats

A record and a design document each carry a format number, each its own, which changes only when that document's rules change. This engine writes record format 5 and design format 5, and reads record formats 1 to 5 and design formats 1 to 5, each under its own rules. A design document before format 5 holds no `context` member and declares no context block, and a record before format 5 holds no `context` in its `plan` item; replay compares such a plan item as though its `context` were null. A record before format 5 holds `snapshot` in a call item in place of the provenance profile's `provider`, `model_requested`, `model_reported`, `revision`, `runtime` and `claimed`, `precision` only as an integer or null, and no `request_id`, `calibrated`, `missing` or `clients` in a `calls` entry, nor any of these, nor the profile's `provider`, `model_requested`, `model_reported`, `revision`, `runtime` or `claimed`, as an `invalid` key; from format 5 `snapshot` is neither a call item's member nor an `invalid` key. A format-1 design document holds no extraction question or `boundaries` roles of one, and a format-1 record no extraction answer and no `truncated` in its `unhashed`. A format-1 or format-2 design document holds no described label, and a format-1 or format-2 record no `unsupported` cause, no call item `precision`, no `warnings` in a `calls` entry, no `warnings` or `precision` as an `invalid` key, and no document `hash`. A record before format 4 holds no entry with a `timed-out` or `raised` marker whose call item is recorded `returned`. A record's cause codes are read against its own format's set. A design document loads to the hash of its own bytes, so a design of an earlier format keeps its hash and replays its records. The record's `design` item also carries the helper version, 2, the version that has `extract`; replay reports a different one as `helpers-mismatch` and runs under the loaded helpers.

## The record document

From format 3 the record document is `{"hash", "log", "unhashed"}`, where `hash` is the record hash over the log, derived from the log's bytes when the record is written. A reader requires it, recomputes it and refuses a mismatch, and refuses any other member. It is never read as truth. It catches an edit made without recomputing it. It does not catch an adversary, whom only a turn id held outside the record catches. A job that edits a record's log recomputes the hash. The redacted view carries `hash` as an open value.

An exception's `traceback` in `unhashed` is CPython's text. It is built as `traceback.TracebackException` with its source lines looked up, its frames edited, then formatted. Along the chain and each exception group, a stack drops its leading run of frames of `ommat`, `asyncio` or a module under either, so its first frame is the component's. A stack made only of such frames, as a builtin component's is, keeps them all. Each remaining frame's filename becomes the frame's `__name__` global when that is a string, else the text after its last `/` or `\`; a pseudo-file such as `<string>` is kept. The same rule holds for a rule, a function `when`, the policy, the codec and a binding. This does not make a raw record shareable: the redacted view is the form to share.

## The binding protocol

Interpret calls a binding as `binding(batch, seconds=s)`: `s` is a positive `float`, the smaller of the batch's timeout and the time left to the deadline, read on the monotonic clock as the call starts, and the engine's timeout ends the call at that same instant. A binding converts it to its provider's deadline once and invents no budget of its own. A binding that does not take the keyword raises `TypeError` at its first call, so the call is `Failed` with `raised` on every ref of the batch. The fixture binding and `from_record` take it and ignore it.

A binding receives a batch's sources in source id order, and each source's refs and questions in the code-point order of their refs. That order is part of the contract: a provider may answer a question differently under another order or beside other questions, so the order is part of what ran, as the wording is. A binding for such a provider may ask one question per request or frame its own order, and names that method in `elicitation`.

Each round, the ready sources that name one oracle form one batch, sent to that oracle's binding in one call; a source that names none is a batch of its own, sent to `default`. A round's calls run concurrently, so a binding bound under several names, or as `default`, is called concurrently with itself. A batch's timeout is the largest its sources declare, else the run settings' `timeout`, 60 seconds by default. Decide refuses with `EntryRefusal`, before any record is written, a `bindings` that lacks an oracle a source names, lacks `default` when a source names none, or holds a key other than `default` that no source names.

Interpret tests what a call returns at the write. The call is `Failed`, and every ref of the batch `Unavailable` with its code, for the first of these that applies: `timed-out` when the call has not returned or raised before its end, whatever it then returns or raises; `raised` for a raise; a returned `Failed`'s code when it is `raised`, `timed-out`, `invalid-return` or `unexpected-ref`, and `invalid-return` for any other `Failed`, for a return that is neither exactly a `CallResult` nor exactly a `Failed`, and for a `values` that is not exactly a `dict` or holds two keys of one string; `unexpected-ref` for a key the batch does not provide; and `invalid-return` for a provenance member or `precision` out of its form. Otherwise each ref is written from its value: a ref left out is `Unavailable` with `not-returned`; a `q:` value off its kind's form, or a span past its text, is `invalid-answer`; a `t:` value its type does not hold, or a candidate set's that repeats a member, is `invalid-return`; and a marker that is not exactly one of the six a binding may return is `invalid-return`. A binding that cannot use a provider's reply returns the code interpret would write: `invalid-answer` for a `q:` ref, `invalid-return` for a `t:` ref.

A `choice` or `score` label is a name or exactly `{"name": n, "description": d}`. A `noul` declares no labels, or exactly `yes` then `no` in that form with at least one described, from format 4; its answer is still a probability for each. Both are strings; the description holds a character that is not whitespace and has no slots. Names are distinct; descriptions may repeat. A candidate-valued set's `fixed` part takes the same form. `extraction` declares no labels. Load's count bound counts labels; its code-point bound sums names and descriptions. The answer grid, the lints, load's `boundaries` checks and every answer read label names alone. The design document, the question's identity and the call item's `questions` hold the labels as declared, a candidate-valued set's as its `{"fixed", "candidates"}` object, so a description change makes a new question identity.

`RenderedQuestion.wording` is the rendered wording: a string, or for a structured wording an exact JSON object or array. `RenderedQuestion.labels` holds the names. `descriptions`, keyword-only, is aligned with them: `None` where a label is undescribed, `(None, None)` for an undescribed `noul`, `()` for `extraction`. A `RenderedQuestion` built with none gets `None` for each label. A candidate-valued question is rendered from its set's entry at each call, its set's members placed among its fixed part's names at `at`, each member's description `None`, so the labels of one `q:` ref may differ from call to call; a binding keeps no labels by ref. They are distinct and there is at least one; a binding whose provider takes fewer marks the ref `unsupported`. The set reaches the binding only as these labels, unless the source declares it a need.

`RenderedQuestion` and `SourceCall` check their fields when built, raising `TypeError` for a field of the wrong type and `ValueError` for a wrong value: `kind` and `value_type` a member or its string, stored as the member; labels `yes` and `no` for `noul` and none for `extraction`; `descriptions` as long as `labels`; an extraction question's `value_type` and `text` present and a probability kind's extraction members unset; and a source call's `refs` and `questions` not both non-empty. The checks read types, lengths and the kind's members only. `CallResult` checks nothing; interpret tests it at the write.

`CallResult.warnings` conforms when it is exactly a `tuple` of exact `str`, each holding a character that is not whitespace and in the value domain, at most 2^8 of them and 2^16 code points in all; order is kept and a repeat is allowed. Interpret tests it on every `CallResult`, a structural fault included. A conforming one is written under the call's `unhashed.calls` entry as `warnings` only when non-empty. A non-conforming one is never a fault: nothing is written and its repr goes under `invalid["warnings"]`. A late result keeps none. `redact` digests the list as one value.

`CallResult.precision` says how the provider rounds probabilities: an integer from 0 to 17, `bool` excluded, the decimal places; the string `unrounded` when it does not round; exactly `{"step": s}`, `s` a string of `0.` then at most 17 digits ending in one from 1 to 9 and no power of ten, for a step no count of places gives, such as `"0.05"`; or `None` when the binding does not know, with a reason (see [The provenance profile](#the-provenance-profile)). Any other value is `invalid-return` for the whole call, with `invalid["precision"]`. It is hashed on the call item, present on every call item from format 3, null on a failed one; `unrounded` and the step are format 5's. It is the provider's declaration, recorded and unchecked: the engine does not test that probabilities are rounded to it, and rounds none. A `noul` whose provider gives one probability holds `yes` as given and `no` as the binding's provider library derives it. The fixture binding returns `None`, as it returns no provenance and no `truncated` or `warnings`.

A binding that will not put a question to any provider, because its ref, kind or shape is outside what the provider takes, returns `Unavailable` with the code `unsupported` for that ref. A binding that refuses a whole batch marks every ref. Interpret writes the marker with a cause naming that ref; the cause carries no text. The binding gives its reason as a line of `warnings`. The call is `returned` and charged, and its call item holds null provenance where no provider was called. `unsupported` is one of the six markers a binding may return, with `refused`, `invalid-answer`, `invalid-return`, `timed-out` and `raised`. It is never a `Failed` outcome's code (a `Failed` carrying it is `invalid-return`), never a plan's cause and never the guard's. A rule that returns the marker it read provides it unchanged, so `unsupported` stands on a `d:` entry with its refs as any binding code does. Replay's fixture returns it as recorded.

When a binding sends a batch as several provider requests and one ends with no answer, each ref that request carried is `Unavailable` with `timed-out` when the request ran out of the call's `seconds`, or with `raised` when it failed any other way, and the other refs stand; the request's status and message go in lines of `warnings`. The call is `returned` and charged, and its provenance, `attempts`, `truncated` and `warnings` are written as for any returned call. A `timed-out` or `raised` on a ref under a `returned` call is the binding's provider request; `Failed` with `timed-out` on the call is the engine's timeout. Replay's fixture returns either marker as recorded; `from_record` returns it as `unsupported`.

A need whose value is a `dict` of exactly `media_type`, a `str` naming an image media type such as `image/png`, and `data`, the image's `bytes`, is an image, declared as `record(media_type=string, data=bytes_)`; a binding whose provider takes images sends it as one.

`from_record(record, loaded)` returns an oracle binding that answers a caller's live turn from a stored record, a `Record` or a document read through `read_record`; a `loaded` that is not exactly a `LoadedDesign` is an `EntryRefusal`. Bound under the oracle names whose sources the record should answer, beside live bindings under the rest, it answers each source from the returned call item that lists it and each ref from the entry that call wrote, in the binding's form. It refuses with `unsupported`: every ref of a source the record holds no returned call for, or whose `needs` differ by canonical bytes from the record's entries at the same refs, `t:state` included; a question whose kind, labels as declared, value type or list flag differ from the call item's `questions` entry; a ref with no stored entry naming its call; and a ref whose recorded marker is not `invalid-answer` or `invalid-return`, which come back as recorded, so a recorded `refused` comes back `unsupported`. Each refusal is a line of `warnings`. It has no provenance, its `usage`, `attempts`, `request_id`, `calibrated`, `missing` and `clients` are null and `truncated` is empty; its first `warnings` line is `answered from record <hash>`, and its lines are bounded by `bounded_warnings`. Nothing in the hashed record tells these call items from live ones. The binding cannot check a question's identity or wording: a `SourceCall` carries no identity and the call item holds no wording. Under the stored record's design neither can differ. Under another design the caller compares, after the turn, each new call item's `questions` identities with the stored call item's, by `q:` ref.

`ommat.bindings` holds the binding kit, what a binding author calls, never imported by `import ommat`. The names its `__all__` lists are public, as are those `ommat.__all__`, `ommat.types.__all__`, `ommat.helpers.__all__` and `ommat.evaluation.__all__` list: removing or changing one needs one minor release of `DeprecationWarning` first. `ommat.bindings.conformance` and the bindings ommat ships are outside the promise. `OracleBinding` is the type of a binding, `async def binding(batch, *, seconds)`, which `ommat.bindings.idia.oracle_binding` returns. `extraction_value(value, guess, p_null, confidence, span)` returns an extraction answer's five-member `dict`, each `date`, `time`, `datetime` or `timedelta` in `value` or `guess`, or in a list of either, written in the grammar below by its exact type, a tuple as a list and `span` as lists; it raises `ValueError` for a negative duration, a duration with a fraction of a second, an offset that is not a whole number of minutes or an aware value with no offset, and checks nothing else. A zero offset is written `Z`, a fraction with six digits, a duration with days as `nD` and `PT0S` for zero. `bounded_warnings(lines)` returns a conforming `warnings` from any lines of `str`: each surrogate escaped as the ledger escapes one, an empty line or one of whitespace alone left out, the order kept, and past either bound, counted after the escapes, a last line `<n> more lines left out` (`1 more line left out` for one). `json_value(value)` returns a need's value in record form: each `Answer` and `ExtractionAnswer` as its document, a tuple as a list, each type matched exactly and any other value as given; it raises `TypeError` for `bytes` at any depth, which a JSON part cannot carry. `image_need(value)` returns the `(media_type, data)` pair of an image need, each member's type matched exactly, and `None` for any other value. `prompt_text(prompt)` returns a `str` or `None` as given, and a mapping as `json.dumps(prompt, sort_keys=True)` with its other defaults, nested mappings as objects and sequences in order; any other value, within a mapping or in place of one, is a `TypeError`. `call_result(values, requests, *, precision, warnings=())` returns the `CallResult` of a call sent as `requests`, each a `RequestProvenance(*, provider=None, model_requested=None, model_reported=None, revision=None, runtime=None, elicitation=None, prompt=None, claimed=(), usage=None, attempts=0, request_id=None, calibrated=None, missing=None, clients=None, truncated=())`, which checks nothing when built, read only from the requests whose `attempts` is above 0: each distinct `provider`, `model_requested`, `model_reported`, `revision`, `elicitation`, `prompt` and `request_id` one per line in the order first given, `None` when none has one; `runtime` and `clients` each merged, a package at two versions a `ValueError`; `claimed` the union, sorted; `calibrated` false when any request that holds one says false, true when every one that holds one says true, else `None`; `missing` a detail's reason when every request gives one for it and the result holds no value for it, the first reason other than `not-sent`, else `not-sent`; `usage` summed per key over the requests that report it, `None` when none does; `attempts` summed; `truncated` sorted and distinct; `warnings` as given. When no request made an attempt, the result has no provenance and `attempts`, `precision` and `missing` are `None`.

`ommat.bindings.conformance.check(binding, *, batches=None, deterministic=False)` holds a binding to this protocol: it calls the binding with each of `batches`, or with its own, a source of each answer kind, one needing an image, and a `t:` source, each alone, then one question asked over three label sets as a candidate-valued question is rendered, its candidates after or before its fixed labels, then all the sources in one batch, and returns its failures as strings, each naming its check (`seconds`, `returns`, `values`, `markers`, `answers`, `per-ref-cause`, `warnings`, `precision`, `provenance`, `unsupported-provenance`, `deterministic`); an empty list passes. `answers` judges each `q:` value by interpret's own test; `per-ref-cause` asks a `warnings` line beside a `timed-out` or `raised` ref of a binding whose `attempts` is above 0, and of none other; `provenance` asks each detail of the provenance profile its form, a value or a reason for each detail of a result whose `attempts` is above 0, never both of any result, no provenance in one whose `attempts` is null or 0, and `claimed` only over details holding a value; `deterministic` compares two calls' results but for `request_id`, which a provider gives each request afresh. It runs its own event loop, and raises `RuntimeError` when called inside a running one. `from_record`, the fixture binding and the tutorial's binding pass it.

## The provenance profile

Each call item states who answered and how, the details idia's request outcome states too. The hashed details, on the call item from format 5: `provider`, the provider's name, a provider library's registered name where one names it; `model_requested` and `model_reported`, the model the binding asked for and the one the provider said answered; `revision`, the exact version of the model's weights or service, pinned or resolved; `runtime`, the code that ran the model, an object from package to version; `elicitation`; `precision`; and `prompt`. Beside them `claimed` lists, by name in code-point order, the details the source asserts and cannot verify, each holding a value. The details kept in the call's `calls` entry, outside every hash: `usage.input_tokens`, `usage.output_tokens` and `usage.passes`, `request_id`, the provider's id for the request, and `calibrated`, its claim that its probabilities are calibrated. `clients`, the versions of the libraries that made the call, sits there too and is no detail.

A call that sent a request reports `attempts` above 0, and each detail it leaves null has a reason in its `calls` entry's `missing`, unless the call failed: `not-reported`, the provider or its runtime gives none; `not-pinned`, no fixed version was asked for or resolved; `not-sent`, the request failed, whether it was sent or not; `hidden`, the provider's service withholds it; `not-applicable`, the detail has no meaning for this source, as `runtime` for a wire API; `not-exposed`, a library between the provider and the binding has it but does not make it public. A detail with a value has no reason, and `attempts` and `claimed` never take one. A call that sent no request, `attempts` null or 0, has no provenance, its hashed details null and `claimed` empty, and owes no reason: a binding that called no model, the fixture binding and a `Failed` call hold it so. The conformance suite's `provenance` check holds a binding to these rules; interpret tests each member's form and writes what the binding gave.

Interpret tests them at the write. A hashed detail off its form, a string detail not null or a string of the value domain, a `runtime` not null or a non-empty object of strings, or a `claimed` not a tuple of distinct detail names each holding a value, fails the call with `invalid-return`; `claimed` is written sorted. A `request_id`, `calibrated`, `missing` or `clients` off its form fails nothing: it is not written, and its repr goes under `invalid`. A `missing` that gives a reason for a detail holding a value is off its form. A `Failed` call holds no provenance and no `missing`; its `calls` entry keeps `usage`, `attempts`, `request_id`, `calibrated` and `clients` as any call's does. The redacted view digests `prompt` and `request_id`, which may echo an id the caller supplied, and keeps the other details open.

The calibration key, by which a calibration job outside the engine would group a question's answers against their gold answers, and which the engine neither builds nor stores, is the question identity, the provider, the model, the reported one else the requested, the revision, whether `model_reported`, else `model_requested`, is in `claimed`, and whether `revision` is, and the elicitation; the runtime moves scores but asks no new question, so it stays out of the key. A reader of a call item before format 5 takes a non-null `snapshot` as a claimed revision, with null provider and model and nothing else claimed.

## The idia binding

`ommat.bindings.idia`, installed by the `idia` extra and never imported by `import ommat`, binds an oracle to a provider through the idia library. `oracle_binding(provider)` takes an idia `Dispatcher` over one leaf and refuses, with `ValueError`, any other provider, a dispatcher whose re-ask is on and one whose overflow policy is not `Fail`, since a second answer or a trimmed state is not what the call item records, and a leaf whose declared probability decimals fall outside 0 to 17. A design that asks label kinds and extractions of different models binds one dispatcher per oracle.

Each call sets one idia deadline from `seconds`; seconds idia's deadline cannot hold send nothing, and every ref is `unsupported` with the reason as a line. Each source is one idia request, its questions in the order received, keyed by their refs, each label carrying its description. A source that asks an extraction sends the text its spans index as the state's first part, so a span indexes part 0, with its reference date in the request's options and its other needs not sent; a source that asks only label kinds sends its needs as one JSON part keyed by ommat's refs, never renamed. A source that asks both sends the extraction's state, so its label questions see the text alone. An image need follows as an image part. A described `noul` is sent with idia's `yes` and `no` labels and their descriptions, an undescribed one with none; a structured wording is sent as idia's JSON instruction, each array a tuple. A question whose described `noul` or structured wording the provider's capabilities do not carry, `carries_noul_descriptions` or `carries_structured_instructions` false, is `unsupported` alone before any call, with a line naming the form. A question idia's `Question` refuses, a `choice` rendered with fewer than two labels among them, is `unsupported` alone, with its reason as a line, and the source's other questions are sent; a source left with none sends no request. These are `unsupported`, each with its reason as a line: a `t:` ref; a source whose extractions read different texts or name different reference dates; a need a JSON part cannot carry; a question idia cannot build; and a request idia's preflight refuses. The sources sent run concurrently.

A `noul` answer is `yes` as the provider gave it and `no` as idia's `noul_complement` derives it; a `choice` or `score` answer is its probabilities by name, idia's chosen label and confidence left out, so a tie at the top abstains in the choice helpers where the provider named one; a refusal is `refused`; a fault is `invalid-answer` with a line naming its rules; an extraction is written through `extraction_value`, and a value it refuses or a span into a part other than the text is `invalid-answer` with a line. Each ref of a wire request that did not answer is `timed-out` when its error is idia's `DeadlineExceeded`, the request the call's time could not hold, else `raised`, with the request's status and message as a line; the other refs stand. Each outcome's `provider`, `model_requested`, `model_reported`, `elicitation`, `calibrated`, `request_id` and `usage` go under the same names, its `snapshot` as `revision`, its `versions` as `runtime`, its `prompt_sent` as `prompt` and its `client_versions` as `clients`, an empty mapping as null, and its `unreported` reasons and `claimed` under those names, never a reason idia did not give; a prompt the leaf built as a mapping is written through `prompt_text`, as JSON with its keys sorted and its lists in order, the form idia's recorded log keeps, which for this binding is the order sent, since it receives refs in code-point order and frames none. `usage` sums each outcome's input and output tokens and passes, `attempts` each outcome's attempts, `truncated` holds the refs of an outcome the provider cut short, and `precision` is the answering leaf's rounding, its probability decimals or `unrounded` for `None`, and `None` when no request made an attempt; a package at two versions across the outcomes fails the call `invalid-return`; each outcome is one `RequestProvenance`, folded by `call_result`. Every line goes through `bounded_warnings`, each need through `json_value` and each image need through `image_need`.

Importing it without idia installed raises `ModuleNotFoundError` naming the extra; any other import failure propagates.

In the binding's source, each site that writes a marker, rounds or raises carries a `# spec: <section>` comment naming the section of this spec that states the rule it keeps, its heading as written.

## The candidate-valued label set

A `choice` may declare its labels as `{"fixed": [...], "candidates": ref, "at": k}`: a fixed part, possibly empty, in the form of any label list, the ref of a candidate set, and `at`, how many fixed labels come before the candidates, an integer from 0 to the fixed part's length. A `Design` that leaves `at` out puts the candidates last; the document always writes it, and the question's identity hashes it. A candidate set is a source's `t:` ref declared `{"type": list<string>, "candidates": true}`, or the `d:` ref of a rule declared with `candidates=True` whose `outputs` is `list<string>`. Load refuses a label set naming any other ref, a candidate set of any other type, and a candidate set of records. A set's members are distinct by canonical bytes: a value that repeats one is `Unavailable` with `invalid-return`, naming the set's ref for a source's set and the rule for a rule's, and is never merged. The asking source has an edge from the set's provider; the set is among its needs only when it declares it.

After the source's own need, requirement and `when` markers, each candidate-valued question takes the first of these that applies, naming the set's ref, with `call` null, and is left out of its batch: a marker on the set, `Unavailable` with `need-unavailable` or `Unasked` with `need-undetermined`; an empty set, `Unasked` with `when-false`; rendered labels past load's bounds, more than 2^18 names or more than 2^24 code points of names and the fixed part's descriptions, `Unavailable` with `label-overflow`; and a member equal to a fixed name, `Unavailable` with `label-collision`. The source's other refs are asked. A source left with no ref to ask is not called and not charged. When the budget or the deadline cuts the round, a question left out keeps its own marker and the source's other refs take the cut's. A key a binding returns for a question left out is `unexpected-ref`.

Otherwise the question is asked over its labels in the declared order, the first `at` fixed names, then the set's members in the set's order, then the rest of the fixed names, and its answer is validated against them. Its identity hashes the fixed part and the set's ref, never the members. Replay's `question-changed` names it when the rerun's set entry holds members, in order, other than the answering record's. `from_record` rebuilds a stored question's labels from its fixed part and the set's entry in the stored record, and refuses the question when that entry is missing or a marker. In the redacted view, each label and `probabilities` key of its answer that is not a fixed name takes the set's class; a set digested whole leaves an opened answer showing how many members it held. The dead-question lint answers it over the labels each run renders, each fixed label on top and then a member, and never reports it on the grid.

## A structured wording

A question's wording may be `Structured(template, slots)`, its template a JSON object or array in the value domain, from format 4. Each string value is a template under the same rendering and escape rules as a `Text`'s, from the one slot list: each placeholder names a slot, each slot is in some string value, and a literal brace is doubled. Keys and other values are literal and never rendered, so the shape is the design's. Some key or value holds a character that is not whitespace outside its placeholders. The document writes `{"template": ..., "slots": [...]}` as for a `Text`, the template's JSON type telling the two apart, and the question's identity hashes it as written. An object is rendered and sent with its members in the canonical order of their keys, so a design and its document ask alike. A plan's `reason` and `reply` stay plain string templates.

## The extraction kind

An extraction question declares, beside its kind and wording, no labels, a `value_type` (`text`, `number`, `date`, `time`, `datetime` or `duration`), `is_list`, `text`, a board ref naming the string its spans index, and `reference_date`, a board ref or null naming the string a date written without a year is read against. Both refs become needs of the source and are exactly of type `string`; the question's identity covers the four members, never the values they name.

The binding returns, for the question's ref, exactly an object of `value`, `guess`, `p_null`, `confidence` and `span`, null where it has nothing. `value` and `guess` are null or a value of the type, or when `is_list` a list of such values with no null; `p_null` and `confidence` are null or a number in [0, 1]; `span` is null, a pair `[start, end]` of code-point offsets into the text, end exclusive, or one pair per list item, and locates `value`, or `guess` when `value` is null. A `number` is held as a float. A `date` is `YYYY-MM-DD`; a `time` is `HH:MM:SS`, an optional fraction and an optional `Z` or `±HH:MM`; a `datetime` is a date, `T` and a time; a `duration` is ISO 8601's `P` form without weeks or fractions. These grammars are the engine's own, with ASCII digits and uppercase `T` and `Z` only. Any other return is `invalid-answer` on that ref alone. A model's none is a null `value`, never a marker; [The choice helpers](#the-choice-helpers) say when `extract` abstains.

The binding may also report the refs whose response was cut short as `truncated`, which the record keeps outside every hash.

## Decide

A caller dispatches on `plan.kind`, never on the verdict alone: a `broken` plan has no steps, and the guard passes it with nothing to run.

A plan the policy returns is checked against seven plan rules. One it breaks is `broken`, with cause `invalid-return` naming `rule:N`, then `step:N` when the break is in a step:

1. A plan whose kind is not `execute` has no steps.
2. A step binds only results of earlier steps, each by a path that is null or in the binding grammar.
3. Every step names a tool the design declares, and no argument is named `idempotency_key`.
4. A step's board-ref origin is one of the policy's declared reads and holds a value on the board.
5. A plan's kind is not `broken`. A `clarify` or `inform` plan carries a `reply`, and no other kind carries one; an `escalate` plan carries a `reason`, which any other kind may carry. Each `reply` and `reason` holds a character that is not whitespace.
6. Every step requirement names a `d:` ref with no field path, that has a provider, and whose value its type can hold.
7. The plan's `reason` and `reply`, and every literal argument, argument name and requirement value, are in the value domain.

The plan-level rules are checked first, 1, 5, then 7 over `reason` and `reply`; then each step by position against 2, 3, 4, then 7, then 6. The cause names the first break only. A requirement on part of a rule's value, such as `d:plan.execute`, breaks rule 6: give the part its own rule and require that rule's ref.

A component that raises, a rule, a `when`, the policy or the codec, does not end the turn: its ref, or its source's refs, is `Unavailable` with `raised`, or the plan `broken`, and its exception's type, message and traceback are in `unhashed["exceptions"]`, keyed by the log index of the item it wrote.

A rule, a `when` and the policy do not change a value they read. A read returns the board's own value, not a copy, so a change reaches every later reader in the turn, while the record keeps the value as it was written.

A `-0.0` in a value written to the board, an answer's probability or member included, or in a plan's literal or requirement value, is held as `0.0`, as the record writes it, so a component reads the same value live and in replay.

A marker a rule returns is written with its cause when each of the cause's refs is a ref the design provides, with or without a field path, a source, rule or tool name, a parameter path, `policy`, `codec`, `rule:N` or `step:N`. Otherwise the rule's entry is `Unavailable` with `invalid-return` naming the rule, so a cause carries none of the turn's input.

Decide and replay refuse an async observer with `EntryRefusal`: an `async def` function or async generator function, a bound method or `functools.partial` of one, or an object whose class defines one as `__call__`. The test reads types and runs none of the observer's code. A sync observer that returns a coroutine is not caught, and what any observer returns is not read.

A cancellation of a running turn appends the `cause` item `cancelled`, closes the record, and propagates the `CancelledError` with the record as its `record`. To read `exc.record`, catch the `CancelledError` inside the task that awaits decide; a `CancelledError` that asyncio raises afresh carries no record. A cancellation requested from the observer takes effect at decide's next await. If the turn has none left, decide finishes, asyncio marks the finished task cancelled and raises a fresh `CancelledError`, and the decision is lost.

## The context block

A design may declare `context=Context(entries, plan=True, steps=True, record=True)`, what a turn hands its next reader, such as an LLM carrying on the conversation. With none, nothing is handed off. Each `ContextEntry(ref, text)` flags `ref`, which is `t:state` or a `d:` ref, with or without a field path, and words it with `text`, a `Text` whose slots name board refs or parameters, one of them `ref` as spelled. There is no reserved `{value}` slot and no size bound. The switches each default on: `plan` gives the plan's kind, `reply` and `reason`; `steps` its steps' tools and arguments, never their requirements; `record` the design's name and hash and the record hash.

Load refuses two entries flagging one ref (check 1); a slot ref with no provider (check 3); a flagged ref that is a `q:` ref, a `t:` ref other than `t:state`, or grounded, and a candidate slot (check 4); a parameter slot naming no leaf (check 8); and, at check 9, a template whose placeholders and slots do not match, one with nothing but whitespace outside its placeholders, a text with no slot naming its flagged ref, a path naming no field, and any slot whose ref's class tree holds `identifier` at the path or beneath it. Slots add no read and no edge to the graph.

Compile renders each entry over the board interpret left, before it calls the policy, and writes the block into the `plan` item's `context`: `{"design", "entries", "plan", "steps", "record"}`, the design's name or null, the entries in declared order and the switches as declared; `context` is null when the design declares none. An entry is `{"ref", "text", "marker"}`: the rendered text and null; or, when any board-ref slot reads a marker, null and that marker's `{"kind", "cause"}`, the flagged ref's if it reads one, else the first in slot order. The engine never words a marker. At a broken entry the block holds no entries. A rendered text is `content`, digested by `redact`, as is the design's name; a ref, a marker and the switches are `open`. Replay renders the block again as part of the plan item, so a changed text is a `plan` finding. `use_answers` compares a plan without its `context`.

An entry's text is the input's own: a slot over `t:state` or a source's `t:` ref carries what the user or a tool wrote, and the next reader receives it as written. So does a step's board-ref argument, which the handoff and the transcript give with its value; load's `identifier` refusal covers slots, not arguments.

## The handoff view

`handoff(record)` takes any closed `Record` and returns `None` when the log has no `plan` item or its `context` is null or absent. Otherwise it returns a plain dict of these members, in this order, each under its switch:

- `design`, `{"name", "hash"}`, and `record_hash`, under `record`;
- `plan`: `kind`, `reply` and `reason` as recorded, and `verdict`, the verdict's kind or null when the log holds no verdict, under `plan`; `steps` under `steps` when the verdict passed, a list, empty for a plan with none; left out when it would hold no member;
- `context`, the entries as recorded, under no switch.

A step is `{"tool", "arguments"}`, each argument tagged: a literal `{"value": v}`, a board ref `{"ref": r, "value": v}` with the value resolved from the record as `run_plan` resolves it, and a binding `{"step": n, "path": p}`. Under a refused verdict, or a log with no verdict, the plan keeps its kind and loses its steps, so a handoff never shows steps that will not run. It holds no guard failure, requirement, probability, span, provenance or version. It raises `ValueError` for a `record` that is not exactly a `Record`, and for a board-ref argument with no value in the log, which no record decide returned holds.

## The transcript view

`transcript(record)` takes any closed `Record` and returns `None` when the log has no `plan` item. Otherwise it returns a plain dict of these members, in this order:

- `record_hash`;
- `design`, `{"name", "hash"}`: the context block's name, null when the `plan` item's `context` is null or, before format 5, absent, and the `design` item's hash;
- `rounds`: for each round with a call item, in log order, a list of calls, one per source of each call item, in the item's source order. A call is `{"id", "source", "asks", "outcome", "answers"}`: `asks` the source's refs whose entry names the call item, in code-point order; `outcome` as recorded; `answers` an object keyed by those refs, each entry `{"value": v}` as recorded or `{"marker": {"kind", "cause"}}`;
- `decide`, `{"id", "board", "plan", "verdict"}` and `context`: `board` every `d:` entry, keyed by ref in log order and tagged as an answer is; `plan` the item's `kind`, `reason`, `reply`, `cause` and `steps`, each step `{"tool", "arguments", "requirements"}`, its arguments tagged as the handoff tags them; `verdict` the item's `{"kind", "failures"}`, or null when the log holds none; `context` the block's entries, absent when the item's `context` is;
- `pending`: for an `execute` plan under a passed verdict with no binding argument, its steps, each `{"id", "tool", "arguments"}`, the arguments resolved from the record as `run_plan` resolves them; else empty. Run a plan with a binding with `run_plan`, which passes each step its idempotency key.

An id is `om_`, the record hash's first 24 hexadecimal digits, then `_c<n>` for the view's nth call, `_d` for decide, or `_s<n>` for step n, so it fits `[A-Za-z0-9_-]` and 40 characters, and one log always gives the same ids. A ref no call asked, such as one whose source's needs were markers, is in no round; its effect is on the board. The view holds no `t:state` entry, no provenance, prompt or question wording, and no `derivation`, `rows` or `slots`. Its values are the record's, content included: to share a turn, share its redacted view, from which no transcript is built. It raises `ValueError` for a `record` that is not exactly a `Record`, and for a step's board-ref argument with no value in the log.

## The conversation renderers

`ommat.conversation` is outside the compatibility promise and may change in any minor release. It takes nothing beyond the standard library. `ommat.conversation.pydantic_ai` takes Pydantic AI, which the `pydantic-ai` extra installs as `pydantic-ai-slim>=2.49`. `import ommat` imports neither. A renderer words nothing: beyond fixed labels, every sentence it gives is a plan's `reason` or `reply` or a context entry's text.

`handoff_text(handoff)` returns the handoff as lines of text, or `""` for `None` or a handoff that gives no line: `Plan: <kind>`; `Verdict: <kind>`, `Reason: ...` and `Reply: ...`, each where not null; `Step <n>: <tool> <arguments>` for each step, its tagged arguments as canonical JSON; each entry's text, or `<ref>: <kind> (<cause code>)` for a marker entry, its kind as recorded, such as `Unsure`; `Design: <name> <hash>`, or `Design: <hash>` with no name; and `Record: <hash>`. A member the view leaves out gives no line. The caller places it: as Pydantic AI's `instructions`, a system or developer message, or, when a slot or a step argument reads free text, fenced as data in a user message.

`to_chat_completions(view)` returns a transcript as Chat Completions messages, or `[]` for `None`. For each round, an assistant message with null `content` and one tool call per call, `{"id", "type": "function", "function": {"name", "arguments"}}`, then one `{"role": "tool", "tool_call_id", "content"}` message per call; then an assistant message calling `decide` and its `tool` message; then, when `pending` holds steps, one assistant message calling them, with no result, for the caller to run and answer. A source call's arguments are `{"asks"}`, decide's `{"design"}`, the view's `{"name", "hash"}`, and a step's its resolved values. A source call's result is its `answers`, failed or not, and decide's is the view's `decide` without its `id`. Every arguments and content string is canonical JSON, so a record renders to the same bytes each time. The caller places the messages after its own user message for the turn.

`to_model_messages(view, *, model_name=None, provider_name=None)` returns the same calls and results as Pydantic AI messages, or `[]` for `None`: for each round a `ModelResponse` of `ToolCallPart`s, then a `ModelRequest` of `ToolReturnPart`s, a failed call's with `outcome="failed"`, which Pydantic AI sends as the provider's error flag, as Anthropic's and Bedrock's, or, for a provider with none, by wrapping the content as `{"error": ...}`; decide alike, its return's `metadata` `{"record_hash", "design_hash"}`; then, when `pending` holds steps, a `ModelResponse` of their calls. Arguments and content are dicts. Every `ModelResponse` carries `model_name` and `provider_name`; timestamps are Pydantic AI's. The source calls' and decide's messages, both the responses and the requests, carry `metadata` `{"ommat": "bookkeeping", "record_hash"}`, which Pydantic AI keeps and never sends to a model; the pending calls' response carries none, since those calls are real actions. An agent run with this history and no new prompt runs the pending calls with its own tools.

ommat's bookkeeping is the source calls and decide, with their results. `is_bookkeeping(message)` tells one, a Pydantic AI message by its mark and a Chat Completions message by its ids, a source call's or decide's, never a pending step's: an assistant message with null or empty `content` whose every tool call has one, or a `tool` message answering one; a Pydantic AI request that holds anything but tool returns is not bookkeeping. `drop_earlier_bookkeeping(messages)` returns the messages without each bookkeeping call message of a record other than the latest, the record of the last such message, and without each result answering one of their calls, wherever that result stands; no result leaves on its own mark. So the current turn's bookkeeping stays for the model to answer from, an earlier turn's leaves with each result beside its call, and a request that Pydantic AI merged loses only the results it holds for those calls. Each module has its own pair; the Pydantic AI one runs as a history processor, `Agent(capabilities=[ProcessHistory(drop_earlier_bookkeeping)])`, which also writes the processed history back into the run's messages.

A source's tool name is its name with each `/` as `-`, which no ommat name holds, and a pending step's is its tool as the design declares it, which must be the caller's tool's name. Both renderers raise `ValueError` for a source or pending tool named `decide`, a pending tool outside `[A-Za-z0-9_-]`, and a name over 64 characters.

## The choice helpers

`pick_with_margin(r, ref, bar, margin)` returns the top label of the answer at `ref` when its probability is at or above `bar` and leads the next by at least `margin`, else `Unsure` with `abstained` naming `ref`; `pick_with_thresholds(r, ref, thresholds, margin)` takes a bar per label. A tie at the top abstains, even at `margin` 0: no helper breaks a tie. A marker read is returned unchanged.

`extract(r, ref, null_bar)` returns `Unsure` with `abstained` naming `ref` when the extraction answer at `ref` has a `None` `value`, or a `p_null` at or above `null_bar`; otherwise it returns the `value`, as a list when the question asks for one. A `None` `p_null` never abstains, so `null_bar` bites only when the model gives a `p_null`. The result depends on `value` and `p_null` alone, never on `guess` or `confidence`: a rule that weighs confidence reads the answer whole. A `null_bar` that is not exactly an `int` or `float` in [0, 1] raises, before a marker read is returned unchanged, and a read holding any other value raises `TypeError`.

## The design lints

`dead_questions(loaded, *, values=None)` fills `t:state` and every ref a source provides, and runs decide at each point; `unsure_to_execute(loaded, *, values=None)` fills the policy's declared reads and calls the policy with each `d:` read set to `Unsure` in turn. Each takes `values`, a mapping from a read it fills to a list of that read's values, for a read its types and the answer grid cannot fill. `dead_questions` never takes a `d:` key, since rules compute those values, and `unsure_to_execute` never takes a `q:` key; a key naming a read the lint does not fill is a `ValueError` before any run.

## Replay

`design-mismatch` gives the recorded and replayed design hashes. A function component's identity includes its module and qualified name, so the usual cause is a component whose module was `__main__`, a design defined in the script that ran it. To find the member that differs, keep the design document beside each record and diff it against `loaded.document` with any JSON diff; a design document is canonical JSON, so the output is stable. A design that must live in a script keeps its document and is loaded elsewhere with `load(document, functions={...})`, keyed by each function component's key, a rule's name, `<source>.when` or `policy`, which keeps its hash.

`not-in-record` names a source the rerun asks that the record never called; `use_answers` reports the entries it would provide as `not-returned`. An `entry` finding compares the two entries whole, so it can differ in its derivation alone.

## Evaluation

`ommat.evaluation.use_answers(record, loaded, other)` reruns `record`'s turn under `loaded` with `other`'s call items and entries answering, `q:` and `t:` refs alike; `use_answers_sync` is its twin. `record` and `other` are each a `Record` or a stored document, read through `read_record`. It returns `{"other": h, "findings": [...]}`, `h` the other record's hash. The state entry, the run settings, the cut and every comparison are `record`'s, so the findings are replay's with `other` as the fixture: `not-in-record` and `ref-not-in-record` name what `other` does not hold, and `question-changed` compares against `other`'s identities. A `Failed` call or a returned marker in `other` comes back as replay returns a recorded one. Neither record is edited, and neither's `unhashed` is read.

After both records are read and before any replay it raises `EvaluationRefusal`, `reason` in this order: `invalid-record` when a document does not read, carrying the `InvalidRecord` as `error`; `unfinished` for a record with no `verdict` item, then `nothing-to-replay` for one with no state entry, then `wrong-design` for one whose design hash is not `loaded.design_hash`, each for `record` then `other`; and `different-turn` when the two state entries or `settings` items differ by canonical bytes. `turn` is the refused record's hash, `other`'s for `different-turn`, and `None` for `invalid-record`, which carries `error` instead; every other attribute is `None`. A `loaded` that is not exactly a `LoadedDesign` is an `EntryRefusal`, before either record is read.

A replay record, the `Record` that `replay` returns, is a valid `record` or `other`. To compare two models decided under different parameter sets, replay the second's record under the first's loaded design and pass the replay record as `other`; a threshold that makes the rerun ask a source the second never called shows as `not-in-record` in the replay's findings, and its entries come back `not-returned` in `use_answers`'s.

## Continuing a workflow

ommat has no resume within a turn. To continue a multi-step workflow, the caller runs the first steps itself, then decides a new turn whose state carries the steps' results and the prior turn's record hash, with those fields declared in the state type. The prior turn's hash is the caller's claim, and ommat does not check it. ommat does not record the steps' results except as the new turn's state. The new turn replays without the earlier one. `run_plan`'s idempotency keys follow the new turn's record. `run_plan` has no option to stop after a step, so the first steps run on the caller's own runner.
