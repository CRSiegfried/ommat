# Spec

The normative description of ommat's behavior. The Python package is one implementation of it. JSON conformance fixtures that any implementation, in any language, must pass come later, and the spec will then carry its own version, separate from the package version.

The spec covers what record format 7 and design format 9 hold and the engine does:

- loading a design, and the load checks that refuse an invalid one;
- decide: the turn's entry with the codec, the broken entry, interpret over rounds of sources and rules, functions or tables, the three markers and their causes, and compile with a function policy or the policy table, the plan rules and the guard;
- source requirements: a source asked only when an estimate holds one of its values, the estimate not sent unless it is also a need;
- the ledger and the observer, the record with its canonical JSON encoding, order objects and hashes, the ordered encoding a model reads, and reading a stored record back;
- the targets a design declares, the properties its questions ask about, bear on and estimate, for a reader;
- the context block a design declares, rendered into the record, and the record views: the escalation view, the handoff view, the transcript view and the redacted view;
- the conversation renderers, which render a handoff as text and a transcript as Chat Completions or Pydantic AI messages;
- the record lens, which renders stored records and designs as an HTML page a person reads;
- replay of a recorded turn with no model call, the evaluation job `use_answers`, and the design store and the evaluation store;
- the binding protocol between the engine and a model adapter, the provenance profile each call states, the binding kit and the binding conformance suite;
- the answer kinds: `noul`, `choice` and `score`, which give a probability to each label, and `extraction`, a typed value read from a text, with the probability that the text holds none, the model's reading whatever it answered, its confidence and the span it read;
- the candidate-valued label set: a `choice` whose labels are a fixed part and the members of a `list<string>` candidate set, rendered each time it is asked;
- per-candidate questions: a source grounded over a `list<string>` candidate set asks a question once per candidate, beside its questions asked once;
- the helpers: the choice helpers, `extract` and the per-candidate picks among them, `determined` and the combinators rules call, and the reference runner that runs a plan's steps;
- the live frontier, the answer grid and the `boundaries` fields, and the design lints that read them;
- decision tables: rule tables under every hit policy, data rules among them, and the policy table with its plan declarations and their declared text.

Outside it, and refused by load in a design that declares one: a grounded declaration that is itself a candidate set; a per-candidate extraction question and a per-candidate question whose labels are a candidate-valued set; and a need on a grounded declared ref. Also outside it: the run-time stopping certificate, multi-turn continuation, and every evaluation job but `use_answers`.

The sections below are written; the rest is not yet.

## Formats

A record and a design document each carry a format number, each its own, which changes only when that document's rules change. This engine writes record format 7 and design format 9, and reads record formats 1 to 7 and design formats 1 to 9, each under its own rules. A design document before format 9 holds no `known` cell, which load refuses (see [Decision tables](#decision-tables)). A design document before format 8 holds no candidate field's name in a `candidates` member, which load refuses, and a record before format 7 holds no `sets` in its `settings` item, which the reader refuses, while from format 7 the `settings` item requires it (see [The candidate-valued label set](#the-candidate-valued-label-set)). A design document before format 7 holds no `targets`, no question's `target` or `latent_targets` and no rule's `target`, which load refuses (see [Targets](#targets)). A design document before format 6 spells a record type's fields in code-point order, which load holds it to, and holds no order member, which load refuses; a record before format 6 holds no `order` in any item, which the reader refuses, and from format 6 each `entry`, `plan` and `verdict` item requires one. A record before format 6 holds no `grounded` in its `settings` item and no grounded ref, which the reader refuses, and from format 6 the `settings` item requires `grounded` (see [Per-candidate questions](#per-candidate-questions)). Replay compares an item of a record before format 6 without the replay's `order`. A design built in Python is written at the engine's design format, so it has a new hash when that format changes, and a stored document keeps its own. A design document before format 5 holds no `context` member and declares no context block, and a record before format 5 holds no `context` in its `plan` item; replay compares such a plan item as though its `context` were null. A record before format 5 holds `snapshot` in a call item in place of the provenance profile's `provider`, `model_requested`, `model_reported`, `revision`, `runtime` and `claimed`, `precision` only as an integer or null, and no `request_id`, `calibrated`, `missing` or `clients` in a `calls` entry, nor any of these, nor the profile's `provider`, `model_requested`, `model_reported`, `revision`, `runtime` or `claimed`, as an `invalid` key; from format 5 `snapshot` is neither a call item's member nor an `invalid` key. A format-1 design document holds no extraction question or `boundaries` roles of one, and a format-1 record no extraction answer and no `truncated` in its `unhashed`. A format-1 or format-2 design document holds no described label, and a format-1 or format-2 record no `unsupported` cause, no call item `precision`, no `warnings` in a `calls` entry, no `warnings` or `precision` as an `invalid` key, and no document `hash`. A record before format 4 holds no entry with a `timed-out` or `raised` marker whose call item is recorded `returned`. A record's cause codes are read against its own format's set. A design document loads to the hash of its own bytes, so a design of an earlier format keeps its hash and replays its records. The record's `design` item also carries the helper version, which [the choice helpers](#the-choice-helpers) give.

From format 6 a design document holds each object's order where a model may read it: a source's `payload_order`, the order object of its `payload`; a parameter leaf's `order`, the order object of its `value`; and a structured wording's `order`, the order object of its template, written only when the template is not in canonical order (see [A structured wording](#a-structured-wording)). Load refuses an order object that does not fit its value, and a loaded design holds each payload and each leaf's value in the order its order object gives, so a binding and `r.param` receive them as declared.

## The record document

From format 3 the record document is `{"hash", "log", "unhashed"}`, where `hash` is the record hash over the log, derived from the log's bytes when the record is written. A reader requires it, recomputes it and refuses a mismatch, and refuses any other member. It is never read as truth. It catches an edit made without recomputing it. It does not catch an adversary, whom only a turn id held outside the record catches. A job that edits a record's log recomputes the hash. The redacted view carries `hash` as an open value.

A model reads the order of an object's members, so key order is part of what the model sees, as the wording is: two orders of one state may draw two different answers. ommat keeps every object's order from where it is declared or built to the text a model reads, and chooses none. A record type's fields stand in the order declared, so `ommat.types.record(b=..., a=...)` spells `record{b: ..., a: ...}`, the design hash covering that order, and a record value's fields stand in its type's order. Any other object keeps the order its author or producer gave it. A record type conforms to one with the same fields in another order.

The canonical encoding is RFC 8785's JSON: each object's members sorted by key, no whitespace. Every hash is over it, so a hash stays interoperable. The ordered encoding writes a value as the canonical encoding does, but with each object's members in the order the value holds them: strings and numbers as RFC 8785 writes them, each key by its string data, lists in order, and no whitespace. It takes a value in the value domain, returns text, and raises `ValueError` where the canonical encoding does. It is the text a model reads.

An order object records the order of some data's objects beside its canonical bytes. It names each object whose members are not in the canonical key order: the object's index among the data's objects, counted from 0 in pre-order as the canonical encoding writes the data, as decimal text, mapped to its members' positions in the canonical key order, listed in the order held. It is `{}` when every object is in canonical order. It holds no key, so it reveals no content, and it grows with the data. The data rebuilt from an order object holds each object it names in that order and every other in the canonical key order; a reader refuses an order object that is not the order object of some ordering of its data. The order object enters the hash beside the data, so two orders are two designs or two records.

From format 6 each `entry`, `plan` and `verdict` item holds `order`, the order object of the item with its own members in canonical order. The ledger derives it at the append from the order each object is given in; no producer writes it. `read_record` takes each such item's `order` off and rebuilds the item in that order before the append, refusing an item with no `order` or one whose `order` does not fit, and the record hash refuses an `order` the append would not derive. `Record.log`, the observer and every other reader get each such item with its `order`, each object in the order it gives. `redact` derives each item's `order` afresh over the item as the redacted view writes it.

An exception's `traceback` in `unhashed` is CPython's text. It is built as `traceback.TracebackException` with its source lines looked up, its frames edited, then formatted. Along the chain and each exception group, a stack drops its leading run of frames of `ommat`, `asyncio` or a module under either, so its first frame is the component's. A stack made only of such frames, as a builtin component's is, keeps them all. Each remaining frame's filename becomes the frame's `__name__` global when that is a string, else the text after its last `/` or `\`; a pseudo-file such as `<string>` is kept. The same rule holds for a rule, a function `when`, the policy, the codec and a binding. This does not make a raw record shareable: the redacted view is the form to share.

## The binding protocol

Interpret calls a binding as `binding(batch, seconds=s)`: `s` is a positive `float`, the smaller of the batch's timeout and the time left to the deadline, read on the monotonic clock as the call starts, and the engine's timeout ends the call at that same instant. A binding converts it to its provider's deadline once and invents no budget of its own. A binding that does not take the keyword raises `TypeError` at its first call, so the call is `Failed` with `raised` on every ref of the batch. The fixture binding and `from_record` take it and ignore it.

A binding receives a batch's sources in source id order, and each source's refs and questions in the code-point order of their refs. `SourceCall.questions` keys a per-candidate question by each of its grounded refs, so its candidates stand in the code-point order of those refs, not the set's order (see [Per-candidate questions](#per-candidate-questions)). That order is part of the contract: a provider may answer a question differently under another order or beside other questions, so the order is part of what ran, as the wording is. A binding for such a provider may ask one question per request or frame its own order, and names that method in `elicitation`.

A source's `needs` is a fresh `dict` keyed by each need as declared, in code-point order. Each value keeps its own order: a record value's fields in its type's declared order, and any other object, a payload's included, in the order its producer gave it. A binding sends each object in the order it receives it, since the model reads that order. A design whose model needs its state in another order declares one record need, in that order, and has the binding send it whole, as the idia binding's `state` does.

Each round, the ready sources that name one oracle form one batch, sent to that oracle's binding in one call; a source that names none is a batch of its own, sent to `default`. A round's calls run concurrently, so a binding bound under several names, or as `default`, is called concurrently with itself. A batch's timeout is the largest its sources declare, else the run settings' `timeout`, 60 seconds by default. Decide refuses with `EntryRefusal`, before any record is written, a `bindings` that lacks an oracle a source names, lacks `default` when a source names none, or holds a key other than `default` that no source names.

Interpret tests what a call returns at the write. The call is `Failed`, and every ref of the batch `Unavailable` with its code, for the first of these that applies: `timed-out` when the call has not returned or raised before its end, whatever it then returns or raises; `raised` for a raise; a returned `Failed`'s code when it is `raised`, `timed-out`, `invalid-return` or `unexpected-ref`, and `invalid-return` for any other `Failed`, for a return that is neither exactly a `CallResult` nor exactly a `Failed`, and for a `values` that is not exactly a `dict` or holds two keys of one string; `unexpected-ref` for a key the batch does not provide; and `invalid-return` for a provenance member or `precision` out of its form. Otherwise each ref is written from its value: a ref left out is `Unavailable` with `not-returned`; a `q:` value off its kind's form, or a span past its text, is `invalid-answer`; a `t:` value its type does not hold, or a candidate set's that repeats a member, is `invalid-return`; and a marker that is not exactly one of the six a binding may return is `invalid-return`. A binding that cannot use a provider's reply returns the code interpret would write: `invalid-answer` for a `q:` ref, `invalid-return` for a `t:` ref.

A `choice` or `score` label is a name or exactly `{"name": n, "description": d}`. A `noul` declares no labels, or exactly `yes` then `no` in that form with at least one described, from format 4; its answer is still a probability for each. Both are strings; the description holds a character that is not whitespace and has no slots. Names are distinct; descriptions may repeat. A candidate-valued set's `fixed` part takes the same form. `extraction` declares no labels. Load's count bound counts labels; its code-point bound sums names and descriptions. The answer grid, the lints, load's `boundaries` checks and every answer read label names alone. The design document, the question's identity and the call item's `questions` hold the labels as declared, a candidate-valued set's as its `{"fixed", "candidates"}` object, so a description change makes a new question identity.

`RenderedQuestion.wording` is the rendered wording: a string, or for a structured wording an exact JSON object or array. `RenderedQuestion.labels` holds the names. `descriptions`, keyword-only, is aligned with them: `None` where a label is undescribed, `(None, None)` for an undescribed `noul`, `()` for `extraction`. A `RenderedQuestion` built with none gets `None` for each label. A candidate-valued question is rendered from its set's entry at each call, its set's members placed among its fixed part's names at `at`, each member's description `None`, so the labels of one `q:` ref may differ from call to call; a binding keeps no labels by ref. They are distinct and there is at least one; a binding whose provider takes fewer marks the ref `unsupported`. The set reaches the binding only as these labels, unless the source declares it a need.

`RenderedQuestion` and `SourceCall` check their fields when built, raising `TypeError` for a field of the wrong type and `ValueError` for a wrong value: `kind` and `value_type` a member or its string, stored as the member; labels `yes` and `no` for `noul` and none for `extraction`; `descriptions` as long as `labels`; an extraction question's `value_type` and `text` present and a probability kind's extraction members unset; and a source call's `refs` and `questions` not both non-empty. The checks read types, lengths and the kind's members only. `CallResult` checks nothing; interpret tests it at the write.

`CallResult.warnings` conforms when it is exactly a `tuple` of exact `str`, each holding a character that is not whitespace and in the value domain, at most 2^8 of them and 2^16 code points in all; order is kept and a repeat is allowed. Interpret tests it on every `CallResult`, a structural fault included. A conforming one is written under the call's `unhashed.calls` entry as `warnings` only when non-empty. A non-conforming one is never a fault: nothing is written and its repr goes under `invalid["warnings"]`. A late result keeps none. `redact` digests the list as one value.

`CallResult.precision` says how the provider rounds probabilities: an integer from 0 to 17, `bool` excluded, the decimal places; the string `unrounded` when it does not round; exactly `{"step": s}`, `s` a string of `0.` then at most 17 digits ending in one from 1 to 9 and no power of ten, for a step no count of places gives, such as `"0.05"`; or `None` when the binding does not know, with a reason (see [The provenance profile](#the-provenance-profile)). Any other value is `invalid-return` for the whole call, with `invalid["precision"]`. It is hashed on the call item, present on every call item from format 3, null on a failed one; `unrounded` and the step are format 5's. It is the provider's declaration, recorded and unchecked: the engine does not test that probabilities are rounded to it, and rounds none. A `noul` whose provider gives one probability holds `yes` as given and `no` as the binding's provider library derives it. The fixture binding returns `None`, as it returns no provenance and no `truncated` or `warnings`.

A binding that will not put a question to any provider, because its ref, kind or shape is outside what the provider takes, returns `Unavailable` with the code `unsupported` for that ref. A binding that refuses a whole batch marks every ref. Interpret writes the marker with a cause naming that ref; the cause carries no text. The binding gives its reason as a line of `warnings`. The call is `returned` and charged, and its call item holds null provenance where no provider was called. `unsupported` is one of the six markers a binding may return, with `refused`, `invalid-answer`, `invalid-return`, `timed-out` and `raised`. It is never a `Failed` outcome's code (a `Failed` carrying it is `invalid-return`), never a plan's cause and never the guard's. A rule that returns the marker it read provides it unchanged, so `unsupported` stands on a `d:` entry with its refs as any binding code does. Replay's fixture returns it as recorded.

When a binding sends a batch as several provider requests and one ends with no answer, each ref that request carried is `Unavailable` with `timed-out` when the request ran out of the call's `seconds`, or with `raised` when it failed any other way, and the other refs stand; the request's status and message go in lines of `warnings`. The call is `returned` and charged, and its provenance, `attempts`, `truncated` and `warnings` are written as for any returned call. A `timed-out` or `raised` on a ref under a `returned` call is the binding's provider request; `Failed` with `timed-out` on the call is the engine's timeout. Replay's fixture returns either marker as recorded; `from_record` returns it as `unsupported`.

A need whose value is a `dict` of exactly `media_type`, a `str` naming an image media type such as `image/png`, and `data`, the image's `bytes`, is an image, declared as `record(media_type=string, data=bytes_)`; a binding whose provider takes images sends it as one.

`from_record(record, loaded)` returns an oracle binding that answers a caller's live turn from a stored record, a `Record` or a document read through `read_record`; a `loaded` that is not exactly a `LoadedDesign` is an `EntryRefusal`. Bound under the oracle names whose sources the record should answer, beside live bindings under the rest, it answers each source from the returned call item that lists it and each ref from the entry that call wrote, in the binding's form. It refuses with `unsupported`: every ref of a source the record holds no returned call for, or whose `needs` differ from the record's entries at the same refs, `t:state` included, by their ordered encoding, so a need in another order differs, or by canonical bytes for a record before format 6, which holds no order; a question whose kind, labels as declared, value type or list flag differ from the call item's `questions` entry, a per-candidate question's under its declared ref; a ref with no stored entry naming its call, a candidate the stored turn did not ask included; and a ref whose recorded marker is not `invalid-answer` or `invalid-return`, which come back as recorded, so a recorded `refused` comes back `unsupported`. Each refusal is a line of `warnings`. It has no provenance, its `usage`, `attempts`, `request_id`, `calibrated`, `missing` and `clients` are null and `truncated` is empty; its first `warnings` line is `answered from record <hash>`, and its lines are bounded by `bounded_warnings`. Nothing in the hashed record tells these call items from live ones. The binding cannot check a question's identity or wording: a `SourceCall` carries no identity and the call item holds no wording. Under the stored record's design neither can differ. Under another design the caller compares, after the turn, each new call item's `questions` identities with the stored call item's, by `q:` ref.

`ommat.bindings` holds the binding kit, what a binding author calls, never imported by `import ommat`. The names its `__all__` lists are public, as are those `ommat.__all__`, `ommat.types.__all__`, `ommat.helpers.__all__` and `ommat.evaluation.__all__` list: removing or changing one needs one minor release of `DeprecationWarning` first. `ommat.bindings.conformance` and the bindings ommat ships are outside the promise. `OracleBinding` is the type of a binding, `async def binding(batch, *, seconds)`, which `ommat.bindings.idia.oracle_binding` returns. `extraction_value(value, guess, p_null, confidence, span)` returns an extraction answer's five-member `dict`, each `date`, `time`, `datetime` or `timedelta` in `value` or `guess`, or in a list of either, written in the grammar below by its exact type, a tuple as a list and `span` as lists; it raises `ValueError` for a negative duration, a duration with a fraction of a second, an offset that is not a whole number of minutes or an aware value with no offset, and checks nothing else. A zero offset is written `Z`, a fraction with six digits, a duration with days as `nD` and `PT0S` for zero. `bounded_warnings(lines)` returns a conforming `warnings` from any lines of `str`: each surrogate escaped as the ledger escapes one, an empty line or one of whitespace alone left out, the order kept, and past either bound, counted after the escapes, a last line `<n> more lines left out` (`1 more line left out` for one). `json_value(value)` returns a need's value in record form: each `Answer` and `ExtractionAnswer` as its document, a tuple as a list, each type matched exactly and any other value as given; it raises `TypeError` for `bytes` at any depth, which a JSON part cannot carry. `image_need(value)` returns the `(media_type, data)` pair of an image need, each member's type matched exactly, and `None` for any other value. `prompt_text(prompt)` returns a `str` or `None` as given, and a mapping as `json.dumps(prompt)` with its defaults, each mapping's keys in the order given at every depth, sequences in order and a tuple written as a list, so the text keeps the order the model read; a key that is not a `str`, which JSON would write as one, and any other value, within a mapping or in place of one, is a `TypeError`. `call_result(values, requests, *, precision, warnings=())` returns the `CallResult` of a call sent as `requests`, each a `RequestProvenance(*, provider=None, model_requested=None, model_reported=None, revision=None, runtime=None, elicitation=None, prompt=None, claimed=(), usage=None, attempts=0, request_id=None, calibrated=None, missing=None, clients=None, truncated=())`, which checks nothing when built, read only from the requests whose `attempts` is above 0: each distinct `provider`, `model_requested`, `model_reported`, `revision`, `elicitation`, `prompt` and `request_id` one per line in the order first given, `None` when none has one; `runtime` and `clients` each merged, a package at two versions a `ValueError`; `claimed` the union, sorted; `calibrated` false when any request that holds one says false, true when every one that holds one says true, else `None`; `missing` a detail's reason when every request gives one for it and the result holds no value for it, the first reason other than `not-sent`, else `not-sent`; `usage` summed per key over the requests that report it, `None` when none does; `attempts` summed; `truncated` sorted and distinct; `warnings` as given. When no request made an attempt, the result has no provenance and `attempts`, `precision` and `missing` are `None`.

`ommat.bindings.conformance.check(binding, *, batches=None, deterministic=False)` holds a binding to this protocol: it calls the binding with each of `batches`, or with its own, a source of each answer kind, one needing an image, and a `t:` source, each alone, then one question asked over three label sets as a candidate-valued question is rendered, its candidates after or before its fixed labels, then a source asking a question per candidate beside one asked once, its candidates out of their grounded refs' code-point order and their refs holding escapes and a non-ASCII character, then all the sources in one batch, and returns its failures as strings, each naming its check (`seconds`, `returns`, `values`, `markers`, `answers`, `per-ref-cause`, `warnings`, `precision`, `provenance`, `unsupported-provenance`, `deterministic`); an empty list passes. `answers` judges each `q:` value by interpret's own test; `per-ref-cause` asks a `warnings` line beside a `timed-out` or `raised` ref of a binding whose `attempts` is above 0, and of none other; `provenance` asks each detail of the provenance profile its form, a value or a reason for each detail of a result whose `attempts` is above 0, never both of any result, no provenance in one whose `attempts` is null or 0, and `claimed` only over details holding a value; `deterministic` compares two calls' results but for `request_id`, which a provider gives each request afresh. It runs its own event loop, and raises `RuntimeError` when called inside a running one. `from_record`, the fixture binding and the tutorial's binding pass it.

## The provenance profile

Each call item states who answered and how, the details idia's request outcome states too. The hashed details, on the call item from format 5: `provider`, the provider's name, a provider library's registered name where one names it; `model_requested` and `model_reported`, the model the binding asked for and the one the provider said answered; `revision`, the exact version of the model's weights or service, pinned or resolved; `runtime`, the code that ran the model, an object from package to version; `elicitation`; `precision`; and `prompt`. Beside them `claimed` lists, by name in code-point order, the details the source asserts and cannot verify, each holding a value. The details kept in the call's `calls` entry, outside every hash: `usage.input_tokens`, `usage.output_tokens` and `usage.passes`, `request_id`, the provider's id for the request, and `calibrated`, its claim that its probabilities are calibrated. `clients`, the versions of the libraries that made the call, sits there too and is no detail.

A call that sent a request reports `attempts` above 0, and each detail it leaves null has a reason in its `calls` entry's `missing`, unless the call failed: `not-reported`, the provider or its runtime gives none; `not-pinned`, no fixed version was asked for or resolved; `not-sent`, the request failed, whether it was sent or not; `hidden`, the provider's service withholds it; `not-applicable`, the detail has no meaning for this source, as `runtime` for a wire API; `not-exposed`, a library between the provider and the binding has it but does not make it public. A detail with a value has no reason, and `attempts` and `claimed` never take one. A call that sent no request, `attempts` null or 0, has no provenance, its hashed details null and `claimed` empty, and owes no reason: a binding that called no model, the fixture binding and a `Failed` call hold it so. The conformance suite's `provenance` check holds a binding to these rules; interpret tests each member's form and writes what the binding gave.

Interpret tests them at the write. A hashed detail off its form, a string detail not null or a string of the value domain, a `runtime` not null or a non-empty object of strings, or a `claimed` not a tuple of distinct detail names each holding a value, fails the call with `invalid-return`; `claimed` is written sorted. A `request_id`, `calibrated`, `missing` or `clients` off its form fails nothing: it is not written, and its repr goes under `invalid`. A `missing` that gives a reason for a detail holding a value is off its form. A `Failed` call holds no provenance and no `missing`; its `calls` entry keeps `usage`, `attempts`, `request_id`, `calibrated` and `clients` as any call's does. The redacted view digests `prompt` and `request_id`, which may echo an id the caller supplied, and keeps the other details open.

The calibration key, by which a calibration job outside the engine would group a question's answers against their gold answers, and which the engine neither builds nor stores, is the question identity, the provider, the model, the reported one else the requested, the revision, whether `model_reported`, else `model_requested`, is in `claimed`, and whether `revision` is, and the elicitation; the runtime moves scores but asks no new question, so it stays out of the key. A reader of a call item before format 5 takes a non-null `snapshot` as a claimed revision, with null provider and model and nothing else claimed.

## The idia binding

`ommat.bindings.idia`, installed by the `idia` extra and never imported by `import ommat`, binds an oracle to a provider through the idia library. `oracle_binding(provider, *, state=None)` takes an idia `Dispatcher` over one leaf and refuses, with `ValueError`, any other provider, a dispatcher whose re-ask is on and one whose overflow policy is not `Fail`, since a second answer or a trimmed state is not what the call item records, a leaf whose declared probability decimals fall outside 0 to 17, and a `state` that is not a non-empty string. A design that asks label kinds and extractions of different models binds one dispatcher per oracle. `state` names a need that a label source sends as the whole JSON part, its value alone in place of an object keyed by refs, so a caller sends the state shape it measured a provider on, since the wrapping is part of what the model reads.

Each call sets one idia deadline from `seconds`; seconds idia's deadline cannot hold send nothing, and every ref is `unsupported` with the reason as a line. Each source is one idia request, its questions in the order received, keyed by their refs, each label carrying its description. A source that asks an extraction sends the text its spans index as the state's first part, so a span indexes part 0, with its reference date in the request's options and its other needs not sent; a source that asks only label kinds sends its needs as one JSON part keyed by ommat's refs, never renamed, or, when `state` names the source's one need besides its images, that need's value as the part. A label source that needs `state` beside another need, images aside, is `unsupported`, with its reason as a line; an extraction source, a source that does not need `state`, and an image need named by `state` send as they would without it. Each object goes in the order the binding receives it. A source that asks both sends the extraction's state, so its label questions see the text alone. An image need follows as an image part. A described `noul` is sent with idia's `yes` and `no` labels and their descriptions, an undescribed one with none; a structured wording is sent as idia's JSON instruction, each array a tuple. A question whose described `noul` or structured wording the provider's capabilities do not carry, `carries_noul_descriptions` or `carries_structured_instructions` false, is `unsupported` alone before any call, with a line naming the form. A question idia's `Question` refuses, a `choice` rendered with fewer than two labels among them, is `unsupported` alone, with its reason as a line, and the source's other questions are sent; a source left with none sends no request. These are `unsupported`, each with its reason as a line: a `t:` ref; a source whose extractions read different texts or name different reference dates; a need a JSON part cannot carry; a question idia cannot build; and a request idia's preflight refuses. The sources sent run concurrently.

A `noul` answer is `yes` as the provider gave it and `no` as idia's `noul_complement` derives it; a `choice` or `score` answer is its probabilities by name, idia's chosen label and confidence left out, so a tie at the top abstains in the choice helpers where the provider named one; a refusal is `refused`; a fault is `invalid-answer` with a line naming its rules; an extraction is written through `extraction_value`, and a value it refuses or a span into a part other than the text is `invalid-answer` with a line. Each ref of a wire request that did not answer is `timed-out` when its error is idia's `DeadlineExceeded`, the request the call's time could not hold, else `raised`, with the request's status and message as a line; the other refs stand. Each outcome's `provider`, `model_requested`, `model_reported`, `elicitation`, `calibrated`, `request_id` and `usage` go under the same names, its `snapshot` as `revision`, its `versions` as `runtime`, its `prompt_sent` as `prompt` and its `client_versions` as `clients`, an empty mapping as null, and its `unreported` reasons and `claimed` under those names, never a reason idia did not give; a prompt the leaf built as a mapping is written through `prompt_text`, as JSON in the order the leaf built it, the form idia's recorded log keeps. `usage` sums each outcome's input and output tokens and passes, `attempts` each outcome's attempts, `truncated` holds the refs of an outcome the provider cut short, and `precision` is the answering leaf's rounding, its probability decimals or `unrounded` for `None`, and `None` when no request made an attempt; a package at two versions across the outcomes fails the call `invalid-return`; each outcome is one `RequestProvenance`, folded by `call_result`. Every line goes through `bounded_warnings`, each need through `json_value` and each image need through `image_need`.

Importing it without idia installed raises `ModuleNotFoundError` naming the extra; any other import failure propagates.

ommat's end-to-end and tutorial tests of the binding replay live runs from idia's `Recorded` logs and call no provider. A log recorded before idia kept the order sent is re-keyed offline by idia, exactly, since each request in it is text or a one-key JSON part whose value holds no object. Its `prompt_sent`, and so the call prompts a replay from it writes, keep sorted keys until the log is recorded again live.

In the binding's source, each site that writes a marker, rounds or raises carries a `# spec: <section>` comment naming the section of this spec that states the rule it keeps, its heading as written.

## The candidate-valued label set

A `choice` may declare its labels as `{"fixed": [...], "candidates": ref, "at": k}`: a fixed part, possibly empty, in the form of any label list, the ref of a candidate set, and `at`, how many fixed labels come before the candidates, an integer from 0 to the fixed part's length. A `Design` that leaves `at` out puts the candidates last; the document always writes it, and the question's identity hashes it. A candidate set is a source's `t:` ref declared `{"type": list<string>, "candidates": true}`, or the `d:` ref of a rule declared with `candidates=True` whose `outputs` is `list<string>`; or a list of a record type whose `candidates` member names its candidate field, a field of type exactly `string`, each other field a scalar, a scalar or null, or a list of a scalar. A member's candidate value is the member itself, or a record member's candidate field: labels, grounded refs, a candidate slot, a per-candidate read's keys and an `in` cell over the set all use it. Load refuses a label set naming any other ref, a candidate set of any other type, `true` over a list of records and a field name over `list<string>`, and the name `candidate` for any declaration. A set's members are distinct by the canonical bytes of their candidate values: a value that repeats one is `Unavailable` with `invalid-return`, naming the set's ref for a source's set and the rule for a rule's, and is never merged. The asking source has an edge from the set's provider; the set is among its needs only when it declares it.

After the source's own need, requirement and `when` markers, each candidate-valued question takes the first of these that applies, naming the set's ref, with `call` null, and is left out of its batch: a marker on the set, `Unavailable` with `need-unavailable` or `Unasked` with `need-undetermined`; an empty set, `Unasked` with `when-false`; rendered labels past load's bounds, more than 2^18 names or more than 2^24 code points of names and the fixed part's descriptions, `Unavailable` with `label-overflow`; and a member equal to a fixed name, `Unavailable` with `label-collision`. The source's other refs are asked. A source left with no ref to ask is not called and not charged. When the budget or the deadline cuts the round, a question left out keeps its own marker and the source's other refs take the cut's. A key a binding returns for a question left out is `unexpected-ref`.

Otherwise the question is asked over its labels in the declared order, the first `at` fixed names, then the set's members in the set's order, then the rest of the fixed names, and its answer is validated against them. Its identity hashes the fixed part and the set's ref, never the members. Replay's `question-changed` names it when the rerun's set entry holds members, in order, other than the answering record's. `from_record` rebuilds a stored question's labels from its fixed part and the set's entry in the stored record, and refuses the question when that entry is missing or a marker. In the redacted view, each label and `probabilities` key of its answer that is not a fixed name takes the set's class; a set digested whole leaves an opened answer showing how many members it held. The dead-question lint answers it over the labels each run renders, each fixed label on top and then a member, and never reports it on the grid.

## Per-candidate questions

A source may declare `grounded=ref`, naming a `list<string>` candidate set as [The candidate-valued label set](#the-candidate-valued-label-set) defines one. A question of a grounded source whose wording holds a slot with the origin `{"candidate": ref}`, naming that set, is per-candidate; at least one of its questions is. Load refuses a candidate slot in an ungrounded source or naming another set, and a ref with a grounded bracket in a need, read, slot, correlated group, label-set ref or a source's `grounded`, and a correlated group whose `q:` refs mix grounded and ungrounded ones or are grounded over different sets. This engine also refuses a grounded source that provides `t:` refs, a per-candidate extraction question, a per-candidate question whose labels are a candidate-valued set, and a need or slot ref naming a per-candidate question's declared ref.

A per-candidate question is asked once per candidate, in the same request as the source's other questions, which are asked once. Each is asked under its grounded ref: the declared ref, then `[`, then the candidate as RFC 8785 serializes a string, then `]`, such as `q:email/folder_role["Dana"]`. A reader parses the bracketed string with a JSON parser, never by searching for `]`. The slot renders the candidate; every other slot, the labels and the kind are the declared question's, and its one identity covers every candidate. The source has an edge from the set's provider, and the set is among its needs only when it declares it, so a binding otherwise gets the candidates in the wordings alone.

What the set holds decides what is written for the question. Over a valued set, interpret writes one entry per candidate at its grounded ref, and the source's own need, requirement and `when` markers, and a cut's, go on each grounded entry. Over an empty set nothing is asked or written for the question; a source left with nothing to ask is not called, nor is its `when`, and is not charged. The source's own markers win over the set's. Over a set holding a marker nothing is asked for it, and one entry is written at its declared ref: `Unavailable` with `need-unavailable`, or `Unasked` with `need-undetermined` for `Unsure` or `Unasked`, naming the set's ref, with `call` null. When asking ends before the set is on the board, the declared ref holds the cut's marker. A key a binding returns outside the grounded refs it was given is `unexpected-ref`.

A rule reads a per-candidate question by its declared ref. The read returns the marker written there, or else a `dict` from each candidate, in the set's order, to its grounded entry's `Answer` or marker, `{}` for an empty set; `require` stops only on the marker at the declared ref. Load adds the set's ref to the read set of each component that reads the declared ref. No read set holds a grounded ref, so a one-ref choice helper takes no single candidate's answer; `pick_each_with_margin` and `pick_each_with_thresholds` pick every candidate of the `dict` at once (see [the choice helpers](#the-choice-helpers)).

## Grounded sources of `t:` refs

A source grounded over a candidate set may provide `t:` refs, facts about each member: each is grounded and declares the type of one candidate's entry, and none is a candidate set. The engine forms each candidate's grounded ref, `t:x["<candidate value>"]`, and passes them in `refs`, in code-point order; the binding returns a value under each, reading the candidate with `ommat.bindings.candidate_of(ref)`, and, when the source declares its set a need, finding a record candidate's other fields as the member whose candidate field equals it. Each value is checked and written as any `t:` value, so a fault or a missing key marks that entry alone, and the declared ref as a key is `unexpected-ref`. The marker paths of [Per-candidate questions](#per-candidate-questions) hold for it.

A per-candidate question may hold a slot on a declared ref grounded over its own set, a field path allowed: the engine fills it with that candidate's entry, and the binding gets it among its needs under the grounded ref, field path kept, `t:x["Ana"].city`, which `from_record` compares. After the source's own markers, a marker there leaves out that candidate's question alone, `Unavailable` with `need-unavailable` or `Unasked` with `need-undetermined`, naming each such needs key of the winning kind once, in code-point order, with `call` null. Load refuses a slot on a grounded declared ref in any other question, and a need on one.

## Grounded rules and tables

A function rule or a table may declare `grounded=ref`, naming a candidate set. It is one node of the dependency graph, with an edge from the set's provider, and the one provider of its declared ref and of every ref grounded from it. Interpret runs it once per candidate, in the set's order, each on a read view whose current candidate is that candidate, and writes the entries once all have run, one per candidate at its grounded ref, `d:x["<candidate value>"]`, in ref code-point order. Each call's outcome is its own entry's alone: a raise, an invalid return, a stop or an undeclared read marks that entry, a function rule's entry holds its own call's `derivation`, and a table's its own `rows`, a `C` table aggregating that candidate's rows. Over a set holding a marker it writes the set's need marker at the declared ref, `Unavailable` with `need-unavailable`, or `Unasked` with `need-undetermined` for `Unsure` or `Unasked`, naming the set's ref, with no `derivation` or `rows`; over an empty set it writes nothing, and its declared ref reads `{}`. A grounded rule or table that is itself a candidate set is nested grounding, which load refuses.

A grounded component reads `candidate`, the current candidate value, and `candidate.f`, a field of a record candidate, each declared as spelt in a function rule's `reads` or a table input's `read`; neither is a ref, and load refuses either in any other component, a `candidate.f` naming no field of the set's record type, and a candidate read in `boundaries`. It reads a declared ref grounded over its own set as the current candidate's entry, a field path allowed, or as the marker written at the declared ref; a component reading a grounded declared ref otherwise gets the `dict`, the policy included, and load refuses its field path. A table reads a grounded declared ref only when grounded over the same set, and the policy table none. A cause and a `derivation` name a `candidate` or `candidate.f` read as the set's ref, once. A design document before format 8 holds no candidate read. In `unsure_to_execute`'s first search a grounded declared read takes a `dict` over its set's candidates at each value of its set, which it fills and holds in `point` as a declared read; each grounded entry, not the declared ref, is set to `Unsure` in turn, its `read` the grounded ref; and its abstention search reports a grounded rule's one-ref pick over the subject's question in its entry at the subject's candidate, `via` that entry.

## Grounded plan declarations

A plan declaration of kind `execute` may declare `grounded=ref`, naming a candidate set, and its step arguments may take `Origin(candidate=ref)`, the candidate value, written `{"candidate": ref}` in the document. Compile expands it into one copy of its steps per candidate, in the set's order, numbered by position in the expansion: a candidate origin becomes the candidate-value literal `{"literal": member, "set": ref}`, the whole member; an origin on a declared ref grounded over the same set, which takes no field path, becomes that candidate's grounded ref; and a binding becomes the same copy's step. Over an empty set the plan has no steps. Its `reason` is rendered once; a slot may name the set's ref, which renders as its candidate values, but no grounded declared ref. A policy table row naming it matches only when the set holds a value and every expanded origin and slot holds one, and load adds the set's ref and the grounded declared refs to the policy's reads; rule 2 names, after the row's inputs, the set's ref when it is `Unavailable`, else the first `Unavailable` expanded origin by expansion position and argument name. Load refuses a grounded plan of another kind, on the policy table's last row, a candidate origin in an ungrounded plan or naming another set, and an ungrounded plan's origin or slot naming a grounded declared ref. A function policy's plan holding a candidate origin or a `set` is `invalid-return` naming `policy`, and one naming a grounded ref breaks rule 4. From record format 7 the ledger admits a grounded ref in a step argument and a literal's `set`, which must name a key of `sets` that an earlier entry holds a value at and whose class tree the literal fits; `redact` digests the literal under that tree.

From format 6 the `settings` item holds `grounded`, an object keyed by each grounded declared ref, a per-candidate question's or a grounded rule's or table's, each value its set's ref, `{}` when the design grounds nothing, and from format 7 `sets`, an object keyed by each candidate set's ref, each value its candidate field's name or null for a `list<string>` set; the replay record takes both from the design replay ran under. The reader refuses a `sets` key that is not a key of `providers`, and a value that is not null over a class name or a field whose node in the set's class tree is a class name; from format 7 each `grounded` value is a key of `sets`. `redact` digests a candidate under its class: the set's class name, or for a set of records its candidate field's. A call item's `questions` holds one entry for a per-candidate question, under its declared ref. The reader refuses a `grounded` key that is not a key of `providers`, a value that is not a key of `providers` with no grounded bracket or field path, or one whose class tree is not a class name. It admits a grounded ref only as an entry's `ref`, in a cause's `refs`, and as a `calls` entry's `sums` key or `truncated` ref, each only when its declared ref is a key of `grounded`; it refuses a call item's `questions` key with a grounded bracket, a `value` entry at a key of `grounded`, and a marker there beside a grounded entry of the same declared ref. A lookup in the `settings` item's maps or a call item's `questions` takes a grounded ref as its declared ref.

In the redacted view, wherever the record holds a grounded ref, its bracketed string is replaced by the keyed digest of the candidate under the class of the set `grounded` names, `q:x["hmac-sha256:<hex>"]`, and passes unchanged under `open`. The digest is over the candidate string's canonical bytes, so where the set's entry is opened it equals the digest of the matching member. A grounded entry's value takes its declared ref's class tree.

Replay grounds the question again from the set's entry in the compared record. The fixture answers each grounded ref from the recorded entry that names the call item listing its source; a grounded ref with no such entry gets no key and is named by `ref-not-in-record`. `question-changed` names a per-candidate question by its declared ref.

## A structured wording

A question's wording may be `Structured(template, slots)`, its template a JSON object or array in the value domain, from format 4. Each string value is a template under the same rendering and escape rules as a `Text`'s, from the one slot list: each placeholder names a slot, each slot is in some string value, and a literal brace is doubled. Keys and other values are literal and never rendered, so the shape is the design's. Some key or value holds a character that is not whitespace outside its placeholders. The document writes `{"template": ..., "slots": [...]}` as for a `Text`, the template's JSON type telling the two apart, with `order`, the template's order object, from format 6 when the template is not in canonical order, so a template in canonical order keeps its identity; the question's identity hashes it as written. Each object is rendered and sent with its members in the order the template declares them, rebuilt from the document, so a design and its document ask alike. A plan's `reason` and `reply` stay plain string templates.

A placeholder renders its slot's value by one closed rule: a string as itself; a list as its members' renderings in order, joined by `, `, so an empty list renders as nothing; any other value as its ordered encoding, each object in the order the value holds it. The same rule renders a `Text` wording, a plan's `reason` and `reply` and a context entry's text. A question's identity does not cover a slot value's order; the design hash and the call's `prompt` do.

## The extraction kind

An extraction question declares, beside its kind and wording, no labels, a `value_type` (`text`, `number`, `date`, `time`, `datetime` or `duration`), `is_list`, `text`, a board ref naming the string its spans index, and `reference_date`, a board ref or null naming the string a date written without a year is read against. Both refs become needs of the source and are exactly of type `string`; the question's identity covers the four members, never the values they name.

The binding returns, for the question's ref, exactly an object of `value`, `guess`, `p_null`, `confidence` and `span`, null where it has nothing. `value` and `guess` are null or a value of the type, or when `is_list` a list of such values with no null; `p_null` and `confidence` are null or a number in [0, 1]; `span` is null, a pair `[start, end]` of code-point offsets into the text, end exclusive, or one pair per list item, and locates `value`, or `guess` when `value` is null. A `number` is held as a float. A `date` is `YYYY-MM-DD`; a `time` is `HH:MM:SS`, an optional fraction and an optional `Z` or `±HH:MM`; a `datetime` is a date, `T` and a time; a `duration` is ISO 8601's `P` form without weeks or fractions. These grammars are the engine's own, with ASCII digits and uppercase `T` and `Z` only. Any other return is `invalid-answer` on that ref alone. A model's none is a null `value`, never a marker; [The choice helpers](#the-choice-helpers) say when `extract` abstains.

The binding may also report the refs whose response was cut short as `truncated`, which the record keeps outside every hash.

## Decide

A caller dispatches on `plan.kind`, never on the verdict alone: a `broken` plan has no steps, and the guard passes it with nothing to run.

A plan the policy returns is checked against seven plan rules. One it breaks is `broken`, with cause `invalid-return` naming `rule:N`, then `step:N` when the break is in a step:

1. A plan whose kind is not `execute` has no steps.
2. A step binds only results of earlier steps, each by a path that is null or in the binding grammar.
3. Every step names a tool the design declares, and no argument is named `idempotency_key`.
4. A step's board-ref origin is one of the policy's declared reads and holds a value on the board.
5. A plan's kind is not `broken`. A `clarify` or `inform` plan carries a `reply`, and no other kind carries one; an `escalate` plan carries a `reason`, which any other kind may carry. Each `reply` and `reason` holds a character that is not whitespace.
6. Every step requirement names a `d:` ref with no field path, that has a provider, and whose value its type can hold.
7. The plan's `reason` and `reply`, and every literal argument, argument name and requirement value, are in the value domain.

The plan-level rules are checked first, 1, 5, then 7 over `reason` and `reply`; then each step by position against 2, 3, 4, then 7, then 6. The cause names the first break only. A requirement on part of a rule's value, such as `d:plan.execute`, breaks rule 6: give the part its own rule and require that rule's ref.

A component that raises, a rule, a `when`, the policy or the codec, does not end the turn: its ref, or its source's refs, is `Unavailable` with `raised`, or the plan `broken`, and its exception's type, message and traceback are in `unhashed["exceptions"]`, keyed by the log index of the item it wrote.

A rule, a `when` and the policy do not change a value they read. A read returns the board's own value, not a copy, so a change reaches every later reader in the turn, while the record keeps the value as it was written.

A `-0.0` in a value written to the board, an answer's probability or member included, or in a plan's literal or requirement value, is held as `0.0`, as the record writes it, so a component reads the same value live and in replay.

A marker a rule returns is written with its cause when each of the cause's refs is a ref the design provides, with or without a field path, a grounded ref whose entry the turn wrote, a source, rule or tool name, a parameter path, `policy`, `codec`, `rule:N` or `step:N`. Otherwise the rule's entry is `Unavailable` with `invalid-return` naming the rule, so a cause carries none of the turn's input.

Decide and replay refuse an async observer with `EntryRefusal`: an `async def` function or async generator function, a bound method or `functools.partial` of one, or an object whose class defines one as `__call__`. The test reads types and runs none of the observer's code. A sync observer that returns a coroutine is not caught, and what any observer returns is not read.

A cancellation of a running turn appends the `cause` item `cancelled`, closes the record, and propagates the `CancelledError` with the record as its `record`. To read `exc.record`, catch the `CancelledError` inside the task that awaits decide; a `CancelledError` that asyncio raises afresh carries no record. A cancellation requested from the observer takes effect at decide's next await. If the turn has none left, decide finishes, asyncio marks the finished task cancelled and raises a fresh `CancelledError`, and the decision is lost.

## Source requirements

A source may declare `requirement=SourceRequirement(ref, values)`: the source is asked only when `ref`, a `d:` ref with no field path, holds one of `values`. It is a `when` written as data, and it reads one estimate a rule wrote. Values are compared by their canonical bytes, so a number matches by its canonical form, `1` and `1.0` alike, and a list matches only a list in the same order.

The ref is an edge in the dependency graph, so the source waits for the rule that writes it, as it would for a need; it is never sent to the binding unless the source also declares it a need. A source may declare both a requirement and a function `when`; both must hold, and the requirement is tested first, after the needs. What the source's refs get follows what the ref holds. Its marker ranks with the needs' markers: an `Unavailable` need outranks an undetermined one, the cause names every need of the winning kind in code-point order, and a marker on any need wins over a requirement that does not hold:

| The ref holds | The source's refs |
|---|---|
| one of `values` | the source is asked |
| another value | `Unasked` with `when-false` naming the ref |
| `Unsure` or `Unasked` | `Unasked` with `need-undetermined` naming it among the undetermined needs |
| `Unavailable` | `Unavailable` with `need-unavailable` naming it among the unavailable needs |

A requirement cannot fail, so `when-failed` comes only from a function `when`, which reads only its source's needs and the state. A need, unlike a requirement, is sent to the binding: under the idia binding's `state=`, a label source that needs the `state=` need beside another need is `unsupported`. The design hash covers a requirement as data, its values in any order, with no `version`.

Load refuses a requirement whose ref is not a `d:` ref, `t:state` and a `q:` answer included, so a condition over an answer goes through a rule; a ref with a field path, so a record's field is projected by a rule of its own; a ref with no provider; no value, a value outside the value domain, a value the ref's type cannot hold, or a value given twice; and a cycle through it. A label-typed ref lets load refuse a misspelt value, which a plain `string` would let through to make the source `Unasked` on every turn.

## Targets

A design may declare `targets={id: Target(statement, values=None)}`, the properties of the state its questions and rules are about, keyed by target id. An id is any string, `tg:` then a name by convention, and is neither a ref nor a declared name; `statement` says what the property is and `values` describes its value space, or is null.

A `Question` may name `target`, the one target it asks about, and `latent_targets`, the targets it is known to bear on without asking about them, each `{"target": id, "role": role}`, the role a `TargetRole`: `reflective`, the answer alone is evidence for or against the target; `formative`, the answer supplies a part of it; or `unknown`, not yet judged. One answer may bear on one target in several ways, so a target may stand under several roles. A `Rule` may name `target`, the target it estimates.

Targets guide a reader and gate nothing: no turn or binding reads one. The design hash covers them, the latent targets as an unordered list, and a question's identity leaves out its `target` and `latent_targets`, so declaring targets keeps every question's identity. Load refuses a target id `targets` does not declare, naming the question's ref or the rule's id; a question's own `target` among its latent targets; one target listed twice under one role; and a role outside the three, a `target` or `statement` that is not a string, `values` that is neither a string nor null, and a latent target that is not exactly a target and a role. A missing link is not declared, not unrelated.

## Decision tables

A rule is a function or a `Table`; the policy is a `Policy` or a `PolicyTable`. A table has no `version`: the design hash covers it as data. A design document of any format may hold a table, read by the members below. Its entry holds `rows`, the positions from 1 of every row that matched, in table order, and no `derivation`.

```python
Table(
    "d:urgent",
    'label{"no", "yes"}',
    "F",
    inputs=[Helper("pick_with_margin", "q:urgent", bar="urgent/bar", margin="urgent/margin")],
    rows=[["-", Input("q:urgent")]],
)
```

**Inputs.** Each input is a ref, a read of a `q:`, `t:` or `d:` ref, a field path allowed, or `Helper(name, read, **params)`, which calls `pick_with_margin`, `pick_with_thresholds`, `size`, `mass` or `extract` over its read, each other argument bound to a leaf path: `bar`, `margin`, `thresholds`, `labels` (a `list<string>` leaf) or `null_bar`. A pick's input is a label of its question, a `string` over a candidate-valued label set, `size`'s an integer of at least 0 over a list read, `mass`'s a `number [0, 1]`, and `extract`'s its question's value type, a list when it asks for one. A bare `q:` input holds an answer, so only `-`, `Known` and marker cells apply to it. A `noul` question takes `pick_with_margin`, or `mass` over `["yes"]` compared against a bar, never `pick_with_thresholds`. A data rule is a one-input table. A value cell stands over a label, boolean or integer input only, never over a `number` or `string` one, but for a pick over a candidate-valued label set, where it may name a fixed label, never a candidate.

**Rows and cells.** A row is a list: one cell per input, then the output entry; a `C#` row is its cells alone. A cell is written as a string in this notation, its form decided by how it begins: `-` matches anything, a marker or null included; `Known` matches any value, null included, and no marker; `Unsure`, `Unavailable` or `Unasked` matches only that marker, `Unsure or Unavailable` either, and `Undetermined` any marker; `>= p`, `> p`, `<= p`, `< p` compare a numeric input against a leaf path, an integer literal over an `integer` input, or another input by the ref it reads; `[p, q]` is a closed interval of leaves or integer literals; `{a, b}` matches any listed label; `in d:x` is membership in a list-valued input; any other text is a label. A `bool` or an `int` is a value cell, a marker class a marker cell, and `Undetermined` or a tuple of marker classes a cell listing those kinds. A cell may also be the document's tagged form, which load writes for every cell: `"-"`, `{"is": v}`, `{"one_of": [...]}`, `{"marker": k}`, `{"markers": [k, ...]}`, `{"known": true}`, `{">=": o}` and its kin, `{"between": [o, o]}` or `{"in": ref}`, each operand `{"param": p}`, `{"literal": n}` or `{"ref": r}`. A label the notation would read as a form, such as `-`, `Known` or `Unsure`, is written `{"is": "-"}`. Null is matched by `-` and `Known` alone, and a marker by `-` and the marker cells. An interval's lower bound may not exceed its upper, and a `{a, b}` cell may not repeat a member.

**Outputs.** An output entry is a value of the table's `outputs` type, the class `Unsure`, `Input(ref)`, a pass-through of an input, or `Param(path)`, a pass-through of a leaf. A row passing an input through matches only when that input holds a value. A value `outputs` does not allow, from a pass-through or an aggregate, is `Unavailable` with `invalid-return`.

**Hit policies.** `U`, rows that never overlap; `A`, rows that overlap only with the same output; `F`, the first matching row; `P`, the highest-ranked output among the matching rows, by the table's `order`, which lists every output value once, highest first, every output a value it lists; and `C+`, `C<`, `C>` and `C#`, the sum, minimum, maximum or count over the matching rows. Under `C+`, `C<` and `C>` the `outputs` type conforms to `number`, and under `C#` it is `integer`. Under `P` every output is a value `order` lists, never `Unsure` or a pass-through, and `order` is null under every other policy; under `C` no output is `Unsure`. The sum is exact over the decimals, an `int` when every output is one, and the sum and count are 0 when no row matches; the minimum and maximum with no match follow the marker rule below. Load decides overlap from the cells and the leaves' values: two comparisons of one input against one other input that split the line never overlap, and any other cell over two inputs is taken to overlap where both inputs hold values.

**Markers.** Every input is read first, in input order, and then every row is tested. When no row matches, the output is the inputs' marker of highest precedence, `Unavailable`, then `Unsure`, then `Unasked`, with `required` naming the first input holding it, or, when every input holds a value, `Unsure` with `abstained` naming the inputs once each in input order. Under `F` a row that failed only on inputs holding `Unavailable`, a pass-through's included, and a cell over two inputs when its other input holds a value, before the winning row, and under `P` such a row ranking above it, makes the output `Unavailable` with `required` naming that row's first such input; under `C` such a row anywhere does, while a row failing on a `Known` cell is left out of the aggregate. A row that failed on `Unsure` or `Unasked`, or on a marker cell or a `Known` cell, does not propagate, so a row `Unsure or Unavailable` placed before the rows that test an optional input's value takes its outage as an outcome of its own, and a row holding `Known` over an input gives way to the rows after it when that input holds a marker, which keeps its cause in its own entry. A row of `-` matches an `Unasked` input too: a table meant to stay `Unasked` when its question was never asked names values in its last row instead. A helper that raises makes the output `Unavailable` with `raised`, with no row matched.

**The policy table.** A design declares its plans in `Design(plans={name: PlanDeclaration(...)})` and the tools their steps name in `tools`. `PolicyTable(inputs, rows)` reads `d:` and `t:` refs only, under `F`; a row ends with the name of a `PlanDeclaration(kind, steps, reason, reply)`, its steps `Step`s and its `reason` and `reply` declared `Text`s whose slots name board refs or leaves. A row matches only when every board-ref origin and slot of its plan holds a value, and load adds them to the policy's reads; a row whose plan should give way to the next row when an optional input is down, not escalate, holds `Known` over that input. The last row is all `-` and names a plan with no board ref. Compile renders the winning plan's text, writes `rows` and, when a text has slots, `slots` on the `plan` item, and holds the plan to the plan rules; rule 2 gives an `escalate` plan with `required` naming the input, origin or slot, and a helper that raises gives `broken` with `raised`. A plan name is a declared name; a declared plan's kind is not `broken`, its `reply` and `reason` follow its kind, and its steps name declared tools, with no argument named `idempotency_key`.

**The grid.** A table's parameter comparisons and helper bindings are its boundaries: a pick's bindings under its read, `extract`'s `null_bar` and comparisons over its input under its read's `null` and `value` roles, a comparison over a `mass` input under its read's `mass` with the set its `labels` leaf holds, and a comparison over a bare `d:` or `t:` read under that read, an integer literal by its value. A cell over a `size` input adds nothing, so the lints probe a count table only over the lists `values` gives. An integer value cell over a bare numeric read is a boundary too. A function rule's `boundaries` field may not repeat a boundary a table already declares. The abstention search counts a lineage table's pick or `extract` input over a subject as resting on its abstention, and the first search leaves out a `d:` read whose table cannot write `Unsure`: one with no `Unsure` output and a row of `-` giving a value or a parameter.

## The context block

A design may declare `context=Context(entries, plan=True, steps=True, record=True)`, what a turn hands its next reader, such as an LLM carrying on the conversation. With none, nothing is handed off. Each `ContextEntry(ref, text)` flags `ref`, which is `t:state` or a `d:` ref, with or without a field path, and words it with `text`, a `Text` whose slots name board refs or parameters, one of them `ref` as spelled. There is no reserved `{value}` slot and no size bound. The switches each default on: `plan` gives the plan's kind, `reply` and `reason` and the verdict's kind; `steps` its steps' tools and arguments, never their requirements; `record` the design's name and hash and the record hash.

Load refuses two entries flagging one ref; a slot ref with no provider; a flagged ref that is a `q:` ref, a `t:` ref other than `t:state`, or grounded, and a candidate slot; a parameter slot naming no leaf; a template whose placeholders and slots do not match, one with nothing but whitespace outside its placeholders, a text with no slot naming its flagged ref, a path naming no field, and any slot whose ref's class tree holds `identifier` at the path or beneath it. Slots add no read and no edge to the graph.

Compile renders each entry over the board interpret left, before it calls the policy, and writes the block into the `plan` item's `context`: `{"design", "entries", "plan", "steps", "record"}`, the design's name or null, the entries in declared order and the switches as declared; `context` is null when the design declares none. An entry is `{"ref", "text", "marker"}`: the rendered text and null; or, when any board-ref slot reads a marker, null and that marker's `{"kind", "cause"}`, the flagged ref's if it reads one, else the first in slot order. The engine never words a marker. At a broken entry the block holds no entries. A rendered text is `content`, digested by `redact`, as is the design's name; a ref, a marker and the switches are `open`. Replay renders the block again as part of the plan item, so a changed text is a `plan` finding. `use_answers` compares a plan without its `context`.

An entry's text is the input's own: a slot over `t:state` or a source's `t:` ref carries what the user or a tool wrote, and the next reader receives it as written. So does a step's board-ref argument, which the handoff and the transcript give with its value; load's `identifier` refusal covers slots, not arguments.

## The handoff view

`handoff(record)` takes any closed `Record` and returns `None` when the log has no `plan` item or its `context` is null or absent. Otherwise it returns a plain dict of these members, in this order, each under its switch:

- `design`, `{"name", "hash"}`, and `record_hash`, under `record`;
- `plan`: `kind`, `reply` and `reason` as recorded, and `verdict`, the verdict's kind or null when the log holds no verdict, under `plan`; `steps` under `steps` when the verdict passed, a list, empty for a plan with none; left out when it would hold no member;
- `context`, the entries as recorded, under no switch.

A step is `{"tool", "arguments"}`, each argument tagged: a literal `{"value": v}`, a board ref `{"ref": r, "value": v}` with the value resolved from the record as `run_plan` resolves it, and a binding `{"step": n, "path": p}`. Under a refused verdict, or a log with no verdict, the plan keeps its kind and loses its steps, so a handoff never shows steps that will not run. It holds no guard failure, requirement, probability, span, provenance or version. It raises `ValueError` for a `record` that is not exactly a `Record`, and for a board-ref argument with no value in the log, which no record decide returned holds.

## The transcript view

`transcript(record)` takes any closed `Record` and returns `None` when the log has no `plan` item. Otherwise it returns a plain dict of these members, in this order:

- `record_hash`;
- `design`, `{"name", "hash"}`: the context block's name, null when the `plan` item's `context` is null or, before format 5, absent, and the `design` item's hash;
- `rounds`: for each round with a call item, in log order, a list of calls, one per source of each call item, in the item's source order. A call is `{"id", "source", "asks", "outcome", "answers"}`: `asks` the source's refs whose entry names the call item, in code-point order; `outcome` as recorded; `answers` an object keyed by those refs, each entry `{"value": v}` as recorded or `{"marker": {"kind", "cause"}}`;
- `decide`, `{"id", "board", "plan", "verdict"}` and `context`: `board` every `d:` entry, keyed by ref in log order and tagged as an answer is; `plan` the item's `kind`, `reason`, `reply`, `cause` and `steps`, each step `{"tool", "arguments", "requirements"}`, its arguments tagged as the handoff tags them; `verdict` the item's `{"kind", "failures"}`, or null when the log holds none; `context` the block's entries, absent when the item's `context` is;
- `pending`: for an `execute` plan under a passed verdict with no binding argument, its steps, each `{"id", "tool", "arguments"}`, the arguments resolved from the record as `run_plan` resolves them; else empty. Run a plan with a binding with `run_plan`, which passes each step its idempotency key.

An id is `om_`, the record hash's first 24 hexadecimal digits, then `_c<n>` for the view's nth call, `_d` for decide, or `_s<n>` for step n, so it fits `[A-Za-z0-9_-]` and 40 characters, and one log always gives the same ids, so a conversation that holds one record twice holds its ids twice unless the renderers' `turn` sets them apart. A ref no call asked, such as one whose source's needs were markers, is in no round; its effect is on the board. The view holds no `t:state` entry, no provenance, prompt or question wording, and no `derivation`, `rows` or `slots`. Its values are the record's, content included: to share a turn, share its redacted view, from which no transcript is built. It raises `ValueError` for a `record` that is not exactly a `Record`, and for a step's board-ref argument with no value in the log.

## The conversation renderers

`ommat.conversation` is outside the compatibility promise and may change in any minor release. It takes nothing beyond the standard library. `ommat.conversation.pydantic_ai` takes Pydantic AI, which the `pydantic-ai` extra installs as `pydantic-ai-slim>=2.49`. `import ommat` imports neither. A renderer words nothing: beyond fixed labels, every sentence it gives is a plan's `reason` or `reply` or a context entry's text.

`handoff_text(handoff)` returns the handoff as lines of text, or `""` for `None` or a handoff that gives no line: `Plan: <kind>`; `Verdict: <kind>`, `Reason: ...` and `Reply: ...`, each where not null; `Step <n>: <tool> <arguments>` for each step, its tagged arguments in the ordered encoding; each entry's text, or `<ref>: <kind> (<cause code>)` for a marker entry, its kind as recorded, such as `Unsure`; `Design: <name> <hash>`, or `Design: <hash>` with no name; and `Record: <hash>`. A member the view leaves out gives no line. The caller places it: as Pydantic AI's `instructions`, a system or developer message, or, when a slot or a step argument reads free text, fenced as data in a user message.

`to_chat_completions(view, *, turn=None)` returns a transcript as Chat Completions messages, or `[]` for `None`. For each round, an assistant message with null `content` and one tool call per call, `{"id", "type": "function", "function": {"name", "arguments"}}`, then one `{"role": "tool", "tool_call_id", "content"}` message per call; then an assistant message calling `decide` and its `tool` message; then, when `pending` holds steps, one assistant message calling them, with no result, for the caller to run and answer. A source call's arguments are `{"asks"}`, decide's `{"design"}`, the view's `{"name", "hash"}`, and a step's its resolved values. A source call's result is its `answers`, failed or not, and decide's is the view's `decide` without its `id`. Every arguments and content string is in the ordered encoding, each object in the order the view holds it, so a record renders to the same bytes each time and the model reads each object in the order it was given. The caller places the messages after its own user message for the turn.

`to_model_messages(view, *, model_name=None, provider_name=None, turn=None)` returns the same calls and results as Pydantic AI messages, or `[]` for `None`: for each round a `ModelResponse` of `ToolCallPart`s, then a `ModelRequest` of `ToolReturnPart`s, a failed call's with `outcome="failed"`, which Pydantic AI sends as the provider's error flag, as Anthropic's and Bedrock's, or, for a provider with none, by wrapping the content as `{"error": ...}`; decide alike, its return's `metadata` `{"record_hash", "design_hash"}`; then, when `pending` holds steps, a `ModelResponse` of their calls. Arguments and content are dicts. Every `ModelResponse` carries `model_name` and `provider_name`; timestamps are Pydantic AI's. The source calls' and decide's messages, both the responses and the requests, carry `metadata` `{"ommat": "bookkeeping", "record_hash"}`, which Pydantic AI keeps and never sends to a model; the pending calls' response carries none, since those calls are real actions. An agent run with this history and no new prompt runs the pending calls with its own tools.

ommat's bookkeeping is the source calls and decide, with their results. `is_bookkeeping(message)` tells one, a Pydantic AI message by its mark and a Chat Completions message by its ids, a source call's or decide's, never a pending step's: an assistant message with null or empty `content` whose every tool call has one, or a `tool` message answering one; a Pydantic AI request that holds anything but tool returns is not bookkeeping. `drop_earlier_bookkeeping(messages)` returns the messages without each bookkeeping call message of a record other than the latest, the record of the last such message, and without each result answering one of their calls, wherever that result stands; no result leaves on its own mark. So the current turn's bookkeeping stays for the model to answer from, an earlier turn's leaves with each result beside its call, and a request that Pydantic AI merged loses only the results it holds for those calls. Each module has its own pair; the Pydantic AI one runs as a history processor, `Agent(capabilities=[ProcessHistory(drop_earlier_bookkeeping)])`, which also writes the processed history back into the run's messages.

Both renderers take an optional `turn`, exactly a `str` or an `int` in the value domain, an `int` of magnitude at most 2^53 - 1 and a `str` with no surrogate, else `ValueError`, checked even for a `None` view. With one, every rendered id's stem, `om_` and 24 hexadecimal digits, is instead the first 24 of SHA-256 over the canonical JSON of `[record hash, turn]`; its suffix stays, so an id keeps its form and bound. The same design, state and answers give the same record, so one conversation holds a record once unless each rendering takes its own `turn`, as an exact retry must, since a provider may refuse a repeated tool-call id. The Pydantic AI mark then holds `turn`, and each filter's record is the record and the turn. With `None`, the ids are the transcript's.

A source's tool name is its name with each `/` as `-`, which no ommat name holds, and a pending step's is its tool as the design declares it, which must be the caller's tool's name. Both renderers raise `ValueError` for a source or pending tool named `decide`, a pending tool outside `[A-Za-z0-9_-]`, and a name over 64 characters.

## The record lens

`ommat.lens` is outside the compatibility promise and may change in any minor release. It takes nothing beyond the standard library, `import ommat` does not import it, and the page it writes loads nothing from the network, which its content-security policy also forbids. The page holds the records whole, user text included, so keep it as you keep them, or pass a redacted view.

`page(records, designs=(), *, skipped=())` returns one self-contained HTML page as a `str`. `records` are record documents as `bytes`, such as `decision.record.document`, a sequence named `record 1`, `record 2` and so on, or a mapping from a name to the bytes; `designs` are design documents as `bytes`, such as `loaded.document`; `skipped` names files the caller passed over, which the page counts. A record is any JSON object holding a `log` list, so a redacted view or a hand-edited record shows too. A design pairs by the SHA-256 of its exact bytes, so pass `loaded.document` as it is. It raises `ValueError` for an argument of the wrong type, a document that is not one JSON object in strict UTF-8 with no byte-order mark, no repeated key, only finite numbers, no integer past 2^53 - 1 that names no double exactly, and nesting at most 128 deep, a record without a `log` list, or a design that is not a JSON object.

The page has three views, chosen by its tab bar; a card selected in one view stays selected in the other when both show it.

**Design** shows one design document, with or without a record: its targets, then the state and each source's questions and tool results, the rules, the policy with the plan-level requirements, and the parameters. A question shows its wording, kind and labels and the targets it asks about or bears on, each with its role; a rule shows its reads, the target it estimates and whether it declares boundaries. Selecting a target lights the questions that ask about it, those that bear on it, and the rules that estimate it, then what follows them through declared reads, and dims the rest as having no link declared, never as unrelated: a link a design leaves out is not known, not absent. Selecting any other card shows what it came from and what it fed through the design's declared needs, reads, slot origins, parameters, boundaries, candidate sets, candidate-valued labels, context entries and requirements. Every link here is the design's. With several designs, a picker chooses one; by default it is the current turn's.

In the Design and Turn views a selection also reorders each column: the selection first, then its direct links, a question's or rule's own declared targets among them, then the rest of its lineage, and under a target its asking questions, then those bearing on it, then its estimating rules. By default the cards it does not reach fold into a stub per column that names how many and why: under a target, "other targets", "with no link declared to" the target or "declaring no target link"; under any other Design card, targets "it declares no link to" or cards "with no declared link to it"; in a turn, "not on its lineage". A stub opens them on a click, and a control switches to dimming them in place. When any column holds more than 40 cards, or when asked, the page is compact: every card but the selection keeps its value on one line, its bars, a call's outcome, the verdict and its boundary notes, and only the selection's direct links keep their meta line. An answer with more than three labels shows its leading label, its runner-up and every label a threshold or a mass bound names, the rest folded into one bar holding their combined probability, and a long list of labels or of a call's questions shows its first few; selecting the card shows them all.

**Turn** shows one record at a time, with Previous, Next, a picker and, outside the columns and the lineage panel, the arrow keys among several. Each round is a column of its call items, then its entries, each in log order: a `noul`, `choice` or `score` answer's probabilities as percentages, an extraction's value with its confidence and the probability that the text holds none, a marker by kind and cause code. A call names each question it asked with the start of its identity. The plan and the verdict come last. A design pairs with each record whose `design` item holds the SHA-256 of the design's bytes. A record with no such design uses the only design there is, or one opened in the page, and the page says it may differ. With a paired design, each entry shows the target its question or rule declares, and each answer shows the parameter values the design's `boundaries` fields name for its ref: a bar as a tick on every label's bar and a threshold as a tick on the bar of each label it names, each tick's parameter and exact value on hover; the lead of the leading label over the runner-up, or its own probability when it stands alone, measured without rounding and shown to three decimals when it lies within rounding of a margin; a label set's mass beside its bound; an extraction's none and confidence bars and its value bound; and a bound on a plain read as the number it is, beside the field it bounds. An answer whose ref no field names says no boundaries are declared. A table rule or table policy, which load refuses, is shown as not traced.

Selecting an entry, a call or the plan shows what it came from and what it fed. Links from the record: an entry's call and candidate set, and every ref a derivation, a cause, a failed call, a step, a context entry or a guard failure names. Links from the paired design: each `d:` entry's rule, by the record's provider map, and its declared reads, the policy's declared reads and context entries, and each source's needs, requirement, candidate set and the refs its questions read, a candidate-valued label set included. The record holds no read set, so a design link is what the component declared, and each link says which it is. **Open in Design** shows the selected entry's declared ref in the Design view, and **Show in this turn** goes back. With several records, **Compare across turns** opens the Turns view on the selected answer's question identity.

**Turns** lists every record as a row, grouped under its design hash: the plan's kind, the verdict, the rounds, the failed calls, the markers by kind and cause, and the plan's reason or reply. Grouped instead by a question identity that a call item names, it lists every answer whose own call item names that identity across the records, so one question asked under two designs is one group: the leading label and its probability, the runner-up, the lead or the marker, and the `d:` entries whose derivation names the answer. Selecting a row opens its record in the Turn view.

`python -m ommat.lens PATH [-o FILE] [--open]` writes the page, by default to `ommat-lens.html`, for a record or design file or for every `.json` and `.jsonl` file under a folder, a file it reaches twice through a symbolic or hard link read once and a link to a folder not followed, and a `.jsonl` line by line, each line's document named `<path> · line <n>`. A JSON object with a `log` list is a record, one with a `policy` and no `log` list a design; any other document, and a file or folder it cannot read, is skipped and named on stderr with the reason, and, when it read a record, a design no record pairs with is named there too. With a missing path, an output that is one of the files it reads, an output it cannot write, or neither a record nor a design, it writes no page and exits 1. The page also opens a further record, design or folder itself.

## The choice helpers

`pick_with_margin(r, ref, bar, margin)` returns the top label of the answer at `ref` when its probability is at or above `bar` and leads the next by at least `margin`, else `Unsure` with `abstained` naming `ref`; `pick_with_thresholds(r, ref, thresholds, margin)` takes a bar per label. A tie at the top abstains, even at `margin` 0: no helper breaks a tie. A marker read is returned unchanged.

`pick_each_with_margin(r, ref, bar, margin)` and `pick_each_with_thresholds(r, ref, thresholds, margin)` read a per-candidate question's declared ref and return a new `dict` in the set's order from each candidate to its pick, to `Unsure` with `abstained` naming that candidate's grounded ref, or to its entry's marker unchanged; the set's need marker comes back unchanged and an empty set gives `{}`. They test every argument and every answered candidate, each answer holding every label in `thresholds`, before picking any; a read that is not a per-candidate question's raises. Only a function rule calls them. A rule weighs the `dict` and returns its own value; returned whole, a `dict` holding a marker fits no type and is `invalid-return`. Passing one candidate's marker on is a valid return. A rule grounded over the same set reads one candidate's answer and uses the one-ref picks.

`determined(r, *refs)` returns a `tuple` of its reads' values in argument order, for one ref as for several, or, when any read is a marker, the marker of highest precedence, `Unavailable`, then `Unsure`, then `Unasked`, the first of its kind in argument order, unchanged. A null and a per-candidate read's `dict` are values. It stops nothing: a rule passes the marker on by returning it. No ref, or a ref given twice, raises.

The helper version, which each record's `design` item carries as `helpers`, is 3. A record of another version replays with a `helpers-mismatch` finding beside any other, and runs under the loaded helpers.

`extract(r, ref, null_bar)` returns `Unsure` with `abstained` naming `ref` when the extraction answer at `ref` has a `None` `value`, or a `p_null` at or above `null_bar`; otherwise it returns the `value`, as a list when the question asks for one. A `None` `p_null` never abstains, so `null_bar` bites only when the model gives a `p_null`. The result depends on `value` and `p_null` alone, never on `guess` or `confidence`: a rule that weighs confidence reads the answer whole. A `null_bar` that is not exactly an `int` or `float` in [0, 1] raises, before a marker read is returned unchanged, and a read holding any other value raises `TypeError`.

## The design lints

`dead_questions(loaded, *, values=None)` fills `t:state` and every ref a source provides, and runs decide at each point; `unsure_to_execute(loaded, *, values=None)` fills the policy's declared reads and calls the policy with each `d:` read set to `Unsure` in turn. Each takes `values`, a mapping from a read it fills to a list of that read's values, for a read its types and the answer grid cannot fill, or to narrow a label question as below. `dead_questions` never takes a `d:` key, since rules compute those values, and `unsure_to_execute` takes a `q:` key only for a read its abstention search fills; a key naming a read the lint does not fill is a `ValueError` before any run. A label question with no grid entry, other than a candidate-valued one, also takes a key, though in `unsure_to_execute` only for a question its abstention search fills; its list is the labels to try, each at 1 in declared order with the marker last, in place of every label; each report's `narrowed` lists such questions in code-point order, so it still says what was searched. A question the grid fills takes no key, since its points carry the grid's claim.

`dead_questions` reports each question `live`, `no-change-found`, `not-on-grid` or `not-grounded`. A per-candidate question has one grid entry, shared by its grounded entries, and one report, under its declared ref. At each assignment of the other reads its set's value is fixed, by the point for a `t:` set and by the run for a `d:` set, which the lint learns by first running the assignment with every grounded entry at its first point, and that value grounds the entries; a later run whose `d:` set differs raises `ValueError` naming the set, since a set that moves with the entries cannot be fixed. Each grounded entry is filled as its question is, and the entries cross after the other reads, in code-point order, so they vary fastest. An empty set, or one holding a marker, grounds nothing. The question is `live` when any of its grounded entries has two points whose runs differ, and `not-grounded` when it is not `live` and no assignment grounds an entry of it. A report's point holds each grounded entry under its grounded ref.

`unsure_to_execute` also runs an abstention search. A rule may turn a pick's or `extract`'s `Unsure` into a value, such as a list leaving a question or a candidate out, so the policy executes on a read the first search never sets. The policy's lineage is every rule and source its reads rest on through the dependency graph, and a subject is each question a lineage rule reads, or each grounded entry of a per-candidate one. For each subject in turn the lint runs whole turns as `dead_questions` does, the subject at its abstention point, every label at 0 for a `noul`, `choice` or `score` and a null value for an extraction, on which every pick and `extract` abstains whatever its arguments. The reads the policy or a lineage rule or source reads, needs, is gated on, is grounded over or is labelled by range over their points, and every other read holds its first. A `d:` set that a run shows changing within one assignment raises `ValueError`, as in `dead_questions`. A subject's runs vary one ranging read at a time from their first points, and stop at its first finding, so they grow with the sum of the other reads' points, not their product, times the same sum over the grounded entries where both range: with no finding, two plain subjects over three points each give 6 runs, three give 15, and a per-candidate question's two or three candidates at three points each give 6 or 15. A finding that needs two reads off their first points at once is not searched. A run is reported when it wrote the subject's abstention answer, a lineage rule returned `pick_with_margin`, `pick_with_thresholds` or `extract` over a plain question, or `pick_each_with_margin` or `pick_each_with_thresholds` over a per-candidate one, and its plan is `execute`; the subject's one report item has `read` the question's ref or the entry's grounded ref and `point` the run's exogenous values, grounded entries and each candidate-valued question as the run wrote it, after the `d:` items. A combinator's `conflict` and an `Unsure` a rule builds itself are the rule's own judgment and are not searched. A pick a rule calls and sets aside is reported too, since a rule's record lists its calls, not their use, and a marker passed on to the policy is reported by both searches: when the run's policy read, one the first search reported, holds an `Unsure` naming the subject, the subject's item holds `via`, that read, the first in code-point order. Once a lineage rule reads a question, the lint needs the free inputs `dead_questions` needs, a `t:state` field, a candidate set or an extraction question among them, and `points` counts the runs too.

## Replay

`design-mismatch` gives the recorded and replayed design hashes. A function component's identity includes its module and qualified name, so the usual cause is a component whose module was `__main__`, a design defined in the script that ran it. To find the member that differs, keep the design document beside each record and diff it against `loaded.document` with any JSON diff; a design document is canonical JSON, so the output is stable. A design that must live in a script keeps its document and is loaded elsewhere with `load(document, functions={...})`, keyed by each function component's key, a rule's name, `<source>.when` or `policy`, which keeps its hash.

An `entry`, `plan` or `verdict` finding compares the two items by canonical bytes, `order` included, so a rerun that holds an object in another order than the record is a finding. Replay builds typed values from the record's values with each object in the order its item's `order` gives, a record's fields in their declared order, both the state entry it passes in place of the encoder and the recorded values its fixture answers with; it reruns a record before format 6 with slot text in the canonical encoding, as that record's engine rendered it.

`not-in-record` names a source the rerun asks that the record never called; `use_answers` reports the entries it would provide as `not-returned`. An `entry` finding compares the two entries whole, so it can differ in its derivation or its `rows` alone.

## Evaluation

`ommat.evaluation.use_answers(record, loaded, other)` reruns `record`'s turn under `loaded` with `other`'s call items and entries answering, `q:` and `t:` refs alike; `use_answers_sync` is its twin. `record` and `other` are each a `Record` or a stored document, read through `read_record`. It returns `{"other": h, "findings": [...]}`, `h` the other record's hash. The state entry, the run settings, the cut and every comparison are `record`'s, so the findings are replay's with `other` as the fixture: `not-in-record` and `ref-not-in-record` name what `other` does not hold, and `question-changed` compares against `other`'s identities. A `Failed` call or a returned marker in `other` comes back as replay returns a recorded one. Neither record is edited, and neither's `unhashed` is read.

After both records are read and before any replay it raises `EvaluationRefusal`, `reason` in this order: `invalid-record` when a document does not read, carrying the `InvalidRecord` as `error`; `unfinished` for a record with no `verdict` item, then `nothing-to-replay` for one with no state entry, then `wrong-design` for one whose design hash is not `loaded.design_hash`, each for `record` then `other`; and `different-turn` when the two state entries or `settings` items differ as replay compares items, `order` included, an item of a record before format 6 compared without `order` and `grounded`. `turn` is the refused record's hash, `other`'s for `different-turn`, and `None` for `invalid-record`, which carries `error` instead; every other attribute is `None`. A `loaded` that is not exactly a `LoadedDesign` is an `EntryRefusal`, before either record is read.

A replay record, the `Record` that `replay` returns, is a valid `record` or `other`. To compare two models decided under different parameter sets, replay the second's record under the first's loaded design and pass the replay record as `other`; a threshold that makes the rerun ask a source the second never called shows as `not-in-record` in the replay's findings, and its entries come back `not-returned` in `use_answers`'s.

### The stores

Both stores are sync, and each is a directory, its `root`, holding one file per hash, named by the hash's algorithm as a directory and its 64 hex characters as the file name. Each file is written whole by exclusive create: the bytes go to a temporary file beside it, synced and given mode `0o644`, which is then hard-linked under its name, so a reader never sees part of a file and of two writers of one name exactly one succeeds. A store therefore needs a filesystem with hard links. Neither store replaces a file. An I/O failure other than a missing file propagates as `OSError`, never a refusal. Each refusal is an `EvaluationRefusal` whose attributes other than `reason` and `key` are `None`; `key` is a one-item list of the key refused for `bad-key`, `not-held`, `hash-mismatch`, `held-mismatch` and `held-key`, a design hash or a `dict` of an evaluation key's members, and `None` for the others.

`DesignStore(root).keep(loaded)` writes `loaded.document` unchanged at `sha256/<hex>` of `loaded.design_hash`, the hash the loaded design carries, never a key given beside it, and returns `None`; a `loaded` that is not exactly a `LoadedDesign` is a `TypeError`. Keeping a hash it holds compares the stored bytes: equal bytes are accepted, and differing bytes refused with `held-mismatch`. `fetch(key, *, functions=None)` returns the design held under `key`, loaded by `load` with `functions` passed through, refusing in order a `key` that is not `sha256:` and 64 lowercase hex characters with `bad-key`, before any path is built; a key it does not hold with `not-held`; and bytes whose hash is not `key` with `hash-mismatch`, the hash found in the message. `load`'s `InvalidDesign` passes through, so a design whose code the process lacks fails check 11, and a function defined in a function or a lambda needs `functions`.

`EvaluationStore(root).write(entry)` keeps an entry, `{"kind", "format", "key", "value"}`, as its canonical bytes at `<kind>/sha256/<hex>`, `<hex>` that of the hash of `key`'s canonical bytes, and returns `None`. The kinds and their key members, each a string:

- `gold-answer`: `gold_set`, `question`, `turn` and `candidate`;
- `switching-value`: `record`, `design` and `parameter`;
- `certificate`: `design` and `sweep`;
- `group-counts`: `design`, `group`, `split` and `gold_set`.

Before building a path it refuses in order: an entry that is not a `dict` of exactly those four members with `invalid-entry`; a `kind` outside the four with `unknown-kind`, the kind in the message; a `format` that is not an `int`, a `bool` excluded, then a `key` that is not a `dict` of exactly its kind's members, each a `str`, with `invalid-entry`, the first fault in the message; and an entry the canonical encoding refuses, `value` included, with `invalid-entry`, the encoder's reason in the message. An entry under a kind and key it holds is refused with `held-key`, whatever its bytes, and the first entry stands; the check and the write are one step. The store keeps `format` and `value` as given and reads neither. `lookup(kind, key)` refuses `kind` and `key` by the same checks, then returns the entry held under them or `None`; a held file that does not parse as an envelope, by the checks `write` makes, or whose `kind` or hash of its `key` differs from its path, is refused with `misfiled-entry`, the path in the message.

## Continuing a workflow

ommat has no resume within a turn. To continue a multi-step workflow, the caller runs the first steps itself, then decides a new turn whose state carries the steps' results and the prior turn's record hash, with those fields declared in the state type. The prior turn's hash is the caller's claim, and ommat does not check it. ommat does not record the steps' results except as the new turn's state. The new turn replays without the earlier one. `run_plan`'s idempotency keys follow the new turn's record. `run_plan` has no option to stop after a step, so the first steps run on the caller's own runner.
